## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Use | Kashering |
|---|---|
| **Cold** | Hadaḥa: rinse/wash |
| **Cooking (liquid)** | Hagala: boiling water |
| **Direct fire** (skewers, baking pans) | Libun: heat in fire |
| **Earthenware** | Can't be kashered (except a kiln) |
| **Ben yomo** | Within 24 hours the absorbed taste forbids food; after, food is OK (unless done on purpose) |
| **Soap-washed metal/glass** | Still must be kashered, but in disputes about released taste (nat bar nat, ḥarif) be lenient |

## 1. Kashering utensils

- Spoils of Midyan: "whatever goes into fire, pass through fire… what doesn't, pass through water" (Bamidbar 31:23). **Ke-bol'o kakh polto**: it releases the way it absorbed.
- Three methods: **libun** (fire), **hagala** (boiling water), **hadaḥa** (rinsing).
- "Absorbed" also meant taste stuck hard to rough walls. Before running water and soap, food residue stayed on pots; hagala melted it off.

## 2. Absorbed taste forbids food

- A treif pot's walls release taste into kosher food. We can't measure how much, so we treat the walls as **full of taste** → food rarely has 60× the wall → forbidden.
- Same with a dairy pot used for meat. A second cooking is also forbidden (maybe it came out now).
- A reliable gentile tasting it could permit it, but custom doesn't rely on that.

## 3. Ben yomo; on purpose vs. by mistake

- A treif pot is forbidden to use until kashered.
- Within **24 hours** (ben yomo), its taste forbids what's cooked. After, the taste is spoiled → food permitted. Unknown → assume not ben yomo.
- Cooked in it **deliberately** (not ben yomo): forbidden to the cook and those it was made for (a penalty); permitted to others.

## 4. Metal and earthenware

- The Torah's kashering is about metal.
- **Earthenware** is porous and can't be kashered: "an earthenware vessel… shall be broken" (Vayikra 6:21).
- Libun would work, but people would go easy for fear it cracks → forbidden; a kiln works.

## 5. Glass

- Lenient (Rosh, Rashba, SA): smooth and hard, absorbs nothing → just wash.
- Strict: made from sand like earthenware → can't be kashered.
- Middle (Rambam): like metal → hagala.
- Sephardim leaned lenient (mostly for kli sheni); Ashkenazim strict, yet bediavad allow hagala. Most of Israel: the **middle path**: separate meat/dairy glass, kasher like metal.

## 6. Metal today

- The Sages felt taste from metal pots even after cleaning.
- Today no one can taste it after proper washing; studies: absorption is tiny (1 in tens of thousands).
- Why the change? Better casting (fewer pores), purer metals, smoother surfaces, and especially **dish soap** and running water (removing fat that counted as "absorbed").

## 7. Practical consequences

- Some argue: if metal doesn't absorb, just washing should suffice, like glass for the lenient.
- Nearly all rabbis: **still kasher**, because of generations of practice and the Sages' many fences between meat and milk and treif; without them people would use dirty treif or mixed pots.

## 8. Why it's still required

- Many: Torah obligation only while ben yomo, yet the Sages require kashering afterward. Likewise after soap washing → rabbinic requirement.
- Others: Torah obligation even after 24 hours (plain reading) → applies even to clean vessels.

## 9. Practical halakha for metal and glass

- Must kasher treif pots, and meat pots used for dairy (and vice versa); glass like metal.
- But in disputes about **released taste** (nat bar nat, ḥarif): soap-washed metal/glass → be lenient.

## 10. Other materials

| Material | Status |
|---|---|
| Metal, glass, enamel | Kasher, but lenient on released-taste disputes |
| Wood, bone, leather, plastic, Teflon | Absorb and release; all the stringencies apply |
| Earthenware, clay, ceramic | Absorb and can't be kashered |
| Porcelain | Like earthenware; in need, smooth uncracked glaze → hagala like glass |

## 11. Nat bar nat

- Parve food cooked in a meat pot, to eat with dairy: some Sephardim permit; most Sephardim only if not ben yomo; Ashkenazim not lekhatḥila (bediavad if not ben yomo).
- With clean, soap-washed metal/glass: **all may be lenient**, even ben yomo, even for a small need. Still, ideally cook parve-for-dairy in a dairy pot.

## 12. Sharp foods (ḥarif)

- Onion, garlic, hot pepper: some say sharpness draws meat taste from the pot or knife → becomes meaty.
- Clean metal/glass pot or knife: stays parve; may go with dairy.

## 13. Cutting boards

- Wood/plastic absorbs (material + knife cuts) → meat, dairy (and parve) boards. Vegetables from a clean meat board into dairy bediavad → kosher.
- Onion cut on a dairy board into meat without 60× → forbidden (Ḥayei Adam); in great need, if no dairy taste is possible, permitted.
- Glass/metal boards: clean ones can be used for anything.

## 14. Spoons and pot walls

- Meat dish stirred with a clean dairy metal spoon → food kosher, spoon needs hagala.
- Parve in a meat pot stirred with a dairy spoon → all fine.
- Milk spilled on the outside of a meat pot cooking → food kosher, pot needs hagala.
- Hot treif gravy (or milk) on a cold kosher (meat) pot → pour boiling water on that spot.

> Line to remember: soap changed the chemistry, not the fence; we still kasher, but we stop fearing tastes that aren't there.
