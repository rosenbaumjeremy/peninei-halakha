## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Idea | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Prevent assimilation: "distinction, not separation" (Rav Tzvi Yehuda) |
| **Chain** | Bread for wine, wine for intermarriage, intermarriage for idolatry (AZ 36b) |
| **Wine** | Strictest; even a Jew's wine touched by a gentile; spirits in a gentile's home or bar |
| **Oil** | Permitted by R. Yehuda Nesia: the public couldn't keep it |
| **Bread** | Lenient in need for a gentile baker's bread (pat palter) |
| **Who** | All gentiles (even those who can't marry); not non-observant Jews |

## 1. Attitude to non-Jews and the decrees

- Love every person (Avraham's open tent); Israel is meant to bring **blessing** to all nations. Rav Kook: love must extend to all humanity; hatred only for evil.
- To do that, Israel must keep its uniqueness. Food brings hearts close, so the Sages forbade gentiles' **wine, bread and cooked food**, even made from kosher ingredients.
- Rav Tzvi Yehuda: "**Distinction is not separation**."
- Where evil dominates a culture, real distancing is needed (wine touched by an idolater is forbidden even for benefit).
- Torah basis: "I have set you apart from the nations" (Vayikra 20:24–26); eating their sacrifices leads to intermarriage and idolatry (Shemot 34:15–16).

## 2. Gentiles' wine

- The strictest: "bread because of wine, wine because of their daughters, daughters because of idolatry" (AZ 36b). A decree upon a decree, all one fence (Ramban).
- Baal Pe'or: Bilam's plan, linen market, an old woman outside and a girl inside, the wine, then the idol (Sanhedrin 106a).
- Zimri and Kozbi; Pinḥas stopped the plague. Pirkei de-R. Eliezer: Pinḥas banned gentiles' wine; later forgotten in the Land.

## 3. Daniel and his friends

- Captives raised in Nevukhadnetzar's palace to assimilate. Daniel "resolved not to defile himself" with the king's food and wine (and oil).
- Ate legumes and seeds, yet looked healthier than all. A model of guarding identity.
- Many in his generation enjoyed Aḥashverosh's feast; Mordekhai knew the sin was "gentiles' cooked food" (Yalkut).
- ~400 years later the Sages decreed formally, and it held.

## 4. The 18 decrees

- Before the Second Temple's fall, in the attic of Ḥanania ben Ḥizkia, Beit Shammai outnumbered Beit Hillel; 18 decrees passed, including bread, wine and oil.
- Shammai: more fences. Hillel: education, fewer decrees (too many → people break them).
- R. Eliezer: "that day they filled the measure." R. Yehoshua: "that day they overflowed it."

## 5. Their fate; oil permitted

- The 18 can't be annulled ("even if Eliyahu comes"). But with the Temple gone, most lapsed.
- Oil was too hard to keep; ~250 years later **R. Yehuda Nesia** permitted it ("no decree unless most of the public can keep it").
- Rav resisted; Shmuel threatened to call him a zaken mamre. Rambam: forbidding it is a great sin.
- Bread: leniency for **pat palter** in need. Wine: kept, even strengthened.

## 6. Cooked food; summary

- Two groups: (1) **bread, wine, oil**, from Daniel through the 18 decrees: our staple foods (dagan, tirosh, yitzhar) should be our own; (2) **bishul akum**, against personal closeness.
- Oil dropped; cooked food remained. Bread: palter in need. Wine and liquor: more fences (touch; drinking at a gentile's home/store).

## 7. Who's included

- All gentiles, even elderly, eunuchs, celibate priests: the fear is gradual blurring of boundaries, not one meal → one marriage.
- Not a Jew forbidden to marry (mamzer, married woman): no assimilation risk.
- Non-observant Jews: the decree doesn't apply; just check the kashrut of what they made.

> Line to remember: distinction is not separation; we keep our table our own so we can bless everyone else's.
