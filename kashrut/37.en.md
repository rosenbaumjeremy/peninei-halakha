## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Item | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Industrial food** | Don't eat without a reliable hekhsher |
| **Gelatin (E441)** | From non-kosher sources: lenient may rely; ideally kosher-animal or fish gelatin |
| **Glycerin (E422)** | Must come from kosher animals, plants or synthetic sources |
| **Colors** | Synthetic OK; E120 (insects) forbidden; E163 may be from wine |
| **Shellac (E904)** | Not certified; wash coated fruit |
| **Medicines, whiskey** | Tasteless pills: no hekhsher. Flavored: prefer certified. Pure whisky: many permit |

## 1. Introduction

- Modern food uses many **additives**: emulsifiers, stabilizers, humectants, anti-caking agents, thickeners (gelatin), preservatives, antioxidants, flavor enhancers, colors.
- Plant-based → usually fine. Animal-based → usually from pigs, insects or unslaughtered animals.
- Gentile wine or wine vinegar → stam yeinam (need 60). Dairy ingredients → ḥalav akum, and the product isn't parve.
- Consumers can't track all this → **no industrial food without reliable supervision**.

## 2. The questions

- (1) If the forbidden material became inedible in processing, is it "**new**" (panim ḥadashot)?
- (2) When does a small amount count as a **setting agent** (not batel) vs. negligible?
- (3) Even if batel, a Jew may not add it on purpose; what if a gentile did?

## 3. Gelatin

- From collagen in bones and hides; sets candy, ice cream, desserts. Big plants use non-kosher sources.
- Permit: bones/hides become inedible even to a dog → new substance (Aḥiezer, Har Tzvi, Yabia Omer). Forbid: just separated collagen, and it's a setting agent (Igrot Moshe, Minḥat Yitzḥak).
- Practice: the lenient may rely (rabbinic dispute; no taste; today's chemistry). But kosher-slaughter or fish gelatin is easy to get → ideally avoid gelatin from forbidden sources.

## 4. Glycerin

- A fat component; used to moisten, thicken, sweeten, preserve, often in tiny amounts. Mostly from pigs or unslaughtered animals; plant/synthetic alternatives exist.
- Lenient: became putrid → new; usually less than 1/60; works with kosher ingredients (zeh ve-zeh gorem).
- Strict (common ruling): only separated, and spoiled on purpose to make it food. So require kosher-animal, plant or synthetic glycerin.

## 5. Food coloring

- Synthetic (petroleum, coal): kosher, though health concerns are pushing back to natural.
- Plant colors: fine, except **E163** (red-blue) when from wine-pressing leftovers.
- Animal colors forbidden: **E120** carmine (insects), sometimes E161g (shrimp, etc.).

## 6. Shellac

- Tree resin hardened by the lac insect's secretion, thinned in alcohol (E904), to make fruit and candy shine.
- Forbid: the insect secretion binds it (Rav Mordekhai Eliyahu). Permit: hard and bitter (spoiled taste); "setting" color isn't setting food (Igrot Moshe).
- Practice: not certified (alternatives exist). Possibly coated fruit → **wash well**; any residue is a sfek sfeika.

## 7. Grown on a forbidden medium

- A starter that "improves" other food stays forbidden even if inedible itself (sourdough, yeast, cheese cultures).
- Today microbes (bacteria, yeast) are grown on a medium, then separated and frozen.
- Even if the medium contains forbidden material, the microbes are kosher: always some kosher ingredients too (zeh ve-zeh gorem); and like an animal that ate forbidden food.
- But microbes feed directly, and zeh ve-zeh is only bediavad → kashrut agencies require a **kosher medium** unless that's hard.

## 8. Medicines

- Tasteless medicine: no hekhsher needed (not "eating").
- Tasty medicine (lozenges, syrups) and tasty vitamins: lekhatḥila certified; health systems should check. If unavailable, may take them (only additives are in doubt; follow the majority).
- Vitamins/supplements eaten for enjoyment (needing a berakha): need a hekhsher.
- Flavored toothpicks and lip balm: permitted; hiddur: unflavored or certified.

## 9. Alcohol and spirits

- Distilled alcohol has the status of its source: brandy from gentile wine → forbidden (Rivash); whiskey and beer from grain → **ḥametz**.
- Spirits need a hekhsher. **Whisky**: many permit without, since the method is known; sherry-cask aging doesn't give enough taste.
- Only genuine quality whisky; cheap or flavored whisky and other spirits need a hekhsher. Local rabbis may permit traditional beers they know.

## 10. Trading forbidden food

- No commerce in **Torah**-forbidden food (nevela, terefa, non-kosher animals, fish, insects). Rabbinic (ḥalav akum, bishul akum): permitted.
- Exception: **ḥelev** may be traded ("used for any work," Vayikra 7:24).
- No raising non-kosher animals for gentiles' food, or taking them as collateral.
- Permitted: selling a slaughtered animal found terefa; non-kosher animals caught by chance (sell quickly); debt repaid in forbidden food (sell quickly).
- Non-food animals (horses, donkeys, dogs, ornamental fish): permitted.

## 11. Lab-grown meat

- Meat grown from stem cells could replace raising billions of animals.
- One view: it follows the source cell (pig → pork; kosher-slaughtered beef → meat, not with milk). Another: the first cell is negligible → new, parve even from pig.
- Rav Melamed: the **first view**: the food's identity follows its originating cell.

> Line to remember: in a world of E-numbers, the hekhsher is your eyes.
