## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Step | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Which blood** | Cattle, wild animals, birds. Not fish or locusts (rabbinic look-alike rule) |
| **Raw meat** | Blood absorbed in the meat is permitted; just rinse the cut |
| **Methods** | Salting or roasting (scalding banned by the Geonim except in need) |
| **Salting** | Soak ½ hour → drip → salt all sides 1 hour (min. 18 min) → rinse 3 times |
| **72 hours** | Unsalted, unsoaked meat after 3 days → roast only; freezing time doesn't count |
| **Liver** | Roast only (then you may cook it) |

## 1. The prohibition of blood

- With permission to eat meat came: "only be strong not to eat the blood, for the blood is the **nefesh**" (Devarim 12:23).
- **Dam ha-nefesh** (the gush at slaughter): karet. Other blood: lashes.
- Dam ha-nefesh is what's sprinkled on the altar "to atone for your souls" (Vayikra 17:11).
- Eating blood can instill animal-like traits and **cruelty** (Ramban, Ḥinukh).

## 2. Parameters

- Applies to species that need sheḥita (cattle, wild animals, birds), not fish or locusts: less developed, feel less pain.
- Blood naturally absorbed in meat is **permitted**: raw meat may be eaten after rinsing the cut.
- Cooking unkashered meat forbids it (blood leaves and is reabsorbed). In a dish, the whole piece counts as blood → need **60×**.
- Most authorities: cooked or salted blood is only **rabbinically** forbidden; kashering prevents that.

## 3. Methods

- Four: **salting**, **roasting**, scalding in boiling water, scalding in wine vinegar.
- Salting and roasting **draw out** blood. Red liquid left afterward is "**meat juice**," not blood ("the Torah wasn't given to angels").
- Scalding **seals** the blood into the meat. The Geonim banned it: water not hot enough, too little, not all at once, vinegar too weak.
- So: salt or roast before cooking.

## 4. Preparing to salt

- Salting doesn't help blood pooled in large vessels (shoulder, cheek, tongue), brain membranes or testicles → cut/remove first.
- Bruised meat with clotted blood → cut it open first (roasting doesn't need a cut). Still red after → fine.
- **72 hours** after slaughter without soaking → blood dries; only roasting works (then don't cook it lekhatḥila). Liver may wait (roasted anyway).
- Soaking before 72 hours resets the clock. Freezing: some count it, but in practice it **doesn't count**, so meat may be frozen for months.
- Frozen meat can't be kashered; thaw in water **below** yad soledet.

## 5. Rinsing first

- Three steps: rinse, salt "very well," rinse "very well" (Ḥullin 113a).
- Soak ~**½ hour** to soften the surface (a good rinse in a pinch). Otherwise the salt soaks up surface blood instead of drawing.
- Salt can "cook" surface blood in; salted without rinsing → rinse and re-salt.
- Let it drip, but don't let it dry (salt won't stick).

## 6. Salting

- Cover every part until it's too salty to eat. Medium "coarse cooking salt"; fine salt works bediavad.
- All sides (one side and cooked → still kosher). Internal organs of a chicken salted separately, inside and out.
- On a perforated or **slanted** surface so blood drains.
- **1 hour** lekhatḥila; in need 18 minutes. Less than 18 and cooked → forbidden.
- Same time for any size. Pieces may be piled (beef on chicken): for 12 hours meat emits, it doesn't absorb.

## 7. Rinsing after

- Rinse **3 times** in fresh water, or under a tap until no blood or salt remains.
- Used salt is forbidden; don't reuse it.
- Red liquid afterward, even from a new cut = meat juice, permitted.
- Rambam: also scald afterward; custom doesn't follow this except some Yemenites, who may rely on the one-hour salting in need.

## 8. Special parts

- **Bones** with marrow: salt like meat.
- **Heart**: cut open and rinse out the chambers first.
- **Intestines**: no salting unless a red spot; the fat on them and the stomachs: salt.
- **Liver**: roast only. **Spleen**: salt like meat, but peeling its ḥelev is hard, so it's usually sold.

## 9. Salt and the sick

- For those who can't have salt: **roast** without salt, then cook to soften.
- Or salt a big piece and give the inner part.
- Otherwise: scald small pieces in water at a rolling boil, one at a time.

## 10. Roasting

- Stronger than salting: works after 72 hours, on clotted blood without cutting, and on liver.
- Rinse, sprinkle a little salt (optional), put straight on the fire, roast until edible.
- Some rinse after; if that ruins the taste, no need.

## 11. How long and how

- Until edible for most people in a pinch: about **half** of full roasting. To cook it after: better roast fully.
- Turning is fine; avoid nonstop fast rolling (bediavad fine).
- Pieces stacked: blood that's drawn out "slides off" (mishrak sharik).

## 12. Liver

- "All blood," yet permitted: the Torah forbade blood that flows like water.
- Rabbeinu Tam allowed salting with crosswise cuts; halakha: **roast only**, then it may be cooked.
- Cutting off the gallbladder opens it enough. Don't roast liver on top of other meat (bediavad fine).

## 13. Fire and utensils

- Open fire on a grill or skewer; smooth hot plate; slanted if ridged. **Never** in a vessel where blood collects.
- Oven (gas/electric, closed) fine; put a tray to catch drips.
- Knife, skewer, grate touching meat while it kashers aren't forbidden (Rosh, SA); some Ashkenazim are strict, but may be lenient.

## 14. Fish, locust and human blood

- Fish/locust blood: no Torah prohibition, but forbidden if it looks like animal blood (**mar'it ayin**); fine with scales in it or mixed into food.
- Human blood: rabbinic. Bit into bread and left blood → scrape it off. Blood from your gums or a finger may be sucked.

> Line to remember: the Torah allowed us the meat, but the life in it stays God's.
