## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Who touched it | Result |
|---|---|
| **Idolater** (Hindu; Christian per the strict view) | Forbidden even for **benefit** |
| **Non-idolater** (Muslim, secular, Druze) | Forbidden to **drink**, benefit permitted; one level lighter |
| **Mevushal** (heated ~70°C+; pasteurized per the main view) | Not affected by touch |
| **Very sweet or diluted wine** | Not "wine" for these laws |
| **Liquor** | Not in a gentile's home, bar or party; occasional single drink in need is OK |
| **Non-observant Jew** | Today, unless defiant, their touch doesn't forbid wine |

## 1. Why so strict

- Wine releases inhibitions and brings joy: poured on the altar, used for kiddush, weddings, brit. Misused, it drives lust and was poured to idols.
- Two stringencies: (1) even a **Jew's wine** touched by a gentile is forbidden; (2) an idolater's wine (**stam yeinam**) is forbidden even for **benefit**, like actual libation wine.
- Benefit-forbidden wine may not be sold or even given as a gift.

## 2. Non-idolaters

- No libation risk, but assimilation risk remains → forbidden to drink, permitted for benefit.
- Rule: everything one step lighter. What's benefit-forbidden for an idolater → drink-forbidden; drink-forbidden → permitted.

## 3. Muslims, Christians, Hindus

- **Muslims** (pure monotheism), secular gentiles, Druze: non-idolaters.
- **Christians**: Rambam strict (benefit forbidden); Rashi, Rashbam, Meiri lenient. Lekhatḥila strict; in great need lenient. Those who see Jesus as a messenger only: not idolaters.
- **Hindus**: idolaters; but since they don't pour wine, in great need lenient on benefit.

## 4. What counts as wine?

- Juice still mixed with skins and seeds isn't yet wine. Once some is drawn off clear, the whole vat is wine.
- Vinegar isn't affected (test: it fizzes on limestone ground).
- Diluted: wine more than 1/6 of the water → still wine. Lots of sugar, honey or pepper changing its natural taste → not wine. Dry, semi-dry and lightly sweet wines → still wine.

## 5. Mevushal

- Idolaters didn't pour cooked wine → a gentile's touch doesn't forbid it (unless touched before cooking).
- When is it "cooked"? Strict: boiled until noticeably reduced. Lenient (halakha, rabbinic issue): reached ~**70°C**, when alcohol starts evaporating.
- **Pasteurized** wine/juice (70–80°C, closed system): strict (R. S.Z. Auerbach, Or Le-tzion) vs. lenient (Igrot Moshe, Rav Goren, Yabia Omer, Minḥat Yitzḥak). Halakha lenient; hiddur strict, but not where it would insult someone.

## 6. Touching

- An idolater touching wine as in libation (stirring with hand/stick, drinking from it) → benefit forbidden, even without intent.
- Non-idolater → drinking forbidden.
- Unintentional touch (hand slipped in; thought it was beer): idolater → drink only; non-idolater → permitted.
- Any doubt in yayin nesekh → lenient.

## 7. Shaking the vessel

- Some: shaking an **open** vessel hard was a libation → forbidden. Closed (even a temporary cap or bag) → never. Carrying or uncorking → fine.
- Others: shaking in a vessel never forbids.
- Idolater: strict lekhatḥila, lenient on benefit in a loss. Non-idolater: no concern.

## 8. Pouring

- Idolater pours from bottle to cup → cup and bottle forbidden to drink (connected stream); benefit OK. Big loss from a large vessel → what's left is fine.
- Non-idolater pours: many permit both; some forbid the cup; some even the bottle.
- Halakha lenient, but don't have gentile waiters pour: custom is diners open and pour. If a waiter did, the wine is kosher (unless seen touching or shaking).

## 9. Guarding wine

- Leaving wine with an idolater: **two seals**; non-idolater: one. Factories: cork/cap + plastic wrap.
- Idolater alone with an unsealed bottle, even briefly → benefit forbidden. Non-idolater who might drink from it → drink forbidden.
- Warned not to touch (like theft) and might be seen (walk-in, window, camera) → fine. Certainly unseen → forbidden.
- Gentile help at home alone: seal or lock up wine. Mevushal or sweetened wine needs no guarding.

## 10. More laws

- Gentile harvesters: lekhatḥila perforated bins; don't let them carry grapes to the press or tip them in (bediavad fine). Mechanical harvester: some say a Jew should operate it.
- A gentile who touched wine to **spite** a Jew doesn't forbid it; drink it in front of him.

## 11. Alcoholic drinks

- Amoraim: no drinking whiskey, arak, vodka, beer, etc. in a gentile's **home, store, café or bar**, even alone and even your own bottle.
- The drink itself isn't forbidden elsewhere (no bishul akum: mostly water).
- Occasional need (strength, headache): one glass in a bar and leave; at a gentile's home if offered and really needed, without sitting to drink. Not a third time.
- Staying at a gentile's home: bring your own; may accept a little to avoid ill will (only drinks, not food).
- Coffee/tea at a gentile's: disputed; as friends socializing be strict, as courtesy to a host lenient.

## 12. A gentile party

- A toast-gathering of gentiles: no alcohol, even your own, even in a public hall; kosher snacks OK. A mostly-Jewish event: alcohol OK.
- A gentile **celebration** (wedding, birth, Christmas, New Year's Eve, Eid al-Adha): no eating there at all, even a mehadrin table. A regular meal (restaurant/home) with kosher food and no alcohol: OK.
- "Lest you make a covenant… and eat of his sacrifice" (Shemot 34:15); AZ 8a: as if eating idolatrous offerings. Food sent to your home is fine.
- Ill will: try to avoid it with a gift and warm wishes; if unavoidable, attend minimally. Never alcohol.

## 13. A Jew who doesn't keep Shabbat

- Classically a public Shabbat desecrator is like a gentile: his touch forbids drinking (a penalty and protest).
- Only touching or drinking forbids; pouring or carrying the cup doesn't.
- Today many poskim: only one who desecrates **defiantly**; those who make kiddush or light candles, or never had a Torah education (like anusim), aren't treated so. Strictness causes insult and family strife.
- Most agree: in need, to avoid insult, don't be strict. They also count for a minyan.

## 14. No frivolous drinking

- Noaḥ, Lot, Nadav and Avihu fell through wine. "Nothing brings wailing to a person like wine" (Sanhedrin 70a).
- Drinking with music and partying led to exile (Yeshayahu 5).
- Wine also gives true joy: "wine gladdens the heart"; kiddush, weddings. The line is **mitzva joy vs. empty joy**.
- No singing/instruments over wine except praise of God or a se'udat mitzva (SA 560:3).
- Avoid secular clubs and parties ("a seat of scoffers"). A work event of that kind: skip if possible; at least don't drink.

> Line to remember: wine can lift you to kiddush or drop you to Noaḥ's tent; what decides is whose joy it is.
