## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Product | Halakha / mehadrin |
|---|---|
| **Ḥalav akum** | Supervised big companies: permitted (Igrot Moshe). Mehadrin: ḥalav Yisrael |
| **Milk powder** | Even more lenient; Israeli hekhsherim allow it |
| **Hard cheese (gevinat akum)** | Forbidden even with microbial rennet, unless a Jew watched/added the rennet |
| **Soft cheese, yogurt** | Better strict (Israel); some abroad permit with supervision |
| **Butter** | Permitted if ingredients are kosher; mehadrin: Jewish-made |
| **Mehadrin extras** | No milk from cows after surgery; only from Shabbat-observant dairies |

## 1. Kosher milk

- From kosher species (cow, goat, deer); not donkey, camel, horse, pig. Kosher milk curdles and is white; non-kosher doesn't and is yellowish.
- Milk of a **terefa** is forbidden; one in a herd → batel in 60.
- Found terefa at slaughter: milk/cheese from before is forbidden, unless the defect may have come later.
- Lung sirkha: milk from the last 3 days forbidden; earlier milk permitted (sfek sfeika).

## 2. Gentile milk

- Forbidden lest non-kosher milk was mixed in, even though unlikely and it'd be batel. Even cheese a Jew made from it.
- Permitted if a Jew **supervises** (can see or walk in any moment); check the vessel if needed.

## 3. When there's no risk of non-kosher milk

- (1) No realistic risk → permitted (Tashbetz, Pri Ḥadash; North Africa, Yemen).
- (2) Any remote risk forbids; only where none at all (Ḥida; much of Eretz Yisrael's surroundings, Ashkenaz).
- (3) A formal decree regardless (Ḥatam Sofer).

## 4. Big companies today

- Large companies milk no non-kosher animals; it'd be costly and pointless → permitted by views 1–2.
- R. Moshe Feinstein: government regulation and fear of fines/lost trust = Jewish supervision → even view 3. Basis for **OU** in the US.
- Strict: regulation may be loose; and it's a decree regardless.
- Halakha: lenient; but where easy, be strict. Electronic monitoring makes it mehadrin.

## 5. Milk powder

- Most: permitted (views 1–2; regulation). Also: the decree was on milk, not a new product (cheese needed its own decree).
- Some strict: powder is just milk without water.

## 6. Practical summary

- Regular hekhsher: supervised gentile milk and powder. **Mehadrin**: milk under Jewish supervision (mashgiaḥ or cameras).
- Israel's rabbinates avoid gentile milk but allow products with powder.
- Mehadrin also excludes milk from cows after **surgery** (caesarean, rumen piercing) and from dairies that milk in **Shabbat violation**.

## 7. Gentile cheese

- Forbidden even from kosher milk. Many reasons given; the accepted one: curdled with **rennet from a nevela's stomach** (not batel: it sets the cheese).
- Today rennet is mostly microbial, but traditional cheeses still use animal rennet.
- Halakha: forbidden even when microbial: a blanket decree. Whey: disputed.

## 8. How it can be permitted

- Milk owned by a Jew who hired the gentile → many permit (Shakh).
- A Jew **watched** the cheesemaking with kosher ingredients → most permit (Rema). Shakh: only if a Jew **added the rennet**.

## 9. Soft cheeses

- Cottage, white cheese, leben, yogurt: thickened by **bacteria**, not rennet.
- Some permit (the decree was about rennet). Most forbid: all thickened dairy, plus the non-kosher-milk concern is greater in soft cheese.
- Better strict; lenient may rely. Some agencies abroad certify gentile soft cheese; in Israel they don't.

## 10. Butter

- Forbidden by some (ḥalav akum; residue of non-kosher milk). Permitted by others (non-kosher milk doesn't churn; no rennet).
- Rambam, Rema, SA: if cooked till moisture evaporates, fine. Sometimes forbidden locally (treif pots, camel milk, lard).
- Halakha: permitted if ingredients are kosher; mehadrin: Jewish-made or supervised.

## 11. Milk milked on Shabbat

- Milking is a Torah prohibition; a gentile may milk to spare the cows pain (muktzeh till after Shabbat). Today: milking machine by **gerama**, or a gentile.
- Milked by a Jew in violation: rabbinically forbidden to him and those it's for. Mixed in stores: a doubt → permitted.
- Prefer Shabbat-observant dairies (certainly kosher; supports Shabbat). Not mehadrin otherwise.

## 12. Human milk

- Permitted once out of the body (e.g., pumped); not "milk" for meat-and-milk.
- Nursing from a woman: babies up to 24 months; continuously nursing up to 4 (weak, 5). A 72-hour break (not illness) ends it.

> Line to remember: the milk is almost always fine; the decrees guard the distance, and hiddur guards the details.
