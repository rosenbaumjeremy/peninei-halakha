## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What** | Metal (Torah) and glass (rabbinic) eating/cooking utensils bought from a gentile |
| **Exempt** | Earthenware, porcelain, stone, wood, plastic, disposables, can openers, storage |
| **Where** | A kosher mikveh (40 se'a), whole vessel at once, nothing intervening |
| **Berakha** | "Al tevilat kelim" (one: "kli") |
| **Made abroad** | Immerse with a berakha; Jewish-made bought from a Jew in Israel: no need |
| **Electric** | May be lenient; better immerse without a berakha or give to a gentile and borrow |

## 1. The mitzva

- A metal or glass food vessel acquired from a gentile must be immersed in a mikveh, like a convert's immersion.
- From the spoils of Midyan (Bamidbar 31:21–23): "pass through fire" = **kashering** (if used for treif); "purified with mei nidda" = **immersion**. Separate mitzvot.
- Purpose: lift the vessel from mere eating to eating connected to Torah values, part of making Jewish eating holy (berakhot, gifts, Jerusalem).

## 2. Materials

- Torah: all **metals**. Rabbinic: **glass** (can be melted and recast like metal).
- Exempt: earthenware, ceramic, stone, wood, plastic (don't extend the decree).
- Coated metal (Teflon, enamel) and Corelle (glass): immerse with a berakha. Porcelain (thin glaze on clay): exempt.
- Mixed vessels (metal pot with plastic handles): immerse the whole thing if the main food-contact part is metal/glass.

## 3. Which vessels

- Eating, serving and cooking: plates, cups, cutlery, bowls, trays, pots and lids, pans, baking pans, skewers, grills.
- Also grater, peeler, garlic press, egg slicer, tea strainer, nutcracker. Not stove grates, corkscrews, can openers.
- Not for earlier stages (slaughter knives, dough tools). Kitchen storage jars (sugar, coffee): yes; wrapped items or pantry storage: no.

## 4. The immersion

- Kosher mikveh: 40 se'a of spring or rain water.
- Whole vessel at once, including handle; no **ḥatzitza**: remove dirt, price stickers and glue. A nice maker's label people keep is part of the vessel.
- Kasher first, then immerse (bediavad either order).
- Assembled vessels may be immersed as is; fill narrow necks with water.
- Loosen your grip a moment, or wet your hand in the mikveh first and switch hands, or use a loose basket and shake it.

## 5. Berakha; minors and gentiles

- "...asher kideshanu be-mitzvotav ve-tzivanu al tevilat kelim" (one vessel: "kli").
- A minor may immerse and bless, but can't testify. Without an adult witness → re-immerse with a berakha (reliable child: without).
- Don't send a gentile; a Jew may bless and do the first, then watch the gentile do the rest.
- Use the dedicated vessel mikveh (safety; more fitting).

## 6. When it becomes obligated

- A Jewish merchant buying stock: no immersion until someone (or he) takes one for use.
- Jewish factory → gentile wholesaler → Jew: must immerse. Jewish-owned factory with gentile workers: exempt.
- Jewish-gentile partnership factory: immerse with berakha. So **made abroad → immerse**. Made in a Jewish Israeli factory, bought from a Jew: no need. Store owners should know their sources.
- Co-owned with a gentile: exempt until the Jew buys him out. IDF/police vessels: immerse with berakha. A **convert's** vessels: exempt.

## 7. Using a gentile's vessel

- Borrowed from a gentile (not used for treif): no immersion. Kept long enough that he gave up on it: some say obligated, unless you intend it stays his.
- No mikveh around, or Shabbat: give it to a trustworthy gentile as a gift and borrow it back. Afterward, ideally get it back and immerse with a berakha.

## 8. Using before immersion

- Forbidden even temporarily; but food made in it is still kosher.
- Guest of a Jew who doesn't immerse: avoid; in great need (would insult, no disposables) may rely on lenient views.
- Children at parents' home: may eat occasionally to keep peace, but then must take the vessels to the mikveh.
- Restaurants/hotels serving Jews must immerse; in great need, at an event, may rely on lenient views.

## 9. Disposables

- Disposable aluminum pans: exempt, even if reused.
- Jars and tins sold with food (jam, coffee, bottles): exempt; reusing them: disputed; lenient may; strict immerse without a berakha.

## 10. Electrical appliances

- Kettle, toaster, sandwich press touch food, but water may damage them.
- Lenient: plugged into the wall = attached to the ground; and they're machines, not vessels. May rely on this.
- Better: simple kettle → immerse without a berakha and let dry 24+ hours. Delicate devices → have a Jewish technician fully disassemble and reassemble the food part, or give to a gentile and borrow indefinitely.
- Removable oven trays, blender blades, mixer beaters: immerse **with a berakha**.

> Line to remember: like a convert, the pot enters a new life through the mikveh.
