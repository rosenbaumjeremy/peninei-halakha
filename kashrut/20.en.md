## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Idea | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Nevela vs. terefa** | Nevela: died or slaughtered wrongly. Terefa: slaughtered properly but had a fatal defect |
| **8 types from Sinai** | Clawed, pierced, missing, removed, torn, fallen, cut, broken |
| **12-month rule** | Helps only in a doubt: lived 12 months or gave birth → not terefa |
| **Checking** | Follow the majority; check only when there's a concern, except the lungs of cattle |
| **Kosher vs. ḥalak/glatt** | Kosher: sirkhot peeled and tested. Ḥalak: no sirkhot "out of order" at all |
| **Fowl today** | Vaccines, transport, knife checks, leg tendons, lungs: sample-check each flock |

## 1. Nevela and terefa

- Clean animals and birds are permitted only with proper **sheḥita**. Died of illness/accident or slaughtered wrongly → **nevela**.
- Slaughtered properly but with a defect that will kill it → **terefa** (Shemot 22:30). Sheḥita can't help: death is already working inside it.
- Eight types given at Sinai (Ḥullin 43a): **derusa** (clawed by a predator), **nekuva** (pierced organ), **ḥasera** (born missing an organ), **netula** (organ lost), **kerua** (torn), **nefula** (fell and was crushed), **pesuka** (severed spine/windpipe), **shevura** (most ribs broken).
- Expanded to 18 (Gemara) and to 70 in cattle / 72 in fowl (Rambam).
- Old age or weakness isn't terefa. Even a dangerously sick animal slaughtered in a hurry is permitted (a pious practice is to avoid it).
- Why: a whole animal's meat can be raised by sheḥita; one already being eaten away by death can't, and would drag the eater down.

## 2. Principles of terefot

- Most Sages: a terefa can't live more than **12 months** or give birth / lay eggs (Ḥullin 57b).
- In a **doubt**: if it lived 12 months or gave birth → not terefa.
- A **definite** terefa stays terefa even if it lives longer (SA YD 57:18).
- Doctors can't change the list either way: the Torah gave the Sages the authority ("do not turn aside," Devarim 17:11).

## 3. Doubtful cases

- Vet cut a terefa spot and sewed it up safely: some say terefa (it was one for a moment); others say not.
- Cut in a **disputed** spot (caesarean, piercing the rumen): if it lived 12 months or gave birth, even the strict view can permit it.
- Many say: once thousands of such animals are known to survive, **reality decides** the dispute → no need to wait 12 months each time.
- Stricter views: only that particular animal is cleared, or none at all. This question underlies the **kosher vs. ḥalak** debate.

## 4. Checking for terefot; checking the lungs

- A properly slaughtered animal is presumed kosher: follow the **majority**; checking all 70 terefot would be an enormous burden.
- Check only when there's a **concern**: hit by an arrow, fell, or a defect showed up while cutting. Source: "meat torn in the field" = attacked but survived → check.
- **Exception: the lungs** of cattle are always checked: lung problems are a "common minority," and sirkhot are visible, so skipping the check would cause chaos later and look like ignoring a prohibition (Rashi, Rashba).
- Milk of a living cow is fine without worrying about its lungs.
- Lungs lost by accident before checking → meat permitted (some: only for a big loss). Lost on purpose → forbidden.

## 5. What is a sirkha?

- A **sirkha** = an adhesion stretching from one spot on the lung to another, or to the chest wall/another organ.
- Why it's terefa: it formed over a hole (Rashi) or will tear off and leave one (Tosafot).
- Only "**out of order**" (she-lo ke-sidran: between spots that aren't naturally touching) is a problem. "**In order**" (ke-sidran: spots that touch anyway) is kosher.
- Two disputes: (1) where "in order" ends; (2) whether "out of order" is always terefa or can be tested. On (1) R. Yosef Karo is lenient and Rema strict; on (2), which decides most cases, R. Yosef Karo is strict and Rema lenient.
- Right lung: 4 lobes; left: 3. Lenient view: any hidden spot between touching lobes is fine. Strict view (Rema): only the inner half of the groove.

## 6. Testing by bubbling

- Strict (Rif, Rashi, Tosafot): out-of-order sirkha → terefa, even if peeled with no hole underneath.
- Lenient (Rambam, as read by Maharitz; Arukh Ha-shulḥan): peel it off, inflate the lung, put it in water. **Bubbles** → hole → terefa; no bubbles → kosher.

## 7. Testing by rubbing

- SA (39:10): any out-of-order sirkha, even hair-thin, is terefa; whoever rubs them off "feeds terefot to Israel."
- Rosh, Mahari Weil: a sirkha that rubs away is just **mucus** (a real sirkha gets stronger the more you rub).
- Those who rub still inflate the lung in water afterward (Baḥ, Shakh).

## 8. Communal practices

- Eretz Yisrael, Syria, Iraq, Egypt: **strict**. Yemen, Persia, Morocco, much of Tunisia/Libya and many Ashkenazim: **lenient**.
- Much of the split came from **financial loss**: where terefot could be sold to non-Jews (Sunni lands), people were strict; where they couldn't (Shiites in Yemen/Persia; hostile Christian lands), a lost cow could mean months of wages, so they relied on the lenient view.
- Morocco: a ~50-year dispute between old residents (strict) and Spanish exiles (lenient); the exiles won, except Algiers and Djerba.
- Later Ashkenaz: some fought rubbing (Shela, Gra); others held leniency is the main halakha (Shakh, Ḥatam Sofer, Arukh Ha-shulḥan).
- Reality supports the lenient side: sirkhot found in ~80% of older cows and ~50% of calves, which live on for years.
- Result: lenient-standard meat is called "**kosher**"; strict-standard meat is "**ḥalak**" / "**glatt**."

## 9. Kosher and ḥalak / glatt

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Kosher** | Sirkhot peeled/rubbed and tested; terefa only if there's a hole |
| **Ḥalak Beit Yosef** | No peeling at all; some lung spots don't make terefa |
| **Glatt (Rema)** | No peeling, and stricter on where "in order" applies; but very flimsy sirkhot are removed |

- In practice today: two levels, **kosher** and **ḥalak** (keeping both Beit Yosef's and Rema's stringencies). In need, one may rely on either ḥalak system (sfek sfeika).
- One may eat "kosher" (strong reasoning). Ideally eat **ḥalak**: it's the most important hiddur in kashrut and usually means more serious supervision overall. Especially if your family was strict.

## 10. More livestock issues

- **Caesarean**: animal with womb removed is kosher (SA 45:1); womb cut is disputed. In practice kosher, since they live and calve again, and vet surgery may not make terefa anyway.
- **Bloat**: piercing the rumen to release gas is disputed; reality supports leniency. Today gas is released by a **tube down the esophagus** instead.
- Swallowed **nails** that fully pierce the reticulum → terefa; lung checkers should watch for this.

## 11. Fowl and vaccinations

- Concern: the vaccine needle might pierce the esophagus, stomach or lungs.
- Growers fixed early mistakes; injections go in the **neck**; leading rabbis say no worry (Yabia Omer).
- Some supervisions inject in the thigh; others found that causes leg-tendon infections and use a **short needle in the breast**. In practice short needles are used.

## 12. Transporting fowl

- A fall of **10 tefaḥim** (~76 cm), or a sudden slam from lower, may make it nefula.
- Walked well after → kosher. Stood or lived 24 hours → check all organs. Slaughtered before that → terefa.
- Rough workers throwing or stepping on birds, or a dropped cage → those birds need checking.
- 2012 Agriculture Ministry rules reduce suffering and the risk; good supervision enforces them.

## 13. Slaughter pace and knife checks

- Ideal: check the knife before and after every sheḥita. For fowl it's checked about every **half hour**; if a nick is found, that batch is terefa (strict supervisions keep each shoḥet's birds separate).
- Today's hard steel knives almost never get a disqualifying nick; old knives (even stone or wood) needed more care.
- Speed: regular ~15–20 birds/min, mehadrin ≤12, stricter ≤10, plus rest breaks. Worry: pressing (**derasa**) or not seeing the cut.
- With sharp knives and a reliable shoḥet, speed isn't a real problem. Rely on any authorized rabbinate unless a body is publicly declared unreliable.

## 14. Tzomet ha-gidin (leg tendons)

- 16 tendons in a bird's leg; one torn → terefa (SA 56:8). Never checked in the past (majority rule).
- Crowded farming spreads a tendon infection: ~1% overall, sometimes much higher in a flock.
- Views: no need to check (Minḥat Yitzḥak) vs. check every bird (Shevet Ha-levi). Rav Melamed: **sample each flock**; problems found → check all.
- Method: cutting the leg open vs. looking/feeling from outside for swelling. Both reject about the same share.

## 15. More checks

- Bird lungs: not required (under 1%), but flocks sometimes have outbreaks → **sample-check**; easy today since organs hang on a conveyor.
- Dislocated hip (buka de-atma): rare → no need to check; only if seen.

## 16. Questions about store-bought chicken

- A broken bone is usually from plucking or a fall after slaughter → fine. Very dark/red area of clotted blood around it → ask a rav.
- Clotted blood (tzrirut dam) in salted chicken → cut it out. Just red color → no need.
- Forgot and cooked it → still kosher (maybe formed after kashering; cooked blood is rabbinic).

> Line to remember: sheḥita can raise a whole animal, but not one that death has already begun to claim.
