## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Torah** | Cooking, eating and benefiting from cattle meat + milk cooked together |
| **Rabbinic** | Eating them uncooked; fowl and wild animals; same table; dairy/meat bread |
| **Same table** | Not while eating, not two acquaintances, unless a heker (sign) or far apart |
| **Parve** | Ideally cooked in a pot of the type you'll eat it with; clean metal/glass works in need |
| **Mistakes** | Clean metal utensil, wrong type: food usually kosher, utensil needs hagala |
| **Appliances** | One dishwasher (not together), one stovetop, one oven (kasher in between), one microwave (covered) |

## 1. The prohibition

- "Do not cook a kid in its mother's milk," said **three times**: forbidden to cook, to eat, and to benefit from (Ḥullin 115b).
- Unique: each is kosher alone. A Jew may not cook it even for a non-Jew, or turn up the flame under it.
- Benefit forbidden: don't sell or give it away, use its heat or ash, or feed it to strays. **Bury** it (or throw in a field/trash).
- Torah: only **cooking**. Rabbis: no eating together uncooked, plus a fence of decrees (same table, bread, waiting).

## 2. Which meat and milk

- R. Akiva: only **ox, sheep, goat** (domesticated: you milk them and slaughter their young). The Torah's example: a goat often has twins.
- R. Yosi Ha-gelili: wild animals too, not fowl (no milk). Some: fowl too.
- Halakha like R. Akiva: wild animals and fowl with milk are **rabbinic** → may cook for a non-Jew and benefit.

## 3. Same table

- While eating meat (incl. fowl), no dairy on the table, and vice versa. Preparing dairy before 6 hours is fine.
- Two **acquaintances** may not eat meat and dairy at one table; strangers may.
- Allowed with a **heker**: an unusual object between them or a separate placemat, or if they can't reach each other's food.
- Done with meat? You may sit by someone eating dairy and eat parve.

## 4. Dairy or meat bread

- No kneading bread with milk or meat fat (you'll forget). Made anyway → the bread is **forbidden** (Pesaḥim 30a).
- OK: enough for one meal, or an **unusual shape** at home. For sale: only an obvious sign (cheese/meat on top).
- Applies to bread and crackers; **cakes** and pastries may be dairy/meat (not eaten with meat or cheese).

## 5. Why so many fences?

- Normally no "decree on a decree." Here: both foods are permitted, always in the kitchen, easy to mix. Without them, the basic decree wouldn't hold; all count as one decree.
- Fowl and wild animals treated like cattle, so people won't confuse.

## 6. Keeping foods apart

- Clean the table/cloth between meat and dairy (wash a fabric cloth; wipe plastic), even if food was on plates.
- Bread or salad from a meat meal: only if it wasn't touched with greasy hands or meat-dirty cutlery. Some avoid it lekhatḥila; may be lenient in need.
- Outside meals: meat and cheese may share a fridge/bag if they don't touch; touched → rinse.
- Don't let milk cartons drip on meat; if it did, rinse until no taste remains.

## 7. Separate utensils

- Separate, **clearly different** pots, dishes and cutlery, stored apart; even for cold food.
- Many keep two **salt shakers**; one is fine if kept clean.
- Sauce/ketchup bottles: tip shouldn't touch food; wipe if needed.
- High-chair tray: just clean between meals (food isn't that hot). If hot food goes on it, assign it a type.

## 8. Parve foods

- Ideally cook parve for meat in a meat pot, for dairy in a dairy pot (avoid confusion).
- In need: parve cooked in a **clean metal/glass** meat pot may be eaten with dairy; sharp foods (onion, garlic) cut with a clean meat knife may go into dairy. Soap-washed metal and glass don't release taste.
- Pot not washed with soap, or earthenware: disputed.
- Wood/plastic **cutting boards** absorb: keep meat, dairy (and parve) boards. Onion cut on a meat board into dairy without 60× → forbidden, lenient in great need.

## 9. Mixing utensils

- Even for cold parve food, use one set (meat bowl + meat spoon); in a pinch, mixing is OK.
- Institutional kitchens (schools, army, hotels) need much stricter rules, set by the supervising rabbi.

## 10. Parve utensils

- Not required; many keep them as a hiddur (cheaper today).
- Some prefer not to: people are less careful with parve and may confuse it.
- Cooked meat/dairy in a parve pot → hagala. Hot spill → wipe and pour boiling water.
- May wash parve with meat or dairy (not in a greasy dishwasher, or long/very hot soaks).

## 11. Mistakes

- Parve in a meat pot stirred with a clean dairy metal spoon → food kosher, spoon fine.
- Meat dish stirred with a clean dairy metal spoon → food kosher; **spoon needs hagala**.
- Hot meat gravy splashed on a **hot** dairy pot → pot needs hagala, food fine. On a **cold** pot → wipe and pour boiling water on the spot (60×, or add soap).
- Hot meat cut with a clean dairy knife → meat kosher, knife needs hagala. Cold → just clean it.

## 12. Sinks and dishwashers

- Custom: two sinks and two counter sides. In need, one, cleaned between.
- Separate sponges is a custom; with soap, one is fine.
- One dishwasher and racks for both, **not at the same time**. Some keep two racks or two machines.

## 13. Stovetops

- Same grates/glass-top for both: the heat burns any spill.
- Meat and dairy pots at once: keep space. Touching dry → fine; connected by spill → food fine, pots need hagala.
- Food dropped on the metal tray under the grates → don't eat (or cut ~2 cm off a thick piece), unless it's known clean.

## 14. Ovens

- Custom: two compartments, or one type only.
- One oven for both: separate trays; clean and heat ~**½ hour** at max between. Allowed in need even for those who are strict.
- Parve in a meat oven: many heat it first; may be lenient (no real taste).
- Dairy cooked in a meat oven by mistake → food kosher (smell doesn't forbid after the fact); kasher the oven.

## 15. Microwaves

- One microwave for both: never put food directly on the turntable (use a meat/dairy plate) and use separate **covers**.
- Or set it as dairy and wrap/cover meat (and parve-for-meat) food, on an extra plate.
- A treif microwave needs kashering; in need, double-cover the food.

> Line to remember: two permitted foods sit in every kitchen, so the Sages built a whole fence to keep them apart.
