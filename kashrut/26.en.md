## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Dairy → meat** | Eat something firm + drink (or brush teeth), check hands; mid-meal, also clear the table |
| **Meat → dairy** | 6 hours (Sephardim, most Ashkenazim); family customs of 1 or 3 hours are valid |
| **Counting** | End of meat to start of dairy; full 6 lekhatḥila, 5½ in need, 5+ in great need |
| **Meat-cooked food** | With meat taste: wait 6. Just "presumed meaty": no wait |
| **Hard cheese** | No extra wait required; an hour is a hiddur |
| **Kids and the sick** | Gradual: ~1 hr (3–5), ~3 hrs (5–6), toward 6 by 9–10; sick: 1 hour + brush |

## 1. Meat after dairy

- Clean the mouth: **kinuaḥ** (chew something firm like bread or fruit; not flour, dates or leafy greens) + **hadaḥa** (drink). Brushing and rinsing works even better.
- Check your **hands**; wash if greasy from dairy.
- Switching mid-meal (Shavuot): also remove dairy food and dishes and clean the table/change the cloth.

## 2. Dairy after meat

- Meat's taste lingers and bits get stuck. Mar Ukva: his father waited 24 hours; he waited "until the next meal" (Ḥullin 105a).
- Most rishonim: the gap between the two daily meals = at least **6 hours**. Sephardim and most Ashkenazim.
- Some Ashkenazi rishonim (Tosafot): just a separate meal → custom of **1 hour** (some Dutch/German Jews). Others wait **3 hours**.
- Rav Melamed: don't pressure families with a 1- or 3-hour custom to change; without a clear family custom, keep 6.

## 3. Six-hour details

- Count from the **end of eating meat** to the start of dairy (not from the end of the meal).
- Some: into the 6th hour is enough; others: 5½. Today (clocks, precise schedules): a full 6.
- In need: 5½; in great need: just over 5; one who relies on 5+ has support. Mehadrin keep 6 always.

## 4. Why, and details

- Two reasons: **taste** lingers (Rashi), meat stuck in **teeth** (Rambam). Both apply: chewing for a baby or eating soft meat → still wait.
- Found meat in your teeth: take it out (if swallowed, the count isn't broken). After 6 hours: remove, clean mouth, then dairy.

## 5. Meat-cooked foods vs. "presumed meaty"

- Potatoes or soup cooked **with meat** (has meat taste) → wait 6.
- "**Ḥezkat basari**" (no meat taste: salad served with meat cutlery, falafel fried in meat oil) → can't eat it with dairy, but no wait after it.
- Same in reverse for "presumed dairy" after meat.

## 6. Hard cheese

- Some Ashkenazi rishonim waited after strong, aged (6-month or moldy) cheese as after meat; later some extended to all hard cheese.
- Halakha: no extra waiting required. **Hiddur**: one hour.

## 7. Children

- Feeding a child forbidden food is a Torah prohibition; parents must also educate. But not in a way that causes distress or harms health.
- Babies: clean mouth and hands, then dairy. Ages ~3–5: about 1 hour if possible (none if needed for sleep).
- ~5–6: about 3 hours. ~9–10: aim for 6 (3 in need). A year before bar/bat mitzva: 6.

## 8. The sick

- Danger to life overrides everything; a non-dangerous illness doesn't permit forbidden food.
- But for the 6-hour custom: a sick person who needs dairy may wait **1 hour**, brushing and rinsing first.

## 9. The reasons

- A **ḥok** (beyond human reasoning), yet we can learn from it.
- Restrain cruelty: don't take an animal's milk and meat and cook them together (Rashbam, Ibn Ezra, Rav Kook).
- Like **kilayim**: keep each kind distinct (Ḥinukh, R. Hirsch).
- Meat: weighty, complex to prepare, adult, from birth (new life). Milk: light, simple, for children, sustains existing life. Mixing values of different scales distorts the moral compass, hence all the fences.

> Line to remember: meat and milk are each good in their place; the sin is mixing scales that don't belong together.
