## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Multiple sets of lashes; even a tiny whole creature counts |
| **Not yet "swarming"** | Bugs that grew inside picked fruit, flour, meat after slaughter, fish: permitted until they leave |
| **Beriya** | A whole creature isn't nullified even in 1000; a crushed or partial one is nullified in 60 |
| **Checking** | Usually infested → must check. Common minority → check. Rare → no need |
| **Tiny bugs** | Halakha: only what normal eyes see. Middle path: soak with salt/vinegar/soap ~4 min and rinse |
| **Flour today** | Reliable sealed white flour, stored briefly: no need to sift |

## 1. The prohibition

- "Do not make yourselves detestable with any creeping thing… be holy, for I am holy" (Vayikra 11:43–44).
- **Water** creatures (crabs, leeches, worms), **land** creatures (worms, spiders, ants, beetles, lizards, snakes, mice), **flying** ones (flies, mosquitoes, gnats, bees, non-kosher locusts).
- Repeated warnings → 4 / 5 / 6 sets of lashes, vs. one for other non-kosher animals (Makkot 16b).
- Normally you need a kezayit; a sheretz is a **whole creature**, so even a tiny one counts.

## 2. Avoiding the repulsive

- "Al teshaktzu": don't eat disgusting foods or eat in a disgusting way (Rambam; SA 116:6).
- Forbidden foods "block" the soul (tum'a = timtum). "Had I brought Israel out of Egypt only for this…" (Bava Metzia 61b).
- Sheratzim pour all their energy into eating and multiplying, swarming wherever there's rot. Israel, meant to lift reality up, avoids them.

## 3. Only what can swarm

- Water creatures in standing water in pits/caves: part of the water, drinkable. Drawn into a vessel → strain first. Grew in a vessel → fine until they crawl out.
- Bugs that developed inside **picked** fruit, meat, fish or cheese: permitted while inside. Once they come out, they're forbidden (SA 84:4).

## 4. A whole creature isn't nullified

- **Beriya** isn't batel, even in 1000 (some rishonim: batel in ~960; may be relied on in great need).
- A fly lost in a big pot → the whole dish is forbidden; clear soup can be strained, solid pieces rinsed; thick parts forbidden.
- Missing even a leg, or crushed out of shape → batel in 60. Many: a **microscopic** one isn't a beriya either.

## 5. Must you check?

| Status | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Usually infested** | Forbidden until checked or cleaned |
| **Common minority** | Check; if impossible, follow the majority |
| **Rare** | No check needed (most produce); unless rot, webs or holes |

- No fixed definition of "common minority": it depends on growing and storage conditions, so each person judges; kashrut bodies need clearer rules.
- Three bugs found in a "clean" package → the whole package needs checking.

## 6. The dispute about tiny bugs

- Agreed: no need to check for what the eye can't see. But visibility depends on size, eyesight, color/background, knowing what to look for, movement.
- Strict (Rav Vaye, Rav Revaḥ): check for anything visible under the best conditions (light box, experts); can't check → don't eat (corn on the cob, cauliflower).
- Lenient: halakha follows ordinary sight; Jews ate this way for generations.

## 7. The halakha is lenient

- (1) It's a **rabbinic** issue (by Torah law, tasteless bugs are batel).
- (2) Several rishonim: a beriya is batel in ~1000.
- (3) Given the dispute, "common minority" is defined leniently.
- (4) A Torah prohibition can't apply to something you can't taste or feel swallowing (thrips, mites); seeing it and eating anyway is rabbinic.
- (5) In a doubt, unaware with each bite: like "davar she-eino mitkaven."

## 8. Three levels

- Strict view has a place today: modern tools exposed tiny bugs, and new tools (cleaners, fridges, protected growing) can deal with them.
- **Kosher** (basic halakha) / **middle path** (soak with a dissolving agent and rinse) / **mehadrin** (strict).
- Ordinary soaking/rinsing is worth doing; great effort or cost → back to the lenient halakha.
- Factories and big kitchens should be stricter: one breach affects thousands, more temptation, worse storage.

## 9. Leafy greens: the strict approach

- Lettuce, cabbage, parsley, dill, cilantro, artichoke, asparagus, spinach, mint attract thrips and aphids, hard to see and not fully removed by soaking (lab tests).
- Strict: an expert checks each leaf against light. In practice: **bug-free greenhouse** produce (Gush Katif method; e.g., Ḥasalat), pricier and still not perfect.
- Or cold-climate growing (under 14°C); in Israel, controlled winter crops + washing.

## 10. Leafy greens: halakha and middle path

- Halakha: rinse well, look normally, remove what you see.
- Middle path: soak ~**4 minutes** with salt, vinegar or (best) soap / a natural cleaner, then rinse under running water.
- Separate leaves (lettuce, cabbage, artichoke); chop for salad first, then soak and rinse. Low-quality greens with visible bugs: wash again and again.

## 11. Cauliflower and broccoli

- Bugs hide in the florets; soaking doesn't reach them.
- Strict: discard the florets (~40%) and eat the stems, or buy specially grown.
- Halakha: soak ~4 min and rinse well. Since it's cooked and tiny bugs may dissolve, that counts as the middle path.

## 12. Corn, green onions, strawberries

| Item | Strict | Halakha / middle path |
|---|---|---|
| Corn on the cob | Cut kernels off, rinse | Rinse the cob |
| Green onion / leek | Split each leaf, soap, rub | Cut off ~3 cm at the base, separate, soak, rinse |
| Strawberries | Peel, or soak and brush | Cut the top, soak, rinse well (same for raspberries) |

## 13. Fruits of concern

- Figs (fresh and dried), guava (quarter it), dates, apricots: open and check. Rot → check around it.
- Nuts like cashews: webs or gnaw marks → check; if just around the nut and it's whole, fine.
- Packaged dried fruit with webs or powder → check well or throw out.
- **Scale insects** on citrus peel: disputed (Rav S.Z. Auerbach strict for peel recipes); main view lenient.

## 14. Grains, rice, legumes

- Reliable brands from clean stores: no check needed. Suspicious bag → sample; bug found → check all. Webs or powder → thorough check, or just exchange it.
- Bulk, old bags or careless stores → spread on a surface and check.
- **Chickpeas and beans**: larvae inside show as a black dot; soak (boiling ½ hour or lukewarm ~8 hours) to make skins clear, then check. Reliable packaged → just look.

## 15. Flour and sifting

- Bugs that grew in flour and haven't left it are permitted. Seen bugs may have left and returned → forbidden.
- Plain flour: many say no need to check (sfek sfeika); others say check. Custom: **sift**. Baked without sifting → kosher.
- Strict: fine silk sieve (70 mesh) for flour mites. Halakha and middle path: regular ~30 mesh.

## 16. Flour today

- Modern milling, nitrogen/heat and sealed bags keep it clean.
- Reliable sealed white flour, clean store, home-stored a few weeks: **no sifting**; just glance at it.
- Bulk, market, long shelf time, dirty storage, businesses → sift. Open bag a day (hot day, hours) → sift; store in the **fridge**.
- A brand found infested several times loses its presumption. **Whole-wheat** needs sifting unless nitrogen/vacuum or kept cold.

## 17. Meat

- Worms in a live animal's meat are forbidden (sheḥita permits meat, not worms). Grew after slaughter (or in cheese) → permitted while inside, unless repulsive.
- Store meat is clean; protect it from flies in the kitchen (screens, traps).

## 18. Fish

- Worms that grew in fish flesh, even while alive, are permitted while inside (fish needs no sheḥita).
- Worms from outside (gut, mouth, gills, skin) are forbidden → buy supervised, or discard innards, peel skin, clean mouth/gills (or drop the head).
- **Anisakis** in salmon/herring: main view lenient (larva was microscopic when swallowed); ideally strict (big, gross, unhealthy) → buy with supervision.

> Line to remember: the Torah asks us to see with our own eyes, not a microscope; but holiness also means not eating what disgusts.
