## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Contact | How deep |
|---|---|
| **Cold** | Nothing absorbed: rinse or scrape |
| **Kli rishon** (≥45°C, even off the fire) | Full cooking: need 60 |
| **Iruy** (poured from kli rishon) | A peel (kedei kelipa) |
| **Kli sheni** | Lekhatḥila a peel; kli shelishi: like cold. Solid lumps: strict lekhatḥila |
| **Roasting together** | Fatty: whole (60); lean: a finger's width |
| **Tata'ah gavar; salty; pickled** | Bottom wins; salted = hot; soaked 24 hrs = cooked |

## 1. Cold

- Avoid contact, but cold and dry → permitted without rinsing (brush off crumbs). Moist or greasy → rinse (and rub).
- Meat touched soft cheese → discard the touched cheese, rinse the meat.
- Bread touched moist meat → fine with meat; for dairy scrape the spot (it can't be rinsed).
- Raw meat fell into cold milk → rinse meat; milk fine. Rema: cooked/roasted meat → peel it; cracked or spicy → all forbidden unless great loss.

## 2. Kli rishon

- A pot on the fire mixes tastes fully → need 60.
- Off the fire but still at least **yad soledet** (~45°C) → still treated as cooking. Below → like cold.
- Even if often it can't cook, the Sages treat it as full cooking (absent a reliable taster); removing the treif piece doesn't help without 60.

## 3. Pouring (iruy)

- Pouring from a kli rishon cooks a **peel** (the thinnest layer that can be removed).
- Hot treif gravy poured on meat → peel the surface, rinse the rest.
- Hot water poured over a kosher piece and a nevela together → peel the kosher one.

## 4. Kli sheni, kli shelishi, solids

- **Kli sheni**: its walls weren't on the fire → doesn't cook; lekhatḥila assume a peel (zucchini in treif soup → peel it).
- **Kli shelishi** and pouring from a kli sheni → like cold.
- **Gush** (solid: meat, kugel, potato) keeps heat: some say it cooks even in a third vessel while yad soledet. Lekhatḥila strict, great loss lenient.

## 5. Why these rules

- They're **fences** for doubtful cases, not claims about reality. Most tastes vanish in 40×, but strong ones need 60, so 60 for all.
- Without 60, removing the treif piece doesn't help, and it can forbid a second and third dish too (all its taste may have come out each time). Time in the pot doesn't matter.
- A reliable taster resolves the doubt (custom: only in great loss).
- Disputes are about how far the fence goes: remote cases yes, extremely rare ones no. Taste a forbidden flavor anyway? Stop; what you ate wasn't a sin.

## 6. Roasting together

- Kosher and treif pieces roasted touching: if either is **fatty**, taste spreads through → need 60. Both **lean** → remove a **kedei netila** (finger's width, ~1.9 cm).
- Fat content is usually unclear → be strict both ways: no 60 → forbidden; with 60 → remove a finger's width. Clearly lean non-meat → just netila.

## 7. Tata'ah gavar

- Heat rises, so the **bottom** wins.
- Cold treif into hot kosher soup → full cooking → need 60 against the piece.
- Hot treif into cold kosher soup → only a peel → 60 against its peel.
- Two pieces stacked: hot bottom → like fatty roasting; cold bottom → peel the kosher one where they touched.

## 8. Melia'ḥ ke-rote'aḥ

- Food too salty to eat alone, touching with moisture, transfers taste **like boiling**. The salty one gives but doesn't receive.
- Salty meat on bland cheese → cheese affected; rinse the meat. Both salty → both.
- Lean: a peel. Fatty: all, unless 60. Unclear → strict both ways.

## 9. Kavush ke-mevushal

- Soaked together in liquid for a full **24 hours** → like cooking → need 60.
- Very salty or sharp liquid → as soon as ~18 minutes; less → a peel.
- Vessels: treif soup left 24 hours in a pot → kasher lekhatḥila; food already cooked in it → permitted.

## 10. Smell and steam

- Cake picked up the smell of fatty nevela baked in the same oven: avoid lekhatḥila, but **smell doesn't forbid** bediavad.
- Heavy **steam** under one shared cover → forbids (Rema). Zucchini used as a lid over treif soup → forbidden.

> Line to remember: hot from below cooks all the way; hot from above only scratches the surface.
