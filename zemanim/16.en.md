## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Why** | Joy of body and soul, love between Jews, and caring for the poor |
| **Matanot la-evyonim** | 2 gifts to **2 poor people** (money or food), each ~a simple meal; not from ma'aser |
| **Mishlo'aḥ manot** | 2 different, ready-to-eat foods to **1 person**; respectable portions |
| **Who** | Everyone, including women and bar/bat mitzva kids; a couple gives 2 mishlo'aḥ + 4 gifts |
| **When** | Purim **day**; matanot may wait if no poor are found |
| **Priority** | Rambam: spend more on the poor than on mishlo'aḥ manot and the se'uda |
| **Se'uda** | By day, with bread, ideally meat and wine, with others; usually after Minḥa |
| **Drinking** | Drink more than usual; "until he doesn't know," but never to the point of shame or harm |

## 1. Joy and kindness

- "Days of feasting and joy, mishlo'aḥ manot… and matanot la-evyonim" (Esther 9:22).
- Haman threatened body and soul → joy is **spiritual (Megilla) and physical (se'uda)**, with wine.
- True joy spreads outward: **mishlo'aḥ manot** for friends.
- And to those who can't rejoice: **matanot la-evyonim**. Ignoring the poor makes the party hollow.

## 2. Jewish unity

- Haman's decree targeted **every** Jew → everyone must be part of the joy.
- Disunity let Haman slander us ("a people scattered and dispersed"); unity ("gather all the Jews") saved us.
- Unity is a precondition for receiving the Torah ("like one man with one heart"); on Purim we re-accepted it out of love.

## 3. Matanot la-evyonim

- Minimum **two gifts to two poor people**; more is praiseworthy.
- **Money or food** (usable for the se'uda), not clothes or books. The poor may use it as he likes.
- Each gift ≈ a simple meal (~3 slices of bread; in Israel about ₪1 minimum).
- Not from **ma'aser**; you may top up the minimum with ma'aser.
- Evyon = can't cover his family's basic needs by today's standards.
- A poor couple, or a widow and dependent child, count as two. Two gifts to **one** person doesn't work.
- Don't know anyone? Give through a reliable **gabbai tzedaka**.

## 4. Mishlo'aḥ manot

- **Two portions of food to one person**, to increase love.
- Food, because shared good food builds friendship, and it helps those who can't afford a fancy meal, with dignity.
- Two portions show you want your friend to enjoy variety, not just not go hungry. More is praiseworthy.

## 5. What counts

- Two **different** foods (bread and meat, fish and eggs, cake and apples; different cuts or cooking methods).
- Not clothes or books (can add them after the food).
- **Ready to eat**: not a live chicken; maybe not raw meat. **Cans** are fine.
- Drinks (wine, beer, good juice): most say yes; to satisfy all, give at least one with two foods.
- **Respectable** portions: not one plum. Le-khatḥila ~3 eggs' volume each, fitting both people's standing.

## 6. Who is obligated

- **Everyone**, including women (they were part of the miracle). Modesty: women to women, men to men (charity is fine either way).
- Married couple: **two mishlo'aḥ manot** (no need to label whose) and **four gifts** to the poor; the husband can give for her.
- Kids past bar/bat mitzva are obligated; mishlo'aḥ manot must be **in their name**; parents may give matanot for them.
- Educate younger kids: let them deliver or send their own; tell them what you gave for them.
- A poor person is obligated too; two can **swap** meals.

## 7. When

- **Purim day**; at night doesn't count.
- No poor found → set the money aside; that's the mitzva. A gabbai may distribute after Purim.
- Mishlo'aḥ manot only on the day; can't be made up. Alone? Call or email someone to send on your behalf.
- In advance: give the gabbai money, keeping ownership until Purim; or leave mishlo'aḥ manot with a friend as your shali'aḥ.

## 8. Comparing the two

- Mishlo'aḥ manot is for love → **not anonymous**. Matanot is charity → **preferably anonymous**.
- Inviting a friend to the meal works: put **two portions** in front of him and tell him it's mishlo'aḥ manot.
- For a poor guest, better not to say it's matanot; or send the poor a nice mishlo'aḥ manot so he isn't embarrassed.
- **Rambam**: better to spend more on the poor than on the meal and mishlo'aḥ manot; gladdening the poor resembles the Shekhina. Ma'aser given through the year may count toward this.

## 9. Rejoicing and the se'uda

- The joy peaks at the **se'uda**. One set meal, **by day**; at night doesn't count (Megilla 7b).
- Also a mitzva to eat a nicer meal at **night**; some eat seeds and legumes (Daniel, Esther).
- Increase joy all day: song, dance, friends, Torah, good food and drink.
- Some joy on the **other** day of Purim too.
- **Meat** if possible (or poultry, or other tasty food with wine). **Bread** to make it a real meal. Eat **with others**.

## 10. The mitzva to drink

- Festivals: drink wine, not a lot. Purim: "**mishteh**," an explicit mitzva to drink a lot. "Until he doesn't know between arur Haman and barukh Mordekhai" (Megilla 7b).
- View 1 (Rif, Rosh): literally, a state where all is good. If you'll act badly, drink until you **fall asleep**.
- View 2: drink **more than usual**, tipsy, not wild (Rabbeinu Ephraim; Tosafot/Ran: until you stumble saying the phrase).
- Each person chooses the way that lets him rejoice for the sake of Heaven.

## 11. Laws of drinking

- Any intoxicating drink; preferably **wine** (the miracle came through wine). Start with wine if you like it at all.
- **Women**: drink joyfully, but **don't get drunk** (tzni'ut).
- Drinking makes you sad or sick → just a little more than usual. Crying from joy or longing is fine.
- If drunkenness makes you violent or degrading → don't get drunk. Self-control all year is real growth.
- Alcohol peaks after ~**20 minutes**: wait **½ hour between drinks**, drink with the meal.

## 12. The meaning of drinking

- Drunkenness is usually shameful, but the Purim miracles happened through **wine** (Vashti, Haman at Esther's feast).
- On Purim wine shows **simple joy** and that even Israel's physical side is holy.
- "Until he doesn't know": a faith beyond our understanding, accepting that God runs the world for good.

## 13. When to eat the meal

- Most: **afternoon, after Minḥa** (mornings are for gifts; pray before getting drunk).
- Starting just before sunset is questioned; "everything follows the beginning" (Al Ha-nisim in benching). Better to start with plenty of daylight.
- Some prefer the **morning** (sober by Minḥa); custom is afternoon.
- Learn some **Torah** first ("ora" = Torah).
- Will drinking make you miss Minḥa/Ma'ariv? Many: don't get drunk. R. Kook: one busy with a mitzva is exempt from another.

## 14. Drunk or tipsy: berakhot and Ma'ariv

- **Tipsy** (shatui): could still speak before a king. **Drunk** (shikor): couldn't.
- Both may say birkhot ha-nehenin, **Birkat Ha-mazon**, Asher Yatzar.
- Ma'ariv: wait to sober up. To catch a minyan: tipsy may pray from a siddur; **drunk may not** (his prayer is invalid).
- About to fall asleep: tipsy prays fully; drunk says **Shema** without its berakhot and skips the Amida; make up the Amida at Shaḥarit (tashlumin).
- Unsure which? On Purim you may be lenient and pray.

## 15. Purim on Friday

- Start the meal **in the morning** in honor of Shabbat; at least 3 hours before sunset; be-di'avad until sunset. Eat moderately.
- Or **combine** with the Friday night meal: Minḥa, start the meal, ~½ hour before sunset light candles, cover the bread, kiddush **without Ha-gafen**, eat a keveitza (or kezayit) of bread, benching with **Retzei** + Al Ha-nisim in the Ha-Raḥaman, then Ma'ariv.

## 16. Costumes and lo yilbash

- No source in the Sages, but reasons: more joy; letting loose; breaking social barriers (unity); noticing externals vs. inner self; Jews who look like gentiles stay Jews inside.
- Mahari Mintz and Rema: cross-dressing for joy is permitted.
- **Most poskim (Baḥ, Taz): forbidden**; this is the correct practice. Changing one garment while gender stays obvious → don't protest.

> Line to remember: on Purim, joy that leaves out the poor isn't joy; give more to the poor than to the party.
