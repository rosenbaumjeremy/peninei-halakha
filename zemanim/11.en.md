## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Why it lasted** | Of all the Second Temple holidays, only Ḥanuka survived: the miracle of the oil + the mitzva of candles |
| **The story** | Greek rule → Antiochus' decrees (167 BCE) → Matityahu's revolt → Temple rededicated 25 Kislev 165 BCE |
| **Its meaning** | The light of the Oral Torah, which shines in the dark of exile |
| **Prayers** | Al Ha-nisim (Amida + Birkat Ha-mazon), full Hallel all 8 days, Torah reading on the nesi'im |
| **Forbidden** | Fasting and eulogies (even on a yahrtzeit); Taḥanun is omitted |
| **Meals** | No obligation, but a mitzva meal with Torah and song; dairy and fried foods |
| **Women** | Custom: no work (laundry, sewing) for the first half-hour the candles burn |

## 1. The holiday that endured

- Shabbat 21b: the Greeks defiled the oils; one sealed cruse was found, enough for a day, and it burned **eight days**. The next year the days became a holiday of **Hallel and thanksgiving**.
- Megilat Ta'anit listed many Hasmonean victory days (e.g. 13 Adar, Nicanor Day). After the Second Temple's destruction they were all **abolished**.
- Only Ḥanuka remains, because of the miracle of the oil and the candle mitzva. With it we kept Al Ha-nisim, Hallel, and no fasting or eulogies.

## 2. The Greek Empire

- Alexander the Great (Aristotle's student) conquered the known world in 3 years (334–331 BCE); his generals split it into Greek kingdoms.
- Hellenism swallowed every culture. In Judea it spread slowly, mostly among the rich.
- Even the High Priests (Jason, Menelaus) were Hellenizers. Jason built a **gymnasium** by the Temple; priests left the service to watch wrestling.

## 3. Decrees and rebellion

- Antiochus IV (169 BCE) looted the Temple; in 167 BCE he outlawed Torah: no mila, Shabbat or sacrifices; idols in the Temple; Torah scrolls burned.
- In **Modi'in**, Matityahu the Kohen killed the Greek officer and the collaborators. The new thing: fighting back instead of only dying al kiddush Hashem.
- **Yehuda the Maccabee** led the fight; after two years they took Jerusalem and on **25 Kislev 165 BCE** began purifying the Temple.
- The Greeks later lifted the decrees, but the Hasmoneans fought on until political independence decades later.
- Had the Greeks been patient, Judea might have assimilated. God "hardened Antiochus' heart," as with Pharaoh.

## 4. Crises in the Hasmonean dynasty

- After the military win, the cultural fight returned. The **Sadducees** tried to mix Written Torah with Greek culture.
- Tragedy: Matityahu's descendants became Hellenizers. King **Yannai** persecuted the Sages and ordered them killed at his death; his wife Shlomtziyon cancelled it.
- Herod (their slave) wiped out the line: "Anyone who says he is a Hasmonean is a slave" (BB 3b).
- Critique: they took the **kingship** (meant for Yehuda) as well as the High Priesthood.

## 5. Lasting spiritual achievements

- Independence ended a ~40% tax, brought growth, settlement and aliya.
- Eretz Yisrael became the spiritual center again; the **Oral Torah** took shape, carrying us through 2,000 years of exile.
- That's why the **oil** is the symbol, not the battles: the dynasty fell, the Torah light lasted.
- In the long run Judaism's ideas (one God, ethics, fixing the world) broke Hellenistic paganism.

## 6. The Oral Torah: light in the darkness

- Ḥanuka falls in the longest, darkest nights, around a new moon. We go out and light.
- It is the holiday of the **Oral Torah**: set by the Sages, and its candles were one of their first mitzvot.
- Written Torah = midday sun; Oral Torah = moon and stars, reaching the dark corners.
- That's why almost every Jew lights, and everyone does it **mehadrin min ha-mehadrin**.

## 7. Adding a candle each night

- Ideas fade with time, but faith in God grows; so we **add** a light each night.
- **Eight** = beyond nature (7 = creation): mila on day 8, Shavu'ot after 7 weeks, Shemini Atzeret.
- Ḥanuka reveals the Oral Torah's above-nature stature → 8 nights.

## 8. Al Ha-nisim, Hallel, Torah reading

- **Al Ha-nisim** in Modim of the Amida and in Nodeh Lekha of Birkat Ha-mazon; not in Me-ein Shalosh.
- Forgot it? **No repeat.** Before saying Hashem's name at the end of that berakha → go back. Later → say it at the end of the Amida, or in the Ha-Raḥaman section of benching.
- **Full Hallel with a berakha all 8 days** (Pesaḥ only gets it on day 1).
- Women are exempt (time-bound); may say it. Berakha: Ashkenazim yes; many Sephardim no.
- Torah reading: the **nesi'im's offerings**, one per day; day 8 continues to the Menora (Be-ha'alotekha).

## 9. No fasting, eulogies or cemetery visits

- No fasting, even on a parent's **yahrtzeit**; a bride and groom don't fast on their wedding day.
- No eulogies at funerals or end of shiva/shloshim, except a **Torah scholar** in the presence of the body. Mourning laws still apply.
- Many don't visit cemeteries (Moroccan Jews and others do). Graves of tzaddikim are OK for all.
- Taḥanun and La-menatze'aḥ omitted; mourners customarily don't lead services.

## 10. Festive meals

- Unlike Purim, **no obligatory** se'uda: the Greeks attacked the spirit, not the body (Levush).
- Still, many say a meal is a mitzva. Practice: eat with **words of Torah and songs** so it's a se'udat mitzva by all opinions.
- **Dairy**: recalls Yehudit, who fed the enemy general cheese and killed him. **Fried** foods: sufganiyot, latkes.

## 11. Women not working

- Custom: women don't work while the candles burn, in practice only the **first half-hour**.
- Reasons: (1) not to use the candles' light by mistake; (2) the day's sanctity, which women merit because of Yehudit's courage.
- Forbidden: Ḥol Ha-mo'ed-type work (laundry, sewing). **Cooking and frying are fine.**

> Line to remember: the dynasty fell, but the little cruse of oil, the light of the Oral Torah, never went out.
