## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Sages: outside the door, on the left (mezuza on the right); upper floor: window to the street |
| **Height** | Ideally 3–10 tefaḥim; above 20 amot (if no one sees it) doesn't count |
| **Apartments** | A window or porch facing the street beats the hallway door |
| **When** | Israeli practice: at **tzeit**; ideally light by **9 PM**; all night in need |
| **Friday** | Before Shabbat candles, with fuel until ½ hour after tzeit |
| **Motza'ei Shabbat** | After Ma'ariv; havdala first or candles first, both fine |
| **Guests** | Eating only → light at home · Sleeping there → share the host's candles (a pruta) |
| **Away from home** | Spouse lights at home → you're covered; Ashkenazim may still light with a berakha |

## 1. Where to light

- Shabbat 21b: at the **doorway, outside**; upper floor → a **window facing the street**; in danger → on the table.
- To the **left** of the door: surrounded by mitzvot (mezuza right, candles left).
- Height **3–10 tefaḥim** (~9–30 in.). Higher looks like light for the entrance; lower looks temporary. Be-di'avad fine; above **20 amot** doesn't count.

## 2. Private homes

- Today wind blows out outdoor candles; only a **glass box** protects them, and the Sages never required buying one.
- So you may light **inside, at a window to the street**, publicizing just as well (minus being "surrounded by mitzvot").
- Top hiddur: glass box at the door, on the left. With several lighters, keep menoras distinct, or some light at the window.
- No walled courtyards today → light at the house door, not the yard gate.
- Door hidden but window visible: the **window** seems better (more publicity); the door has its own merit.

## 3. Apartment buildings

- Best: a **window or porch facing the street**. Use a menora with no back so both family and passersby see it.
- Window above 10 tefaḥim is still better than lower but unseen.
- Hallway by the apartment door works, but the street window is better.
- Even above 20 amot, if passersby can see it, the window is preferable. Inside the home still fulfills the mitzva.

## 4. Time and duration

- "From sunset until the market empties out" (Shabbat 21b). Start of sunset (crowds) vs. end (darker): Israeli practice is **tzeit**, ~20 min after shki'a.
- Men who pray Ma'ariv at tzeit: **pray first** (more frequent, plus Shema), then rush home. Those who pray later: light at tzeit.
- Many make the candles last **2+ hours** today, since people are out later.

## 5. Friday and Saturday night

- Friday: light **before** Shabbat candles, before the community's Shabbat time (Israel ~20 min before sunset).
- Enough fuel to last until **½ hour after tzeit**.
- Prefer Minḥa with a minyan first, but don't miss the minyan for it.
- Motza'ei Shabbat: **Ma'ariv first**. Then havdala → candles (Taz), or candles → havdala (light sooner). Both fine; if candles first, say Ata Ḥonantanu or "ha-mavdil" first.

## 6. Delaying when needed

- Ideal remains **tzeit to ½ hour after**.
- Hard to be home? Light later; try not to pass **9 PM**. Don't eat a meal (keveitza of bread/mezonot) before lighting.
- A regular post-Ma'ariv shiur: keep it, light afterward (Torah study takes precedence).

## 7. Waiting for a family member

- Technically one spouse's lighting covers both, so light at tzeit.
- Usually better to **wait**: (1) the other may not hear She-asa nisim elsewhere; (2) feelings; (3) their connection to the mitzva (especially Sephardic kids).
- Light without them only if it's one-off and they'll hear berakhot elsewhere. Even waiting, not past **9 PM**; no meal from ½ hour before tzeit.

## 8. Very late or before shki'a

- Missed 9 PM: may light **all night until dawn**. Berakha only if someone (street or family) will see it.
- Missed the whole night: lost; next night light normally.
- Weekday before shki'a only when there's no choice: from **plag ha-minḥa** (Israel ~40 min before sunset), with a berakha per most. Up to an hour before: **without** berakha.

## 9. Guests

- **Eating only** at the hosts: not your home. Best that one of you goes home to light at tzeit; the others see the hosts' candles. Far away: light with berakhot at home later.
- **Eating and sleeping**: temporary home → buy a share for a **pruta** (or get it as a gift).
- Ashkenazim: each guest lights his own with berakhot anyway.
- **Separate apartment** given to them: light there with berakhot, by all customs.

## 10. Guests on Shabbat Ḥanuka

- Friday: buy a share in the host's candles; Ashkenazim better light their own. Separate apartment → light there.
- Saturday night: home soon → light at home. Home very late → light like Friday. In between → **your choice**.

## 11. A married person away from home

- Husband away, wife home and lighting → he's covered. Still see candles and hear berakhot (She-asa nisim).
- Ashkenazim: he may light with berakhot, ideally before she does. Sephardim: don't.
- Hotel/empty apartment: light there. Ashkenazim with berakhot; Sephardim: in Israel without, abroad with.
- Reservist: his wife covers him; hear someone's berakhot. If nobody lights, light in the mess hall with berakhot (even Sephardim, for the single soldiers).
- If the wife is **also away**, the husband must light where he is, with berakhot.

## 12. Unmarried people living alone

- Same as a family: own place (owned or rented) → light there.
- Visiting but sleeping at home → go home to light.
- Sleeping at the host → a pruta share (Ashkenazim: better light their own). Separate place → light there with berakhot.

## 13. Yeshiva students, soldiers, college students

- Yeshiva dorm: light **in the room**, at a street window if there is one, else left of the door.
- Sephardim: many say a student's home is his parents' → covered by his father. Others: he's independent, one roommate lights for all (others buy a share).
- Soldiers and college students: Ashkenazim (and some Sephardim) light in the room with berakhot; most Sephardim rely on parents; if no one in the room lights, light without a berakha.
- Same for women in seminaries or dorms.

## 14. Hotels

- Must light. Families and single people: with berakhot. Covered at home: Sephardim without, Ashkenazim with.
- Best **in the room** (street window if possible). Stay ½ hour; after that, put out what's still burning. If not allowed, the dining room.

## 15. Hospital patients

- Married, or young living with parents → covered at home.
- Ashkenazim may light in the room with berakhot; most Sephardim don't.
- Room is best; the cafeteria works.
- Try to **hear someone's berakhot** (She-asa nisim; first night also She-heḥeyanu).

## 16. Fields and vehicles

- Dispute: is the mitzva only for someone with a **home**?
- So: sleeping in a field, a trench or a tiny tent (< ~2 m²) → light **without** berakhot. A large tent → with berakhot at its entrance.
- Overnight train, plane or ship with cabins → **with** berakhot. If only one candle is allowed for everyone, all are covered.

> Line to remember: light where the most people will see it, when they're still on the street, in the place you call home tonight.
