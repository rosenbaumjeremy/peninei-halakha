## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Seven mitzvot** | Megilla, mishlo'aḥ manot, matanot la-evyonim, se'uda + Torah reading, Al Ha-nisim, no fasting/eulogies |
| **Dates** | 14 Adar everywhere; **15** in cities walled since Yehoshua (today: Jerusalem) and Shushan |
| **Megilla** | Night and day; day is the main one; with a large crowd |
| **Who** | Men, women, converts; a minor can't read for adults |
| **Every word** | Miss one word → not yotzei; read it right away from a printed text |
| **The scroll** | Ink on parchment, by hand; small letter errors are OK be-di'avad |
| **Berakhot** | 3 before (She-heḥeyanu at day: Ashkenazim yes, Sephardim no); Ha-rav et riveinu after, with 10 |
| **Work** | Custom: no ordinary work on Purim day; night is fine |

## 1. The miracle of Purim

- After the First Temple's destruction, few returned. Jews in Persia **assimilated**, bowed to the idol, and enjoyed Aḥashverosh's feast with the Temple vessels.
- An accusation in Heaven → **Haman** (from Amalek): kill all Jews on 13 Adar.
- Some blamed Mordechai for not bowing.
- God had prepared the cure in advance (Esther as queen). Everything **reversed**: Haman hanged on his own tree; Jewish prestige grew; the Second Temple was built.

## 2. Accepting the Torah anew

- Haman's decree revealed the Jews' **segula**: they could have assimilated and survived, yet they repented.
- Sinai: "the mountain like a cask" (Shabbat 88a), a kind of coercion. **"Kiyemu ve-kibelu"** (Esther 9:27): they now accepted it **willingly**.
- They even added new mitzvot. This opened the door to the Second Temple and the Oral Torah.

## 3. A holiday for all generations

- Esther asked: "write me" (into Tanakh) and "establish me" (as a holiday). The Sages hesitated (provoking the nations; another Amalek text), then found allusions.
- **Anshei Knesset Ha-gedola** (120 elders, incl. Ezra and Mordechai) wrote the Megilla with ru'aḥ ha-kodesh.
- "Esther is the end of all the miracles": the last miracle written into Tanakh.
- Purim's mitzvot are **divrei kabbala**, between Torah and rabbinic.
- **Seven mitzvot**: Megilla, mishlo'aḥ manot, matanot la-evyonim, se'uda (unique); Torah reading, Al Ha-nisim, no fasting/eulogies.

## 4. Walled and unwalled cities

- 13 Adar: fighting everywhere; **14**: rest and celebration.
- In **Shushan**, Esther asked for another day of battle, so they rested on the **15th**.
- Prominent (walled) cities like Shushan celebrate the 15th. In **honor of Eretz Yisrael**, "walled" means walled **in Yehoshua bin Nun's time**, even if now ruined. Shushan is the exception.
- Today only **Jerusalem** certainly reads on the 15th (more in ch. 17).

## 5. Reading the Megilla and publicizing the miracle

- Obligated: men, women, converts. Hearing a reader works, if he's obligated. **Not from a minor.**
- Purpose: show God runs the world and even the worst turns to good.
- Kohanim delayed the Tamid offering for it; Torah scholars stop learning to hear it with the crowd.
- Big shul with several minyanim → try to gather together. A regular small minyan is fine.
- Alone only as a last resort (still with a berakha).

## 6. When to read

- **Night and day**: they cried out day and night.
- Night: tzeit to dawn; day: sunrise (be-di'avad dawn) to shki'a. The zealous read right after Ma'ariv and Shaḥarit.
- No eating or sleeping before the reading; learning is fine. Hard after the fast → drink (not alcohol), or a snack (fruit; < keveitza of mezonot).
- Day: same; a woman who hasn't heard yet shouldn't eat a meal (snack OK).
- Many Rishonim: the **day** reading is the main one; make extra effort for a big crowd.

## 7. Women

- Rashi, Rambam: women are obligated like men and may read for men. Behag: women must **hear**, men must **read**.
- So a woman reads for a man only in pressing need.
- A woman may read for women, with the same berakha (some disagree); with 10 women, Ha-rav et riveinu. Le-khatḥila hear it from a man, ideally in shul.
- A man reading for women: he says the berakha, or one woman does. Both fine.

## 8. Minors

- Train children once they can listen to the **whole** Megilla properly, usually around **9**.
- Bring 5–6 year olds if they won't disturb; don't make others miss words.
- The congregation reads four verses aloud (Ish Yehudi, U-Mordekhai yatza, La-Yehudim, Ki Mordekhai), then the reader repeats them.
- Noisemakers excite the kids, but **no noise while the reader reads**.

## 9. The scroll

- Like a Torah scroll: **ink on parchment**, tanned for it, sewn with sinew, lines scored, by hand, with intent.
- Difference: missing/faded letters are OK be-di'avad if most is right (it's an "iggeret," a letter); fill in the missing words orally.
- A translation on parchment would technically work, but we don't do it; hearing Hebrew without understanding works, with intent.

## 10. Reading and missing a word

- From a **kosher scroll**, not by heart or from print. Most from the scroll, the rest from print, works if no word is missed.
- Reader skips a word or changes its meaning → **reread** (most poskim).
- **Listener misses a word → not yotzei.** The big practical issue: noise after Haman's name.
- Fix: read the missing words **immediately** from your printed copy and catch up. Don't read along otherwise.

## 11. Laws of reading

- Spread out and folded like a **letter**; afterward roll it up, then Ha-rav et riveinu.
- Sit or stand; only the **reader stands**. Most stand for the berakhot.
- With cantillation; without it, be-di'avad.
- **In order**: fill in a miss right away, not at the end.
- Dozed off → not yotzei. A pause or talking in the middle doesn't invalidate.
- **Microphone, phone, radio**: many say not yotzei. Avoid; in no-choice cases may rely on the lenient. Hard of hearing: use a device if needed.

## 12. Berakhot and order

- Night: **Al mikra Megilla**, **She-asa nisim**, **She-heḥeyanu**.
- Reading without a berakha still counts with intent.
- Day She-heḥeyanu: **Sephardim no**, **Ashkenazim yes** (the day reading is its own mitzva, and more important).
- Have the other mitzvot in mind for She-heḥeyanu (Sephardim at night, Ashkenazim by day).
- **Ha-rav et riveinu** after rolling up, only with **ten** present.

## 13. Revenge on Haman and his sons

- Justice done: rising against Israel is rebelling against God.
- The ten sons are written as a "song," but in a closed, **linear** layout: names right, "et" left, unlike the Song at the Sea (Maharal: strict justice, not expansive salvation).
- Read all ten in **one breath** (they died together); a tall **vav** in Vaizata.
- After: "Arur Haman, barukh Mordekhai… and Ḥarvona too is remembered for good."
- "Beating" Haman: from the Rishonim; Rema: don't mock customs. Fine as long as **everyone hears every word**.

## 14. Al Ha-nisim, Torah reading, eulogies, Taḥanun

- Al Ha-nisim in the Amida and in benching (not Me-ein Shalosh). Forgot → no repeat; same as Ḥanuka.
- Meal began on Purim and ran into the night → still say Al Ha-nisim.
- Torah: **"Va-yavo Amalek"**, 3 aliyot, only **9 verses** (some repeat the last). God's name isn't complete while Amalek exists.
- **No Hallel**: (1) miracle outside the Land; (2) still Aḥashverosh's servants; (3) the Megilla is the Hallel.
- No fasting or eulogies on **14 and 15** everywhere (except a Torah scholar in the body's presence). No Taḥanun both days.
- Wear **Shabbat clothes** night and day.

## 15. Work on Purim

- Not originally forbidden, but a binding **custom**: "whoever works won't see blessing from it."
- Allowed: avoiding a big loss; a poor person who needs to eat.
- **Joyful** work (prepare a son's home, plant decorative trees), mitzva work (Torah insights), light work (a letter), if it doesn't spoil the joy.
- Nails OK; laundry, sewing, haircut not, unless needed for Purim.
- Business technically OK; better avoid; open only shops for Purim needs.
- **Night is allowed.** Each city may work on the other's Purim.

> Line to remember: every word of the Megilla counts, because every detail of the story shows God's hidden hand.
