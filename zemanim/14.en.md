## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Adar** | "When Adar arrives we increase joy": the month that turns bad into good |
| **Four parshiyot** | Shekalim, Zakhor, Para, Ha-ḥodesh, read as maftir from a second scroll |
| **Leap year** | Purim and the parshiyot in **Adar II**; Purim Katan (14–15 Adar I): no fasting or Taḥanun |
| **Amalek** | Three mitzvot: remember, don't forget, wipe out |
| **Zakhor** | Torah-level per most; hear it from a scroll with kavana, once a year |
| **Women** | Exempt per most, but best to hear Zakhor |
| **Ta'anit Esther** | 13 Adar, by custom; Shabbat → moved to Thursday |
| **Half-shekel** | Charity before Minḥa of the fast; custom: a half-coin for each family member |

## 1. When Adar arrives

- Ta'anit 29a: Av → less joy; **Adar → more joy**. Times have their own nature.
- Adar can **turn bad into good**. Ordinary joy is incomplete while evil exists; when bad itself becomes good, joy is complete.
- A Jew with a court case against a non-Jew should avoid Av and aim for Adar.

## 2. The four parshiyot

- Two scrolls: the week's parsha in seven aliyot, then the special parsha as **maftir**; the haftara matches the special reading.
- **Shekalim**: reminder of the yearly half-shekel for communal offerings from Nisan. Usually the Shabbat before Adar.
- **Zakhor**: remember Amalek, the Shabbat before Purim (Haman was from Amalek).
- **Para**: purification before the Pesaḥ offering.
- **Ha-ḥodesh**: Nisan is the first month; prepare for Pesaḥ.
- Rosh Ḥodesh Adar/Nisan on Shabbat → **three scrolls**.
- Zakhor is Torah-level (most poskim); some say Para too, hence extra care.

## 3. Leap years

- Months follow the moon, years the sun (Pesaḥ in spring). Add **Adar II**: 7 leap years per **19-year cycle**.
- Purim and the four parshiyot in **Adar II**: Purim's redemption next to Pesaḥ's.
- **Purim Katan**, 14–15 Adar I: no fasting, eulogies or Taḥanun; a nicer meal.
- Joy already starts in Adar I.

## 4. Three mitzvot about Amalek

- (1) **Remember**, (2) **don't forget**, (3) **wipe out** their memory (Devarim 25:17–19).
- Amalek = the root of evil and of Jew-hatred: attacked newly freed slaves, unprovoked.
- "God's name and throne are not complete until Amalek's name is wiped out" (Rashi).
- A Jew is naturally merciful; the Torah demands we remember evil and fight it without compromise.

## 5. Wiping out Amalek

- Mainly on the **nation**: king → destroy Amalek → build the Temple (San. 20b).
- **Shaul** spared Agag and the best animals → lost the kingdom. Survivors kept attacking (Tziklag; 400 fled).
- From Agag came **Haman the Agagite** (Megilla 13a).
- Individuals are commanded too (Ḥinukh 604), but Amalek's descendants are unknown today.

## 6. Parashat Zakhor

- Once a year is enough: forgetting = a whole year without remembering. Read the Shabbat **before Purim**.
- Remember **out loud**; hearing someone read works.
- Many Rishonim: from a **Torah scroll**. Use a fine scroll, read very precisely.
- Ideally in your own family's tradition, but any accepted reading (Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Yemenite) works.
- No minyan → read from a scroll alone; no scroll → from a ḥumash.
- Needs **kavana**; the gabbai should announce it.

## 7. Women and Zakhor

- Most: **exempt**, tied to waging war (Ḥinukh 603). Minḥat Ḥinukh: obligated, women help in war and it isn't time-bound.
- Practice: exempt, but **best to hear it**; many do. Can't come? Read it from a ḥumash.
- A Torah reading for women at a shiur (even without a minyan) is fine.

## 8. Can an Amalekite be saved or convert?

- An Amalekite who accepts the **seven Noahide laws** is no longer "Amalek."
- We **offer peace** first (Noahide laws + tribute). Refused → war to the end, no peace later (Rambam).
- Conversion: **Rambam yes** (Haman's descendants taught Torah in Bnei Brak). Mekhilta: **no**; explained as a mistake, descent via a Jewish mother, etc.
- Middle views: not le-khatḥila but valid be-di'avad; or after first becoming a Noahide.

## 9. Ta'anit Esther

- Since the geonim, fast **13 Adar**, recalling Esther's fast and the Jews' fast on the day of battle.
- Haman's decree couldn't be cancelled; a second letter let the Jews fight back. They fasted and repented.
- Based on **custom**, so a bit more lenient than other fasts; in practice almost the same.
- Minḥa: **no Taḥanun** (and no Avinu Malkeinu for Ashkenazim), since Purim is next day.
- 13 Adar on Shabbat → fast **Thursday**, regular Minḥa.

## 10. The half-shekel

- Charity in Adar in memory of the Temple half-shekel. Best **right before Minḥa of Ta'anit Esther** (charity + fast = atonement).
- Amount: a half-unit coin; or three (teruma ×3 → 3 half-shekels / $1.50); or ~10 g silver. More is better.
- Who: men 20+ (Rema), 13+, children, women. Common today: **one per family member**, even an unborn child.
- Not from **ma'aser** money; under pressure, give the minimum yourself and top up from ma'aser.

> Line to remember: Adar is the month when even the bad turns to good; we remember Amalek so we never make peace with evil.
