## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | Basic: 1 candle per house · Mehadrin: 1 per person · Mehadrin min ha-mehadrin: add one each night (Beit Hillel) |
| **Who lights** | Sephardim: head of household for all · Ashkenazim: everyone, separate menoras |
| **Berakhot** | Le-hadlik ner (shel) Ḥanuka + She-asa nisim; She-heḥeyanu the first night |
| **Fuel** | Any oil/wick that lasts **½ hour**; olive oil is best; electric bulbs don't count |
| **The mitzva** | The **lighting** itself, in its proper place |
| **No benefit** | Don't use the light; the shamash is for that, placed apart |
| **Order** | Add on the left, light the new candle first, move right |
| **Before** | No meal, work or sleep from ½ hour before lighting time |

## 1. The mitzva

- Eight days, recalling the victory and the oil. Rabbinic, yet "**ve-tzivanu**": the Torah empowers the Sages (Devarim 17:11).
- **Women are obligated**: they were part of the miracle. (A married woman usually goes by her husband's lighting.)
- All the laws aim at **publicizing the miracle** (door/window, after sunset), but even someone alone must light.
- A poor person must beg or sell clothes for one candle a night.

## 2. Number of candles

- Three levels: one candle per **household**; one per **person** (mehadrin); a different number each night (**mehadrin min ha-mehadrin**). All Israel does the third.
- Beit Shammai: 8 down to 1 (days left). **Beit Hillel: 1 up to 8** (days of the miracle; "ascend in holiness"). We follow Beit Hillel: 36 candles total.
- A **shamash** is added and set apart, so the count stays clear.

## 3. The Sephardic custom

- One person (the head of household) lights **for everyone**, the number of the night, so passersby can count the days.
- Children may light their own, in separate menoras. Most Sephardim: **no berakha**; R. Mordechai Eliyahu allowed it under bar mitzva.

## 4. The Ashkenazic custom

- **Everyone lights** the night's number, menoras kept apart.
- Children from about **6** light with a berakha.
- Married women usually don't ("ishto ke-gufo") but may, with a berakha. Good to encourage girls to light, at least until bat mitzva.

## 5. The berakhot and Ha-nerot Halalu

- (1) **Le-hadlik ner (shel) Ḥanuka**; (2) **She-asa nisim**; first night also (3) **She-heḥeyanu**.
- Don't talk between berakhot and lighting. Say **Ha-nerot Halalu** after the first candle, while lighting the rest (or afterward).
- Let each flame catch most of the wick before moving on.

## 6. Family participation

- Gather everyone to hear the berakhot and answer amen, especially those who rely on another's lighting.
- Didn't hear? Rambam/Rashi: say She-asa nisim on seeing candles; Rashba/Ran: not. **Don't say it**; try to hear someone else's lighting.

## 7. The candles

- **Any oil or wick** that lasts ½ hour (no use of the light → no Shabbat concerns).
- Wax/paraffin give a nice flame; many say **olive oil is best** (clear light, recalls the miracle).
- Must hold **½ hour** of fuel; a few-minute candle → light without a berakha.

## 8. Electric bulbs

- Most poskim: **not valid** (no wick or fuel; maybe a "torch"; not what the Sages enacted, R. Kook).
- Unlike Shabbat candles (light is the point), Ḥanuka candles must resemble the Temple's.
- No candle at all? Light a bulb **without a berakha**. Big public electric menoras have value as reminders.

## 9. Lighting is the mitzva

- The **act of lighting** fulfills it. Went out early by accident → no need to relight (proper to relight, no berakha).
- Lit in a windy spot and it blew out → not fit from the start; **relight without a berakha**.
- A candle already burning → can't just lift and set it down; **extinguish and relight** for the mitzva.
- Light **in its place**: don't light at a sick person's bed and move it. (Sliding it closer to the window afterward is fine.)

## 10. No benefit from the light

- Not for mundane or holy use (counting money, learning), like the Temple Menora, and so it's clearly for the miracle.
- Leftover oil and wicks: use on later nights; after Ḥanuka, **burn** them (or down the drain/trash). Unused oil in the bottle is fine.
- Shamash went out? Don't relight it from a Ḥanuka candle, unless there's no other way to finish.
- After ½ hour, technically allowed; custom: still don't use them.

## 11. The menora and the shamash

- Beautify with a nice menora; candles stuck on a surface or glass cups work. No sooty, disgusting vessels.
- **Space between** candles: each visible, flames mustn't merge, wax mustn't melt.
- Not level or in a circle is valid; ideally a **straight line**.
- **Shamash**: so any use is of its light. Place it **higher** or apart.

## 12. Setting up and lighting

- Night 1: candle on the **right**. Each night add one to the **left**.
- Light the **newest (leftmost) first**, then move right: the new one shows the growing miracle, and turns go to the right.
- Stand on the menora's **left** so the first candle is the nearest.

## 13. Before lighting

- From **½ hour before** lighting time: no meal (snack < keveitza of bread/mezonot; fruit OK), no alcohol, no long work, no sleep, unless someone will remind you.
- Started earlier? Still stop and light at tzeit.
- Stuck at work: may keep working; don't eat until lighting.
- Don't start learning then; a fixed shiur may go on, then remind everyone to light.

## 14. Lighting in the synagogue

- Custom, with all the berakhot, to publicize more; near the **southern wall** like the Temple Menora. Doesn't exempt anyone from home.
- **Between Minḥa and Ma'ariv** (most people present). Late Ma'ariv → before it.
- Friday: before Minḥa (if Minḥa is near sunset). Motza'ei Shabbat: after Ma'ariv.
- Needs **ten** present; if not yet, some say berakha anyway, some say without.

## 15. Public gatherings

- Weddings, parties, lectures: may you say a berakha?
- Many: **no**, the custom is only for synagogues (a mini-Temple).
- Others: yes, wherever the public gathers; best if Minḥa/Ma'ariv is said there.
- May rely on the lenient view, especially when non-observant Jews are present; nice to let one of them light.

> Line to remember: we light to publicize the miracle, one more each night, and never use the light.
