## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Step | What | When / how |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Hefsek tahara** | A careful internal bedika with a soft white cloth, after cleaning out residual blood | Before sunset (best within ~2½ hours of it); if not clean, clean again and re-check before sunset |
| *(optional)* **Mokh daḥuk** | A cloth left in place from before sunset until after tzeit (~½ hour) | A hiddur, not required |
| **2. Seven clean days** | 7 full days (each starts at night) with no blood | Bedikot: ideally 2 a day; at least 1; when needed only day 1 and day 7 |
| **3. Tevila** | Night after day 7 → same weekday evening a week later | See ch. 5 |

**When can the 7 days start? (because of semen that may leave in the 72 hours after intimacy)**

| | Shulḥan Arukh | Rema (Ashkenazim, many Sefaradim) |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | After 3 full days (in practice, end of the **4th day**) from intimacy; or right away if she cleans out the canal | Never before **5 days** from the start of bleeding (day 1 = first day of bleeding) |
| No intimacy before | Start as soon as the hefsek tahara is done | Still 5 days |

## 1. Hefsek tahara

- Three stages: (1) **hefsek tahara**, (2) **seven clean days**, (3) **tevila**.
- When she thinks the bleeding has stopped, she does a careful check. Clean → after **sunset** she starts day 1. Without a hefsek tahara she's still "seeing blood," even if it actually stopped.
- **Best time:** close to sunset; anything within **~2½ hours** before sunset counts as the best time. After sunset it's already the next day → she can only start a day later.
- Worried she'll forget or won't be able to check then → better to do it **morning or midday** than risk losing a day.

## 2. Preparing

- Use a **soft white cloth** so any blood shows (SA YD 196:6). Not white tissue (it crumbles) unless there's no choice. *Edei bedika* are sold for this (already in the Talmud, Nidda 17a).
- First **clean residual blood** from the canal with a damp cloth: bleeding may have stopped but leftover blood would spoil the check.
- Worried a dry cloth will hurt → slightly dampen it (not wet, which could dissolve a drop). Or apply oil some time **before**, so it absorbs and doesn't mask blood.
- Good to clean the outer area (wipe or water) and check fingers for lipstick etc.

## 3. How to check

- Wrap the finger in the cloth, insert as deep as is comfortable, and gently press it to **all sides** ("*ḥorim u-sedakim*") so blood in the folds shows.
- **Blood** on it → not valid. **No tamei color** (2:3), even with other-colored discharge → valid; start counting.
- Blood or a doubt → clean again with a damp cloth and **recheck before sunset**. So do the hefsek with time to spare.
- Can't insert (a virgin) → check up to that point (8:3). A wound that may bleed when touched → check up to the wound. What matters is checking as far as she can.

## 4. Mokh daḥuk

- A hiddur: after the hefsek, a cloth stays in place from **before sunset** until **after tzeit** (bein ha-shmashot), ideally close to **half an hour**, to know beyond doubt it stopped.
- Agreed: a **hiddur, not required**.

## 5. Semen after intimacy (Shulḥan Arukh's view)

- Before Sinai: "*al tigshu el isha*" for 3 days (Shemot 19:15): semen that can still conceive leaves the body for 3 days, and losing it carries tum'a (Shabbat 86b).
- So: if they were together and then she saw blood, she **can't start** the 7 clean days while viable semen might still leave; the 7 days must be clean of that too. After **72 hours** it's no longer viable.
- **Shulḥan Arukh** (196:11, 13): after 3 full days. In practice those spread over **4 calendar days**: intimacy on Sunday (Motza'ei Shabbat or Sunday afternoon) → 72 hours end late Wednesday → 7 days start **Wednesday night** (Thursday). Even if bleeding lasted an hour.
    - Want to start earlier → **clean the canal** (cloth, or a bath in warm water), then hefsek tahara and start without waiting.
    - Most Rishonim; practiced in some Sefardi and Mizraḥi communities.

## 6. The Rema's view and the stringent customs

- **Rema:** always wait **5 days** from the start of bleeding (the first day of bleeding is day 1).
    - Bein ha-shmashot doubts about when intimacy happened (Sunday or Monday night) could make people start a day early.
    - Didn't want to rely on cleaning the canal (maybe not thorough).
    - Didn't want to count from intimacy (people forget if and when).
    - To keep it simple: **5 days even if there was no intimacy**.
- Some communities waited **6 or 7** days (since many periods last longer and some women didn't do the hefsek properly). Most poskim rejected that as excessive.
- **Standard strict practice = Rema**: all Ashkenazim and many Sefardi/Mizraḥi communities.
- Usually no real cost (most can't do a hefsek before day 5 anyway). It's a stringency for **short periods**, and especially after a **stain** or brief non-period bleeding.

## 7. Can the custom be changed?

- It's not just wanting a leniency: ona is a Torah mitzva, and extra days hurt it and can lead to sin. The Rema's rule fit a time with fewer means of cleaning and less-educated women who might miscount. Now we're gathered in Israel; maybe a community custom doesn't bind individuals the same way.
- **In practice:** without broad rabbinic agreement, the custom isn't changed; education in mitzvot rests on keeping traditions.
- But **where it isn't a clear change**, a couple may adopt the Shulḥan Arukh:
    - **Sefardi/Mizraḥi** families who followed the Rema here (they follow the SA in most things anyway).
    - **A Sefardi/Mizraḥi bride and an Ashkenazi groom** (in women's laws she may follow her mother's custom, especially here where the main halakha is like the SA).
    - **Both Ashkenazi** and really want to → consult their rav.

## 8. The seven clean days and their bedikot

- **7 full days** without blood, then tevila. Each day starts at night → she immerses on the **same weekday evening** a week later (started Motza'ei Shabbat → tevila next Motza'ei Shabbat).
- **Bedikot** (like the hefsek: soft white cloth, deep, all sides):
    - Le-khatchila **twice a day** (morning and before sunset).
    - Hard → **once a day**.
    - Blood found → the count is broken (the days must be consecutive): new hefsek before evening, restart that night (SA 196:10).
    - When needed (daily checks are hard) → **day 1 and day 7** only (SA 196:4).
    - Prone to **stains** → le-khatchila day 1 and day 7 only (with a wound, check up to it).
    - **Sha'at ha-dechak:** only one bedika in the 7 days besides the hefsek → may still immerse.

## 9. White underwear

- After the hefsek, put on **clean** underwear without stains; if she sleeps without, check that the sheet and blanket are clean (SA 196:3).
- **Proper custom:** white underwear in the 7 days (Rema). Can be fulfilled with a **white panty liner**: it can't become tamei, so stains on it don't break the count.
- Prone to stains (wounds, IUD, hormonal pills) → **colored or black** underwear from the start (R. Akiva Eger; Maharsham).

## 10. Keeping the count in mind

- Le-khatchila she shouldn't **stop thinking** about the count. Saw something that looks tamei → don't decide to restart yet; a ḥakham may rule it tahor and the count continues. Husband traveling for a long time → keep counting; he might come back early.
- Be-di'avad: decided mid-count to stop, then changed her mind → if she saw no blood, she continues and immerses.
- **No verbal counting** (not like Sefirat Ha-omer); she just needs to know she's in the 7 clean days.
