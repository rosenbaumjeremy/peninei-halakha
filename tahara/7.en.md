## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | In short |
|---|---|
| **Who decides** | She does: she tells her husband when she's tamei; doubts → ask a ḥakham (she or her husband) |
| **Don't be needlessly strict** | Any chance it's tahor (color, size, wound) → **ask**, don't just assume tamei; it cancels ona |
| **Whom to ask** | A **fixed rav** ("*aseh lekha rav*"); women may prefer the rabbanit; husband can bring it; when needed, she asks the rav directly |
| **Preparing a question** | Let the cloth dry, keep it in an envelope, write the date; give all details (pregnancy, nursing, IUD, pills, which day, sensation, wounds) |
| **Light** | Daylight best; good daylight-like bulbs ok; yellow light makes things look redder, white fluorescent less red; red/brown surroundings make it look redder |
| **Two rabbis** | One forbade → don't ask another, unless you tell him (when really needed); "an opinion" or "I can't permit" → may ask another |
| **Medical exams** | Without anesthesia → tahor. Bleeding not at her ona → wound blood, tahor. At her ona → tahor only if sure it's from the exam. A D&C → tamei |

## 1. Her responsibility; asking a ḥakham

- **She** is responsible to tell her husband when she becomes tamei, and to ask a ḥakham about any doubtful sight (herself or via him). Then a mitzva to become tahor quickly: "*ve-safra lah*" → she counts **for herself** (Ketubot 72a).
- Since all blood colors are forbidden, the question is simpler: does the discharge contain **blood** or not? Learn your own period color, wet and dry.
- **Young couples:** ask about every doubt. After a while of asking and seeing, she'll recognize most cases herself and ask only real doubts. Some women find it harder (less sharp eye, many borderline sights) → keep asking.
- **Don't be needlessly strict.** Any chance it's tahor (color, size, a wound) → ask; strictness cancels ona, a great Torah mitzva.

## 2. The ḥakham and how to ask

- Rabbis learn by seeing hundreds and thousands of questions with experienced rabbis.
- **Have a fixed rav** ("*aseh lekha rav ve-histalek min ha-safek*", Avot 1:16): you learn to think systematically; he knows the background, can spot a medical issue and refer her. If your rav isn't expert or too busy, choose another fixed one for tahara.
- **The cloth:** let it dry, keep it in an **envelope**, write the **date** on each. *Edei bedika* aren't muktzeh on Shabbat.
- **Give the details:** pregnant, nursing, IUD, hormonal pills; internal bedika near her ona or in the 7 clean days (which day); a stain with or without sensation; a wound or possible wound (pain at the bedika).
- For generations women brought questions to rabbis without embarrassment. Today some feel uncomfortable:
    - Better: **the husband** brings it to the rav; or where the **rabbanit** helps, the wife brings it to her (she passes doubts to the rav). Often other questions get answered along the way.
    - If neither works, she asks the rav; he acts as a professional. Like overcoming embarrassment to see a doctor.
    - Some rabbis have a box by their home (with a phone number); some prefer phone lines of institutes. Still, a fixed rav who knows the family is best, especially on family guidance.

## 3. Lighting

- In the past: daylight (balanced colors), with the sample in **shade** (direct sun's glare distorts). Simple questions by candle when needed; never by moon/starlight. Even after forbidding all reds: day le-khatchila; candle only when needed; doubts waited for morning.
- **Electric light** made night answers possible, but borderline cases wait for day:
    - **White fluorescent** → tamei can look tahor.
    - **Incandescent** (yellow-red) → tahor can look tamei.
    - The rav must know this and compensate, or wait.
- Today **daylight-balanced** bulbs allow answering almost everything.
- **Surroundings**: brown/red walls, curtains, furniture, books make samples look redder; white surroundings matter less.
- **She** should know this too: yellow light or a red/brown towel nearby → looks redder; white light → less red. Slight doubt → wait for morning or look under balanced light.

## 4. Changes in appearance

- May ask about a sample **wet or dry**. Rarely it changes on drying; we rule by what we see: "*the judge has only what his eyes see*" (Nidda 20b). Women decide by the wet look; doubtful ones needn't be rushed in wet.
- **Tahor when wet, then dried** with the faded color gathering at the edges so the edges look tamei → **tahor**: it was tahor when it left; mixed back it would be (Arukh Ha-shulḥan 188:15).

## 5. Asking two rabbis

- "*A ḥakham declared it tamei; his colleague may not declare it tahor*" (Nidda 20b). Don't go shopping for a heter (Avoda Zara 7a):
    1. The ruling has already made it forbidden.
    2. Respect for the first rav.
- **When needed** (really seeking a heter, or a talmid ḥakham wanting to clarify) → may ask another **if he tells him** the first forbade. If the second persuades the first, it can be permitted.
- Only **the same question**; a new question → may ask a lenient rav (Ritva; Radbaz; Rema YD 242:31).
- Asked for **an opinion, not a ruling** → may ask another. Some rabbis answer "**I can't permit**" (not "forbidden") so one may ask elsewhere.

## 6. Choosing a doctor

- A mitzva to look after health, especially a woman expecting a baby. Gynecology now has routine checks (before, during, after pregnancy; after menopause; fertility).
- Do the **recommended** checks, without excess worry or unnecessary tests. In Israel: what the **kupot ḥolim** fund is the standard; extras usually aren't needed. Those with **supplemental insurance** chose a stricter line, so what it funds counts as routine for them.
- **Male doctor** → permitted (professional, medical touch; his livelihood depends on propriety). Not alone in a **locked** room, or it should be known that staff may knock. Where no one else is around, she comes with her husband, sister or mother.
- Prefer a **God-fearing** doctor (halakhic sensitivity: e.g., postpartum contraception that causes tamei bleeding; fertility treatment; abortion questions). When needed any doctor, and consult a rav on questions.
- Routine care in Israel: prefer a doctor **within the kupa** (no need to pay; private care may make people skip needed tests).
- Prefer a **fixed doctor**: problems are solved by trial and error; switching delays solutions. Specialists in coordination with the regular doctor.

## 7. After medical exams and procedures

- Early on, poskim worried exams might open the uterus and cause bleeding; women were told to ask the doctor and then a rav.
- Now it's clear: **any exam or procedure without anesthesia** doesn't open the uterus enough to make her tamei → **tahor**, no question needed.
- **Bleeding** after an exam **not at her ona** → wound blood → tahor. **At her ona** → tahor only if she's sure (by sensation or the doctor) it's from the exam; in doubt → tamei.
- **Inside the uterus** (IUD in/out, removing growths) → wound blood → tahor. At her ona → may be mixed with period blood → tamei unless sure it isn't (2:13–14).
- **The one procedure that surely makes her tamei: a D&C around a miscarriage** (removing the lining that held the pregnancy, like a period).
