## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Area | During the days of issur |
|---|---|
| **Touch** | ✗ any touch, even with a pinky, even through clothing |
| **Talk** | Friendly and warm ✓; talk that arouses ✗ |
| **Looking** | ✓ at her and her clothes; ✗ at what's normally covered; she covers it |
| **Singing** | ✓ (main view) |
| **Sleeping** | Separate beds that don't touch (ideally ~½ m apart); ✗ his/her bed, blanket, pillow |
| **Meals alone together** | A reminder on the table (an unused object, separate placemats, a changed seat); ✗ same serving bowl; ✗ her leftovers knowingly |
| **Drinks** | ✗ pour/serve wine the usual way; ✓ with a change (put it a bit farther) or when serving everyone |
| **Passing things** | ✗ hand to hand or carrying together; put it down; when needed (embarrassment, a stroller up the bus step) lenient |
| **Illness** | Someone else helps if possible; if not, the spouse may help, with gloves (more caution when she's the patient) |

> **The middle path:** don't stir up desire, but don't push each other away either. "*If she becomes repulsive to him, he'll divorce her*" (R. Akiva).

## 1. What's forbidden

- From when she sees blood until tevila: no intimacy ("*ve-el isha be-nidat tum'atah lo tikrav*", Vayikra 18:19; karet, 20:18).
- **Any touch of desire** (hug, kiss, even a pinky) → most say **d'oraita** (Rambam, Ra'avad, Ḥinukh). The Sages added: even touch **without** desire, and even through clothing. (Ramban: only intimacy itself is d'oraita, other touch d'rabbanan.)
- More fences (this chapter) help a couple master their desire; their love is refined and they rejoice more in the days of tahara.
- A priest asked Rav Kahana: can fire be near flax and not catch? He answered: "*sugah ba-shoshanim*" (Shir 7:3): even a fence of **roses** won't be breached by Israel (Sanhedrin 37a). Gentle fences, kept without police.
- Fences are the Sages' role ("*u-shmartem et mishmarti*"), and we're commanded to follow them ("*lo tasur*", Devarim 17:11).

## 2. How to behave in these days

- Avoid talk about intimacy or that arouses it: ARN 2 reads "*lo tikrav*" as not even "*idle talk*" with her. In days of tahara such talk is part of ona; now it's "idle."
- Each couple knows what arouses them; avoid long conversations of that kind. Many read "*al tarbeh siḥa im ha-isha*… *be-ishto amru*" (Avot 1:5) this way.
- Avoid a **couple's vacation** in these days.
- **Makeup and jewelry:** the early elders said no (*nidda* = distancing). **R. Akiva:** then she'll become unattractive to her husband and he'll divorce her; rather, she stays nidda **until she immerses** (Shabbat 64b). → She may dress up and wear makeup as usual (SA YD 195:9).
- **The middle path:** don't arouse desire; don't overdo distance. If they become repelled or too distant, the love won't be renewed after tevila.
    - Speak kindly, give compliments, but don't linger on arousing talk.
    - In practice it's tricky: warmth can arouse; coldness can hurt. Ideas: use these days for draining tasks (cleaning, maintenance, work or study deadlines), family visits (Shabbat at parents).
    - Often the **husband** wants less closeness (afraid desire will grow), and the **wife** wants signs of affection (to feel loved). Each couple finds its middle path: halakha marks the limits on both sides, closeness and hurt.

## 3. Looking and singing

- He may **look** at her and her clothes and enjoy her beauty, but not let it lead to thoughts of desire (SA YD 195:7; EH 21:4).
- He shouldn't look at **normally covered** parts; she covers them.
    - Lenient: only what she usually covers **at home** in days of tahara.
    - Strict: what she covers **outside**, including her hair.
    - **Halakha:** lenient (it's d'rabbanan). Since tzniut strengthens love, le-khatchila good to be strict. Some cover their hair even for sleep (not worrying if it slips).
- **Singing:** if she usually sings, she may, including zemirot. Some forbid (as before strangers); the **main view is lenient**.

## 4. Sleeping

- ✗ Same bed, even clothed and not touching. ✗ Two beds that touch. ✓ Beds with some gap; ideally **~½ m**, so they won't touch if they stretch out in sleep (SA/Rema YD 195:6).
- ✗ He lies in **her** bed or she in **his**; ✗ the other's own blanket or pillow. A bed/blanket/pillow that isn't specifically either's → ok.
- ✗ Making the other's bed **in front of them** for sleep (a personal gesture). Not in front of them → ok (Ketubot 61a; SA 195:11). Making the bed as housework, not for bedtime → ok even in front (Ritva).

## 5. Eating together

- Meals bring hearts close, so the Sages set reminders:
    - **Alone at one table** → something to remind them: an object on the table they won't use (both know why), **separate placemats** if they usually share, a special dish, or one of them changes **seat** (SA YD 195:3).
    - **Another person** eating with them, even family, or in a **restaurant** → no reminder needed (people are restrained in company).
- ✗ **Same serving bowl**: soup, stew; with a bowl of ḥummus, each takes some onto their own plate, no dipping in the common bowl. ✓ Each spreads from the same jar onto their own slice. A bowl of almonds → each takes some in front of them. If a third person eats from it too → ok.
- ✗ He eats/drinks her **leftovers** (a sign of closeness). Some Rishonim permit; practice strict, lenient when needed. ✓ If moved to another dish; ✓ if she's not there; ✓ if he doesn't know they're hers (she needn't tell him) (SA/Rema 195:3–4).

## 6. Pouring drinks; signs of affection

- ✗ Serve each other **wine/liquor** the usual way. ✓ With a **change**: place the cup a bit farther away. One who mixes a special drink shouldn't make it for the other in front of them (SA 195:10, 13).
- **Serving a group** → may place it before the spouse like everyone else.
- **Kiddush:** alone → set her cup a bit farther than usual; with others → like everyone.
- ✗ Sit together on a **swing** like lovers; ✓ a bench even if it rocks (Beit Yosef). Some say not on one sofa where one's movement moves the other; with their child between them → ok (Rema 195:5).
- ✓ Same seat in a **bus or car** without touching even through clothes.

## 7. Passing things

- ✗ Handing something hand to hand (may touch; and it's a closeness). ✗ Carrying something big together (SA 195:2). Le-khatchila ✗ tossing either (Rema). → Put it down on a table or bag; the other picks it up.
- Others present and it'd be embarrassing → postpone it.
- **When needed** → lenient:
    - Lifting a stroller onto a bus or upstairs together, carrying a bed, when there's no natural way to get help.
    - To prevent **embarrassment** (a key that can't naturally be put down) → hand to hand, carefully not touching. The meticulous plan ahead.
- **Babies:** ✗ pass a small baby between them. An older baby who reaches from one to the other → may help him across without touching. When needed, even a small baby (with no easy place to put him down) → carefully. Better not to kiss the baby while the other holds him.

## 8. Illness

- **He's sick** and needs help getting up or dressing: someone else, if available, does anything that needs touch, even through clothes. A professional nurse is better than his wife (service vs. affection).
    - No one else, or it costs money → his wife may help (the touch is care, not desire). Preferably with **gloves**; washing him → definitely gloves.
- **She's sick** → stricter (he's healthy and might be overcome). Hire a caregiver if they can afford it. If hard to find or pay for → in a great need he may care for her, with gloves, both careful that it's kindness and not desire.
- These are for non-dangerous illness where the spouse is bedridden and really needs help. **Danger to life** → do everything; asking a rav first is bloodshed (Yerushalmi Yoma 8:5).
- **Badly injured** and helped by a caress of encouragement, or **terminally ill** in the last days or hours → the healthy spouse may stroke them to encourage and strengthen, like the love between a mother and son or brother and sister, without desire. Not in normal times, but in danger or a hard parting, lenient when needed.
