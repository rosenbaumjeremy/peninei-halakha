## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| What was found | Where / how | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood with a hargasha** (she clearly knows her period started) | Anything, even a tiny drop | **Tamei** (Torah) |
| **Internal bedika** (deep in the vaginal canal) or a **tampon** | Even a mustard seed, even on colored cloth | **Tamei** |
| **Ketem** (stain, no sensation) | > 2 cm, on white, on something that can become tamei, surely from the uterus | Tamei (d'rabbanan) |
| | ≤ 2 cm, or several small stains, or on **colored** underwear | Tahor |
| | On **toilet paper, a pad, a panty liner, the toilet or bath** | Tahor |
| | Could have come from somewhere else (wound, hemorrhoids, beets, lipstick, chicken blood on her hands…) | Tahor |
| **Wiping after the bathroom** | Toilet paper, colored cloth, or white cloth with ≤ 2 cm | Tahor |
| **Wound** (vaginal or uterine; IUD insertion, procedure) | She believes it's from the wound | Tahor |

**Colors:** a reddish, blood-like tint (burgundy/purple of blood) → tamei. Yellow, gray, clear brown without blood-red → tahor. Unsure → ask a rav.

> **Rule for stains:** "*The Sages said this only to be lenient*" (R. Akiva, Nidda 58b). When in doubt about a ketem, be lenient.

## 1. Tamei and tahor blood

- Menstrual blood makes her tamei and forbids the couple (Vayikra 15:19). It's blood from the **uterus**, the "*mekor dameha*" (Vayikra 20:18).
- The womb that creates life is also where tamei blood leaves. Ona in the days of tahara and separation in the days of nidda strengthen the bond toward life.
- **Torah colors** (Nidda 19a–20a): four reds are tamei:
    1. **Like blood from a fresh wound**;
    2. **Like saffron** (red-orange);
    3. **Like muddy soil** of the Galilee valleys (red-brown);
    4. **Like diluted wine** (Sharon wine with two parts water; red-purple);
    5. plus **black like ink** (the first color, darkened).
- By Torah law, all other shades of red, black, pink, orange and brown are tahor.

## 2. Hard to tell apart

- Distinguishing needs real expertise; doubtful cases go to the Sages, rarely even the Great Sanhedrin ("*ki yipalei… bein dam le-dam*", Devarim 17:8). Blood was not examined at night (Megilla 14a).
- The definitions are subtle: like a bull's blood at the moment of shechita, not after; like a head louse, not a body louse; a young man's wound, not an older man's. Black like ink is tamei; black like a raven or pitch, tahor (Nidda 20a).
- After the Ḥurban the tradition weakened. Ameimar, Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi at bloodletting: Ameimar distinguished the first bowl from the second; Rav Ashi said he couldn't tell (Nidda 20a).
- Over time poskim stopped distinguishing; by the Rishonim, **all reds and blacks** were treated as tamei.

## 3. Between the colors

- Even so, ruling still needs training: what has the **redness of blood** and what doesn't, especially when a stain dries and fades.
- Naming colors (red, pink-red, burgundy, black forbidden; yellow, gray, brown permitted) is what many poskim wrote, but it's not enough: there are endless in-between shades. Wet blood tends to burgundy; dry, toward purple or brown.
- **Closest working rule:** an in-between color with the **blood-red tint** (burgundy or purple of blood) → tamei; without it → tahor.
- In practice one learns from seeing one's own period blood, wet and dry. Clearly blood-red → tamei; clearly not → tahor; **in doubt → ask a ḥakham**.
- Some women's bleeding tapers: reddish-brown (tamei) gradually becoming plain brown (tahor).

## 4. Blood vs. ketem

- **Torah:** tamei only if it left **with a hargasha**, like a period ("*bi-vsarah*" → until she feels it, Nidda 57b), for both nidda and zava (Rambam, Issurei Bi'a 9:1).
    - Reason: tum'a comes from blood that leaves in a process of lost vitality (an egg lost, or a problem in the cycle); by nature such blood comes with sensation.
- **The Sages' decree on stains** (no sensation): tamei only if (1) **more than 2 cm** across, (2) on **white**, (3) on something that **can become tamei**.
    - Why: in Temple times that blood itself made taharot tamei, so the Sages made it forbid her too, under those conditions.
- **Any doubt about stains → lenient.** R. Akiva: "The Sages said this only to be lenient" (Nidda 58b).

## 5. What is a hargasha?

- With a hargasha, even a tiny drop is tamei; without, only a qualifying ketem.
- The Talmud and Rishonim treated the sensation as obvious. Later poskim offered three explanations:
    1. A sense of the **uterus opening** or a **shudder** (from close reading). Problem: many women don't feel that; it can't be that nidda is cancelled for them, or that nature changed so much.
    2. The feeling of **blood flowing** from the vagina.
    3. General **bodily signs** before or with the period. Problem: the Mishna describes women who felt the very **moment** it began, even mid-intimacy (Shevuot 14b), which 2 and 3 don't capture.

## 6. The working definition

- Per most views: not one specific feeling but a **cluster** that differs from woman to woman. **The rule: she knows clearly, from her body, that menstrual blood is coming out.**
- Like hunger or fatigue: hard to define, everyone knows it. Hormonal changes show up as headaches, cramps, heaviness, irritability, low mood, a shiver, the uterus opening, the flow.
    - Some notice the fine moment the lining starts to shed; some feel a period is coming and then know the wetness is it; some only become sure mid-period when the flow is heavy.
- In the past more women felt the onset (the period affected their lives more and they were alert to it). Even then some didn't, so the Sages had a woman who saw blood worry about taharot from the previous 24 hours. But no one suggested such women weren't tamei: everyone feels it at least mid-period.
- **Bottom line:** whenever she clearly knows from her body that she's menstruating → tamei d'oraita, whatever the combination of feelings. If she doesn't know she's bleeding from a period, what she sees is a **ketem**.

## 7. Size of a forbidden stain

- **Gris** = half a large bean ≈ a **circle 2 cm** across. Up to that it might have been from a louse; bigger, it's from the body.
- Today there are no such lice, but a stain ≤ a gris still **isn't** tamei: by Torah it's tahor; the decree covered only > gris. Many explain the decree was mainly for taharot, extended to the couple; we don't extend it further.
- **Several small stains** that add up to more than a gris → don't combine; tahor.
- A **thick** stain that would be bigger if spread → it didn't spread → tahor.

## 8. Stains on colored clothing

- On something **colored**, even a light color → **tahor**. The decree applied only where tamei and tahor blood could be told apart.
- White with colored patterns/stripes: only the part on the **white** counts; tamei only if that part is > a gris.
- **Practical:** wear **colored underwear** in the days of tahara (Rambam; Rema 190:10).
- In the 7 clean days the custom is **white** (Rema 196:3). But a woman who expects stains (wounds, IUD, hormonal pills) should wear **colored** then too; white isn't required (4:9).

## 9. Stains on things that can't become tamei

- On the **toilet seat, bathtub** → tahor, even if surely from the uterus. The whole decree is tied to the stain making its surface tamei.
- **Panty liners, pads, cotton, toilet paper** → can't become tamei (material, and/or disposable) → tahor.
- **Very large stains** on colored cloth or such surfaces:
    - Some are strict on a very large one (e.g., 7 × 7 cm): maybe it came with a sensation she didn't notice.
    - Some are lenient: without the sensation of a period, it's a ketem however big.
    - **Practice:** many are strict, especially in the 7 clean days. Those who want to be lenient may, as long as it's clear it wasn't her period.

## 10. Doubt whether it came from the uterus

- A > gris stain on white is tamei only if **known to be from the uterus** (on underwear; or on a white sheet/nightgown and clearly from there).
- **Any real possibility** it came from elsewhere, even if unlikely → **tahor** ("*dam ve-lo ketem*", Nidda 58b):
    - A wound on her body, even scabbed, if it might have reopened;
    - Hemorrhoids, urinary infection, upset stomach, eating beets;
    - She touched chicken or fish blood, beets, lipstick, a red marker, and then her underwear.

## 11. Internal bedika and wiping

- **Internal bedika** deep in the canal: even a mustard seed of blood, even on colored cloth → **tamei** (near the cervix; maybe it came with sensation now or earlier, so the ketem leniencies don't apply). A **tampon** = internal bedika.
- **Wiping after using the bathroom** and finding blood on the paper:
    - Some are strict (surely from the canal; urination might have masked the sensation) → better not to look at the first wipes.
    - **Practice:** it was caught **outside** and without sensation → a ketem → on toilet paper → **tahor**. Same with colored cloth, or white cloth ≤ a gris.

## 12. Unsure whether her period began

- She **clearly felt** her period starting → they separate, and she checks **externally** with toilet paper (no bedika cloth needed). Any blood → tamei; none → tahor.
- **A feeling that usually, but not always, means her period** → check externally; meanwhile no intimacy.
    - Blood enough to know it began → tamei. None → tahor. A little → it's a ketem (external), tahor; but if it raises a worry the period is coming, refrain until the end of that *ona* (day → sunset; night → sunrise). No period by then → tahor.
- A feeling that **usually isn't** followed by a period → ignore it; no check needed.
- Ovulation sensations resembling a period, then just a drop → it's not a period sensation → treat as a ketem.

## 13. Blood from a wound

- Only uterine blood from the cycle (or zava) is tamei. Blood from a **wound**, in the vagina or even the uterus, is not.
- **Three situations:**
    1. **She knows it's from the wound** → tahor. She's believed (Nidda 66a; SA YD 187:5–6): pain of a wound, feeling it with her finger, blood mixed with pus, always on the same side in bedikot (187:7). Even internal, even at her expected time, as long as she thinks it's the wound. Tamei only if she stops thinking so, feels a period sensation, or the amount shows it's a period.
    2. **Known wound, unsure if this blood is from it:** internal bedika **at her fixed time** → tamei; internal **not** at her fixed time, or a stain even at her time → tahor (Rema 187:5).
    3. **Unsure whether she has a wound:** stain on white, even > gris → tahor. Internal bedika → strict. Ask a rav (maybe it's recognizable). If it recurs, see a doctor/knowledgeable nurse: a wound found → like case 2; none → like everyone.

## 14. Uterine wounds

- Bleeding from a **wound in the uterus** isn't tamei.
    - **IUD inserted or removed** → wound blood → tahor; in the 7 clean days, keep counting.
    - Surgical removal of growths → tahor.
    - A medical exam that may wound the canal or cervix → tahor (7:7).
    - **Fibroids or polyps** a doctor says may bleed: blood **not** at her period time → tahor; at her period time, or she thinks it's her period → tamei.
- **IUD and stains after her period:** stains on underwear → tahor (IUDs cause wound bleeding; stains → lenient).
    - Internal bedika: some are strict (maybe an inflammatory shedding like a period). **Halakha:** after the period ended and she did a hefsek tahara, if it's not heavy like a period, attribute it to the IUD wound.
    - To avoid the doubt: with a bleeding-prone IUD, avoid **unnecessary internal bedikot** and white underwear (4, n. 10).
