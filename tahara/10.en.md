## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Gathered in the ground** | Spring, sea, or rain collected in something attached to the ground (even a pool on the 100th floor). Not in a **kli** (a plastic pool, a ship) unless holed (~8 cm) and fixed to the ground |
| **40 se'a** | Enough to immerse the whole body at once. Main view ~310 L (others 455 / 650); in practice ≥ 1,000 L |
| **Not *she'uvin*** | Water that was in a vessel. 3 log (~0.9 L) falling into a mikveh under 40 se'a disqualifies it all |
| **Not made by a person** | E.g., splashed in by hand |
| **Not through something that can become tamei** | E.g., an unattached metal pipe |
| **Not crawling** | A rain-mikveh must stand still; a spring/sea may flow |
| **Fixes** | *Hamshakha* (flowing ≥ 3 tefaḥim / ~23 cm over the ground); *zeri'a* (pouring into a kosher mikveh); *hashaka* (touching one through a ~5 cm hole); melted ice |
| **Today** | Tap water is treated as *she'uvin*; the immersion pool is made kosher by an *otzar zeri'a* and/or an *otzar hashaka* of rainwater |

## 1. Tahara through water

- The Torah requires holiness (Mikdash, korbanot, ḥalla, teruma, ma'aser sheni) to be handled by the tahor.
- Tum'a tracks death: a corpse is *avi avot ha-tum'a*; less severe, the loss of what could bring life (nidda, semen) or damage to that system (zav/zava).
- The process matches the severity: tum'at met → 7 days, para aduma on days 3 and 7, tevila; zav/zava → 7 clean days + tevila; semen or touching a carcass → tevila, tahor at nightfall. All end with **immersion in water**.
- **Why water:** it preceded life ("*ve-ru'aḥ Elokim meraḥefet al penei ha-mayim*"); day 3, "*yikavu ha-mayim… u-le-mikveh ha-mayim kara yamim*." One who moved away from the source of life returns to it and is re-created (1:11–12).
- After the Ḥurban, one tahara remains: from nidda, because it touches the bond of husband and wife, where the Shekhina rests (Sota 17a; 1:9–10).
- So the custom is to build mikva'ot with **extra care**, kosher even by minority views (Tashbetz; Da'at Kohen), but not if that makes building one too hard, and **no new stringencies** that cast doubt on past tevilot.
- Every community **must** build a mikveh (Rema ḤM 163:3), and a mitzva to make it **beautiful, clean and pleasant** (R. Akiva Eger).

## 2. Pure water in the ground

- Tevila is in pure water **gathered in the ground**: a spring or rainwater (Vayikra 11:36). In the ground it can't become tamei; water separated by a person or vessel can (Ḥatam Sofer YD 213).
- ✓ Spring, sea, a pit, a **pool built of blocks and concrete** in or on a building (even 100 floors up: attached as a building).
- ✗ Water in a **kli**, even huge (plastic pool, ship). To use a large tank: **hole it** (~8 cm) and **attach it to the ground** (concrete or buried); then seal the hole with anything (Rambam; SA YD 201:7).
- Rebar in the concrete is fine (attached to the ground, not a kli).
- Plus three rules (§7–8): not *she'uvin*; not formed by a person; not formed through something that can become tamei.

## 3. Forty se'a

- Enough to immerse the **whole body at once**: 1 × 1 × 3 amot (body height without the head ~3 amot; the water rises and one bends) = **40 se'a** (Ḥagiga 11a).
- A small person can't use less: it must suit people in general. Even slightly less → **invalid** (Ketubot 104a). Exactly 40 se'a → after one tevila the drops leave with him and it's invalid (SA 201:62).
- Modern measures: main view **~310 L**; others ≥455 L (R. Ḥaim Na'eh); ~650 L (Ḥazon Ish). **Practice:** at least **1,000 L** (certainty and comfort); a small one as far above the minimum as possible.
- Shape doesn't matter as long as one can immerse fully at once; le-khatchila comfortable (SA 201:1, 61).
- **Only water**: not wine, oil, milk; can't top up with them (201:24).

## 4. Spring vs. mikveh

- Two kinds (Vayikra 11:36): a **spring** (groundwater) and a **pit of rainwater** ("*mikveh*").
- **Spring** → kosher even **flowing** (that's its nature). **Rain mikveh** → only standing (*ashboren*): rain is transient until it gathers (Mikva'ot 1:7–8; SA 201:2).
- **Red wine** fell in and changed the color: a spring stays kosher (*mayim ḥayim*); a mikveh is invalid, but having 40 kosher se'a, add more she'uvin until it looks like water. Color changed by age or washing vessels → still kosher (201:25–28).
- A spring also needs 40 se'a; when flowing, all its water from source to end counts (201:1).

## 5. Sea and river

- **All seas**, large or small → kosher for people and vessels, even with waves or inflowing rivers (nature, like a spring) (Rashi; Tosafot Shabbat 109a).
- **River from a spring** → like a spring, kosher while flowing.
- **Seasonal stream** (from rain, then stops) → like a mikveh, kosher only when standing.
- **Spring-fed river swollen by rain or snowmelt** → follow the majority: mostly spring → kosher flowing; mostly rain/snow → like a mikveh, invalid while flowing. Fix: enclose the spot with a mat so the flow isn't felt inside (SA 201:2).

## 6. A mikveh invalidated by crawling (*zeḥila*)

- A rain mikveh whose water flows out is invalid; so prevent leaks.
- **Crack**: if, after draining to the crack's height, 40 se'a would remain → kosher even while leaking. If not → invalid (even though it now has 40) until sealed with 40 remaining.
- Seal with something that **can't become tamei** (sealant, a plastic or wooden plug without a receptacle), not metal or cloth (201:50).
- Only **noticeable** flow invalidates; slight seepage → kosher if 40 se'a remain (201:51). Gra is strict even then; le-khatchila consider him. A tiny drop in level noticed only after many hours → all agree it's fine.

## 7. She'uvin

- Water that was **in vessels** → invalid, even rain that fell into vessels left out to catch it.
- Pipes from springs/wells → fine, unless a pipe has a **receptacle** (then it's a kli). Since municipal water usually passes through receptacles, **tap water is treated as she'uvin** (§12).
- **3 log** (~900 ml) of she'uvin entering rainwater **under 40 se'a** → invalidates it all, even unintentionally, even if 40 kosher se'a are added later. Only fix: drain and wait for rain.
- **Already 40 se'a** of rain → thousands of liters of she'uvin don't invalidate.
- She'uvin poured **beside** the mikveh and flowing **3 tefaḥim (~23 cm)** over the ground before entering → become ground water → kosher, provided rainwater is the **majority** of the 40 se'a. So >20 se'a of rain can be completed by she'uvin this way: **hamshakha** (201:44–45).

## 8. Formed by a person, or through tum'a

- Filled by a person without making it she'uvin (a holed vessel; splashing by hand from a puddle) → invalid: formed by man, not by Heaven. Even 3 log this way into less than 40 se'a → all invalid (201:15, 39).
- "*Mikveh mayim yihyeh tahor*" → its formation must be in tahara (Zevaḥim 25b): rain entering **directly** through something that can become tamei (an unattached metal pipe) → invalid. If it left that and flowed over the ground first → kosher (201:48).
- Custom: rain from the roof ends in a **3-tefaḥ channel** of soft, absorbent concrete, which fixes any of these problems.

## 9. Zeri'a and hashaka

- **Zeri'a** ("sowing"): a kosher mikveh makes kosher any she'uvin that **falls or flows into it**, even a thousand times its size, and overflow fills more pools that are kosher too. (Ra'avad: d'rabbanan invalid if under 20 se'a of the original rain remain. Others: no limit.) **Halakha:** no limit (SA 201:15, 24).
- **Hashaka** ("kissing"): a pool of she'uvin next to a kosher mikveh, connected by a hole the size of a **flask's spout (~5 cm)** → the moment they touch, the pool is kosher, and stays so even after the hole is closed (some disagree; halakha: stays kosher) (201:52).

## 10. Mikva'ot today

- In the past many immersed in springs, seas and rivers (people lived by water). Elsewhere, a rain pit in the yard was set aside for tevila.
- With piped water and cities, communal mikva'ot were needed. One pool for everyone would get dirty, so the **otzar** (rain reservoir) was separated from the **immersion pool**, made kosher by zeri'a or hashaka, so the water can be changed often.
- Recently many enhance with **both**: two otzarot, each with 40 se'a of rain:
    1. **Otzar zeri'a**: tap water flows into it and overflows via hamshakha into the pool.
    2. **Otzar hashaka**: the pool touches it through a hole ≥5 cm, opened during tevila and closed otherwise (to keep it clean). Closed during a tevila → still kosher (opening is a hiddur) (Igrot Moshe YD 1:112).

## 11. A mikveh from melted ice and snow

- Snow, hail, ice → not she'uvin even if put in a vessel (they stand on their own). Even she'uvin that **froze** lose the she'uvin status; melted, they're kosher.
- So in summer, instead of waiting for rain: freeze water, move the blocks on wooden platforms (not vessels), and let them melt beside the mikveh so the water flows over the ground into it.
- Some question this (minority views) → le-khatchila rainwater; **when needed**, ice is fine (Da'at Kohen 94, 98).

## 12. Tap water

- The national water system mostly comes from kosher sources: lakes, groundwater, springs; treated sewage that seeps back into groundwater (SA 201:47); today mostly **desalinated sea water**, in principle kosher.
- The question is whether pipes have a **receptacle**. Water utilities' pipes often pass through receptacles (pump or meter housings, filters), towers, tanks, boilers, solar heaters → in practice **tap water is she'uvin**. Even attached to the ground, a receptacle made before attaching stays a kli.

## 13. Renewing tahara in Israel

- After the sin, tum'a clings to a person, blocking the heart and the link to the source of life; death clings. The whole system of tum'a and tahara, centered on the Mikdash, raises the physical and reconnects it. Torah and mitzvot need no tevila ("*divrei Torah einam mekablim tum'a*", like fire, Berakhot 22a).
- After the Ḥurban only one tahara remains: a woman's tevila, revealing the life and holiness in the body, in the love and joy between husband and wife, the summit of "*ve-ahavta le-re'akha kamokha*."
- May our return to the Land bring the Torah of Eretz Yisrael, blessing spreading from the joy of husband and wife, and the Third Mikdash.
- The **spring from the Kodesh Ha-kodashim**: thin as a locust's antenna, then a thread, then a small jug's mouth (Yoma 77b); in Yeḥezkel 47, ankle-deep, knee-deep, waist-deep, then a river that heals the **Dead Sea** (Sedom's sea of death): fish, trees fruiting every month, leaves for healing.
- Yoel 4:18: the spring "*will water Naḥal Ha-shittim*," where Israel sinned with Moav: desire itself repaired into love between husband and wife.
- Zekharia 14:8–9: half to the eastern sea, half to the western, touching all the world's waters: "*Hashem eḥad u-shmo eḥad*."
- Then nidda tum'a will end ("*ve-et ru'aḥ ha-tum'a a'avir min ha-aretz*", Midrash Tehillim 146), and death too: "*bila ha-mavet la-netzaḥ*" (Yeshayahu 25:8).
