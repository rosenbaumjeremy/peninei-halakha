## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| | Torah law | After the Sages and the custom |
|---|---|---|
| **Nidda** (regular period) | Tamei 7 days from the start of bleeding, then tevila | Wait until bleeding stops, then **7 clean days**, then tevila |
| **Zava** (bleeding 3 days outside the period) | 7 clean days, then tevila (+ korban in Temple times) | Same as everyone: 7 clean days |
| **Which blood** | Only certain colors are tamei; only with a *hargasha* (sensation) | All reds and blacks are treated as tamei; a **ketem** (stain) without sensation is tamei d'rabbanan |
| **Stain conditions** | – | Bigger than a *gris* (~2 cm circle), on something that can become tamei, on a white surface |

**How it happened:** Rebbi (~150 years after the Ḥurban): nidda counts like zava (1–2 days of blood → 6 clean days; 3+ → 7). Then *benot Yisrael* took on 7 clean days always (accepted as binding halakha). Net effect: about **5–6 extra days** of issur a month.

> **Line to remember:** in the Temple era taharot touched all of life; today **one tahara is left**, a wife's tahara for her husband, and through it the holiness of the Mikdash comes into the Jewish home.

## 1. Nidda and zava

- Each month an egg is released; the uterine lining thickens to receive it. Not fertilized → about 14 days after ovulation the lining breaks down and leaves as **menstrual blood**.
- **Nidda (Torah):** menstrual blood makes a woman tamei for **7 days**, then she immerses (Vayikra 15:19).
- **Zava (Torah):** rarely, illness causes bleeding outside the cycle. If in the **11 days** after the 7 nidda days she bleeds **3 days in a row** → zava. She counts **7 clean days**, then tevila (15:25–28); in Temple times, a korban (two birds) on the 8th day.
- **Two aspects** of this tum'a:
    1. **Marital closeness** is forbidden the whole time: "*ve-el isha be-nidat tum'atah lo tikrav*" (Vayikra 18:19).
    2. **Mikdash and kodashim**: no entering Har Ha-bayit or eating korbanot; kohanim's families ate teruma and ḥalla in tahara.
- Since the Ḥurban there's no para aduma, so all taharot lapsed. **Only in the Jewish home**, in the love between husband and wife, do tum'a and tahara still apply, to direct, raise and sanctify that love.

## 2. The idea of tum'a and tahara

- The root is beyond us; but every mitzva is "*le-tov lanu*" (Devarim 6:24), and we can see its blessing.
- **Tahara ↔ life; tum'a ↔ death.** The more developed the life, the more severe the tum'a when it's lost (Kuzari 2:60–62):
    - Human corpse → *avi avot ha-tum'a*; animals and sheratzim → *av ha-tum'a*; plants don't become tamei when they die (but produce and things made from them can); minerals generally don't (unless made into vessels).
- **The womb** is the source of life and tahara, and so also of tum'a. Nidda = an egg that could have become a child wasn't fertilized and is lost, leaving with the lining meant to support life.
- **Birth tum'a** (7 days for a boy, 14 for a girl): a birth is joy, but also a real crisis and loss of vitality (9:8). The mitzva teaches us to name the lack and rise from it.

## 3. The sin of Adam and Ḥava

- After the sin, death and tum'a entered the world; livelihood, family, and childbirth came with pain: "*harba arbeh itzvonekh ve-heronekh*" (Bereshit 3:16) = the blood of nidda and of virginity, the pain of pregnancy and raising children (Eruvin 100b).
- Tzror Ha-mor: before the sin, women weren't meant to see nidda or zava blood at all.
- Ignoring pain and lack makes us crash. **Awareness** of the brokenness is what starts the repair, up to a level higher than before (like ba'alei teshuva, Berakhot 34b). The mitzvot of tum'a and tahara name the lack and pave the way to fix it.

## 4. Renewing love

- The fall also hurt couples' ability to express love freely and keep it alive; that's why so many marriages fade or break.
- R. Meir (Nidda 31b): why 7 days? Because he becomes used to her and tires of her; so she's tamei 7 days "*so that she'll be as dear to him as on the day of her ḥuppa*."
- During the days of issur: no touching at all, even a hug or through clothing. Hard: the longing grows daily. But the love is **refined**, and anticipation builds until the night of tevila.
- The separation also teaches **gratitude**: not taking each other for granted → more attentive and generous.
- Fixed periods of separation are the best way to keep love burning, but without the mitzva people lack the strength.
- Over the years the love becomes deeper and more committed; after menopause there's no more need for nidda (and no new children, but grandparents nurture life through their descendants). Couples without children: their influence is refined through the cycle, then continues without it (*Simḥat Ha-bayit* 8:5–8).
- In the future, life will grow without needing crisis as a springboard. A taste of it: **pregnancy and nursing**, when love deepens without separation.

## 5. Tamei and tahor blood; stains

- **Torah:** only blood of the **tamei colors** ("*bein dam le-dam*", Devarim 17:8: tamei vs. tahor; ask the Sages) and only with a **hargasha**, a sensation like the onset of a period ("*bi-vsarah*" → until she feels it, Nidda 57b).
- Blood that left without sensation doesn't make the woman tamei by Torah law, but the blood itself is tamei and makes clothing tamei. So the **Sages decreed**: a **ketem** (stain from blood without sensation) makes her tamei like nidda, but only if it's found where it *could* make a garment tamei:
    1. **Larger than a gris** (circle ~2 cm in diameter);
    2. On something that **can become tamei** (cloth, a utensil; not stone or paper);
    3. On something **white**, so the color can be judged (2:4, 7–9).
- The difference between the colors is very fine. After the Ḥurban the tradition was lost; Amoraim already said they couldn't tell. By the Rishonim, all Israel agreed to be strict on **all reds and blacks** (2:2).

## 6. Nidda, zava, and Rebbi's enactment

- **Nidda (Torah):** count 7 days from the start; if bleeding stopped by late afternoon of day 7, immerse on night 8.
- **Zava gedola:** 3 consecutive days of bleeding in the 11 days → 7 clean days + tevila (+ korban).
- **Zava ketana:** 1 or 2 days in the 11 → *shomeret yom ke-neged yom*: if clean the next day, immerse (Nidda 73a).
- ~150 years after the Ḥurban, **Rebbi** saw women confusing nidda and zava counts, and counting nidda from tahor-colored blood a few days before the real period. So he decreed: **1–2 days of blood → 6 clean days; 3+ days → 7 clean days**, then tevila (Nidda 66a).

## 7. The custom of benot Yisrael

- Fearing confusion between Rebbi's two counts, women took on **always 7 clean days**, even after one day of blood.
- About three generations later, R. Zeira: "*benot Yisrael* were strict that even if they see a drop of blood like a mustard seed, they sit 7 clean days" (Nidda 66a). Accepted as **binding halakha** (Berakhot 31a; SA YD 183:1).
- Actually small: under 3% more than Rebbi's enactment.
- Gradually also accepted: treating post-birth *dam tohar* as tamei (9:8).

## 8. Why the Sages add fences

- Around each Torah issur is a gray zone that leads to the issur. The Torah told the Sages to fence it: "*u-shmartem et mishmarti*" → "make a guard for My guard" (Yevamot 21a); and "*lo tasur*" (Devarim 17:11).
- **Written Torah** = the heavenly principle; **Oral Torah** = the form it takes in practice.
- First Temple, with prophecy: fewer fences. After it, Anshei Knesset Ha-gedola added fences (Avot 1:1), and that's what kept Israel loyal through all the exiles.
- **For nidda**, while the Second Temple stood and taharot were practiced, the Sages avoided adding days:
    - Merging nidda and zava, or tahor and tamei blood, would distort Torah law (zava brings a korban, nidda doesn't). Meshekh Ḥokhma: when the Mikdash is rebuilt, Rebbi's enactment will lapse.
    - Extra days would keep women from the Mikdash and taharot, and burden homes where a tamei woman kept separate utensils and clothing.
    - Instead, fences on taharot and kodashim served as fences for nidda too.
- After the Ḥurban and exile, traditions were forgotten and errors spread → gradually Rebbi's enactment, the women's 7 days, and strictness on all colors. Total: **about 5–6 extra days a month**.

## 9. Mitzvat ona and the inner reason for the fences

- **Question:** how could fences add days and cancel two Torah mitzvot, *ona* and *peru u-revu*?
- **Answer:** after the Ḥurban all joy was dimmed, ona included, so the Sages fenced nidda as they fenced everything.
    - The source of the Shekhina between a couple is the **Kodesh Ha-kodashim**: the keruvim were shaped like a man and woman embracing ("see your love before Hashem like the love of male and female", Yoma 54a); when Israel sinned they turned apart (BB 99a).
    - "From the day the Mikdash was destroyed, the taste of intimacy was taken away" (Sanhedrin 75a).
    - R. Yishmael ben Elisha (BB 60b): we should have decreed against meat and wine, even against marrying, but a decree the public can't bear isn't made.
- **Another reason:** since separation keeps her "as dear as at the ḥuppa," after the Ḥurban, when joy weakened, more days were needed to strengthen longing and renewal (*Simḥat Ha-bayit* 3:15).

## 10. The joy of ona and caution about stringencies

- During the days of tahara, **ona is in full force**: the husband must bring his wife as much joy as he can, and she him.
- Ona and peru u-revu partly repair the exile: they draw light from the Kodesh Ha-kodashim into Jewish homes ("wherever Israel was exiled, the Shekhina went with them"; peru u-revu brings the geula, Yevamot 62a).
- So poskim followed **King David**, who "dirtied his hands with blood… to permit a woman to her husband" (Berakhot 4a).
- **Can we cancel the 7 clean days today?** Arguments: we're back in the Land and could do more ona; temptations are greater and long issur is hard for many. Still, **we have no authority** to cancel Rebbi's enactment (head of the Sanhedrin) or a custom the Talmud made binding. It fits our spiritual state: the Land is being rebuilt, the Torah not yet fully. We pray "*hashiva shofteinu*."
- **But:** wherever halakha allows a leniency, **be lenient**: strictness here blocks a Torah mitzva and can cause stumbling. And enhance simḥat ona in the permitted days.

## 11. The meaning of tevila

- Tahara comes through immersing the whole body (5:1; 10:1). Tum'a is tied to death; in tevila one can't breathe, and coming up is like **being born again** (Ḥinukh 173).
- On its own, dunking wouldn't help; it purifies because it's **Hashem's mitzva**, connecting us to the Source of life: "*ve-atem ha-devekim ba-Shem… ḥayim kulkhem ha-yom*" (Devarim 4:4).
- Yeḥezkel 36:25–26: "I will sprinkle pure water… a new heart." R. Akiva: "Fortunate are you, Israel… *mikveh Yisrael Hashem*" (Yoma 8:9). The one who immerses joins Knesset Yisrael.
- Rishonim: tevila doesn't require teshuva, but it's fitting to intend that thoughts and deeds be renewed, and the soul cleansed like the body (Ḥinukh 173; Rambam, Mikva'ot 11:12).

## 12. The meaning of water

- Water = the basis of life; a day of rain is like the day of creation, like Matan Torah, like teḥiyat ha-metim (Ta'anit 7a).
- Water expresses **ḥesed** given to all equally (trees and thorns, sheep and wolves, righteous and wicked). In Kabbala, water = *ḥesed*. Immersing, one is included in that ḥesed, and the lack and death fall away.
- Water preceded creation ("*ve-ru'aḥ Elokim meraḥefet al penei ha-mayim*"); "*olam ḥesed yibaneh*." Avraham's trait was ḥesed.
- The mikveh must be water gathered **naturally in the ground**, a return to the primal ḥesed (10:1–2).
- Mikveh water also = **unity**: drops gathered together. Tum'a comes from separation; the mikveh reconnects.
- Water = **Torah** ("*hoy kol tzame lekhu la-mayim*") and **da'at** ("*ka-mayim la-yam mekhasim*").

## 13. Tahara in the Mikdash and after the Ḥurban

- The goal of tahara is to connect the **body** and physical life, with its joy and emotion, to holiness. Torah and mitzvot themselves need no tahara (the spirit doesn't become tamei, Berakhot 22a), but the physical can be "blocked."
- With the Mikdash, all Israel was connected to it and blessing spread to every physical area; kohanim kept tahara everywhere.
- After the Ḥurban all taharot lapsed. **Only one remains: a wife's tahara for her husband.** Through it a couple brings the holiness of the Mikdash into their home and sanctifies the physical side of their joy.
- May the merit of Jewish women's tahara bring the Mikdash soon.
