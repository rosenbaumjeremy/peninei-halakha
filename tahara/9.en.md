## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy** | Ona continues; once confirmed, no onot (mesuleket damim) until weaning or first period. Bleeding: from a vaginal/cervical wound → tahor; from the uterus → tamei unless a doctor is sure it's an unrelated wound |
| **When does labor make her tamei?** | When blood leaves the uterus, or when the baby's head (or most of it) emerges. Water breaking → tahor; cervical-check blood the midwife says is from the cervix → tahor; mucus plug with blood / membrane sweep without heavy bleeding → may be lenient |
| **Husband in the delivery room** | Not looking at covered areas; holding her hand: many are strict, permitted if she's in real distress |
| **Shabbat** | Dangerously ill: drive normally; nearest hospital; one companion |
| **After birth** | Torah: boy 7 + 33 days, girl 14 + 66; custom: all post-birth blood is tamei → when bleeding stops, hefsek + 7 clean days + tevila (girl: not before 14 days). C-section: like nidda |
| **Miscarriage** | Before 40 days: nidda. After: birth tum'a (unknown sex → 14 days). "*Dayan ha-emet*" |
| **Postpartum low mood** | Rest and sleep; persistent → family doctor; depression → psychiatrist and medication, no delay |

## 1. Pregnancy

- **Ona continues** throughout pregnancy (for most, twice a week, *Simḥat Ha-bayit* 2:7). Early on, nausea etc. → more gently, but the mitzva stands. The Sages: in the last six months it benefits the baby; in the last three, an easier birth.
- **Confirmed pregnant** → mesuleket damim: no onot until weaning or her first period (6:10).
- **Bleeding** → see a doctor. From a **vaginal or cervical wound** → tahor. From the **uterus** → tamei (ch. 2), unless the doctor is **sure** it's from a wound unrelated to the pregnancy (2:14).

## 2. Medical matters

- In the last three months, pray that the baby comes out safely (Berakhot 60a).
- A special mitzva to look after her health: do the **recommended** checks (in Israel, what the kupa funds; extras usually not needed; with supplemental insurance, what it funds) (7:6).
- Exams don't make her tamei; nor does a **cervical cerclage** (wound blood, from the cervix) (7:7).
- **Amniocentesis/genetic testing** when recommended → do it (*Simḥat Ha-bayit* 9:9). Severe illness in the fetus → whether abortion is advisable, permitted or forbidden depends on severity and the parents' emotional state → ask an expert rav. **Danger to the mother** → all agree abortion is a mitzva.
- Anything doctors recommend is permitted, including an **epidural**.
- Doctors advise **induction or a C-section** for mother's or baby's safety → follow them; if worried and there's time, a second opinion. **Don't refuse for *segulot*** (e.g., fear that changing the birth time harms the child); protecting life is a mitzva, and those who ignore medical advice for segulot will answer for it.

## 3. When labor makes her tamei

- **Birth itself** makes her tamei (Torah) from when the baby's **head or most of it** emerges, even without blood: a boy 7 days, a girl 14 (Nidda 28a; SA YD 194:10).
- Often **blood** from contractions comes first → tamei. If the doctor/midwife says it's from the canal or cervix → tahor.
- Some say once she's "*on the birthstool*" (strong, frequent contractions, can't walk) she's presumed tamei. **Halakha:** only if she actually saw blood. Even after being checked in the delivery room, if labor hasn't begun and no blood → tahor.
    - No internal check needed; enough that no blood is flowing onto her clothes/pad. Better **not** to check internally (avoid doubts).
    - A non-tamei stain (colored cloth, pad) → tahor; **a lot** of blood → presumably uterine → tamei even on a pad.

## 4. Questions before birth

- **Dilation check**: a little blood on the glove, usually from the sensitive cervix. Midwife is sure it's cervical → tahor; otherwise tamei.
- **Waters breaking** (amniotic fluid) → tahor as long as no uterine blood.
- **Mucus plug**: no blood color → tahor. With blood: some are strict (uterine blood into the plug), some lenient (wound blood). **May be lenient.**
- **Membrane sweep** (stripping): usually painful with light bleeding. Some say tamei. **Halakha:** without heavy bleeding it's from the procedure → tahor. Heavy flow → uterine from contractions → tamei.

## 5. The husband's help

- She's tamei and needs support getting into the ambulance or walking → a woman helps if present. If none, in a pressing case he may (touch without desire is d'rabbanan per the vast majority; permitted for a sick person when needed, 3:8).
- He may not look at **covered areas** (3:3): she stays covered, he sits or stands by her head, even during exams. At the birth itself the strict step out or behind the curtain; some stay where they see only her face.
- **Holding her hand:** in the past births were at home with women helping; later in hospitals, often with her mother or sister. Now often the husband is the main or only companion.
    - Some permit holding her hand and supporting her in hard contractions (d'rabbanan, a sick person).
    - Many forbid (it needn't be him). **Proper to be strict**; but if she's in **severe distress** and demands that **he** hold her hand → lenient.

## 6. The hour of birth

- A unique moment for a couple: no greater partner than him, yet she's forbidden to him. Perhaps it expresses its greatness: pain and joy, fear and hope; the gate between life and death opens. No touch can express it; separation and prayer fit.
- Like **Yom Kippur**: the covenant of marriage is revealed (shidukhim in Temple times; the Kohen Gadol entering the Kodesh Ha-kodashim, invalid if widowed), yet husband and wife are forbidden; the Kohen Gadol separated 7 days before (Yoma 2a).
- **Teshuva** before birth: a time of danger ("*for three sins women die in childbirth: nidda, ḥalla, candle-lighting*", Shabbat 31b). Pray for a safe birth and a healthy child. The staff is devoted, but the **key of birth is in Hashem's hand** (Ta'anit 2a).
- **Custom:** from the ninth month, honor the husband with **opening the ark** in shul so he prays that she "opens" safely (Ḥida).
- After birth: a **boy** → "*Ha-tov ve-ha-metiv*"; a **girl** → "*Sheheḥeyanu*" (*Berakhot* 17:13).

## 7. Birth on Shabbat

- A woman in labor is **dangerously ill** → drive to the hospital as on a weekday when the medical guidance says to.
- **Carrying without an eruv:** what's needed medically (medicines, documents, ID) ✓. Important but not medical (clothes, food, books) → with a **shinui**, walking straight to the car.
- **Muktzeh** (money, phone needed after Shabbat) → put in the bag with a shinui. Rushing → take what's needed without thinking about shinui. Muktzeh already in the bag → may take the bag. So pack before Shabbat (*Shabbat* 27:6).
- **Nearest hospital.** Not a farther one because it's more professional or halakhic (not pikuaḥ nefesh; on weekdays women in the north/south aren't told to go to the center). Slightly farther to calm her → ok. Special risk with a protocol at the far one → ok, per accepted medical guidance (*Shabbat* 27:7).
- **One companion.** A doula isn't pikuaḥ nefesh; with husband or mother present she doesn't drive on Shabbat. Exceptions: hysteria demanding both, or severe anxiety demanding the doula. Don't **plan** for more than one. Long drive with the husband driving → mother/doula may come to help.

## 8. Tum'at yoledet and becoming tahor

- **Torah:** boy → **7** days tamei, then **33** days of *dam tohar*; girl → **14**, then **66** (Vayikra 12). By Torah law she could immerse after 7/14 even if still bleeding; blood until day 40/80 doesn't forbid her to her husband (but forbids the Mikdash and kodashim; then a korban olah in thanks and a ḥatat for the lacks of birth).
- Boy vs. girl: with a boy the tum'a and repair are revealed (orla → brit, shorter); with a girl deeper and hidden → double.
- **In practice:** following the women's 7-clean-days custom, all Israel treats **dam tohar** as tamei too. So: when post-birth bleeding **stops** → hefsek, **7 clean days**, tevila. Bleeding again later in those days → tamei again, same process.
    - A girl, and she finished 7 clean days before day 14 (rare) → wait until 14 days to immerse.
- **C-section:** no birth tum'a and no dam tohar (Nidda 40a); but post-birth uterine bleeding makes her tamei as nidda → hefsek, 7 clean days, tevila.

## 9. The meaning of birth tum'a

- Every descent of a great idea into the world involves something like death: "*yehi or*" — the great light was diminished so the wicked wouldn't misuse it (Zohar); after the repair it will shine even more. Every vision starts greater than its fulfillment.
- In a birth, hopes are infinite: this child will be perfect, maybe the redeemer. Then come routine, pain, exhaustion; the child will face struggles like everyone. Many women feel some **postpartum low mood**.
- The tum'a expresses sorrow over hopes that won't be fulfilled. In those days, a kind of mourning, she can accept reality, welcome her child with joy, gather strength, and delight in being a **mother**. The father too, helping her rest, absorbs becoming a **father**.
- **Stage 1** (more severe): the mother emptied of dreams; the baby falling from the wonderful world of the womb into this one. **Stage 2** (*dam tohar*, milder): life grows with the knowledge it includes struggles.
- **Practical:** ensure she can **rest and sleep** to recover; without it, low mood can become depression.
    - Persistent low mood → **family doctor** for mild medication, plus family help to sleep more.
    - **Postpartum depression** → must see a **psychiatrist** (through the kupa) and take proper medication. Don't delay or rely on alternative treatments: it's a **dangerous illness**.

## 10. After a miscarriage

- **Before 40 days** from conception (limbs not yet formed) → **nidda** only: hefsek, 7 clean days, tevila.
- **After 40 days** → **birth tum'a**: boy 7 days, girl 14; **unknown → 14** (hefsek no earlier than day 7, so tevila after 14) (SA YD 194:3). Usually bleeding lasts more than a week anyway.
- Shabbat is desecrated to save a fetus even under 40 days (*Shabbat* 27:3).
- **Burial:** a baby who died within 30 days: some say no mitzva to bury (*nefel*; Or Zarua; SA OḤ 526:9–10); others say bury, even months premature, if it has human form (MA 526:20). **Custom:** strict; burial by the **ḥevra kadisha** without the parents' involvement, to spare them needless pain.
- A **D&C** is often recommended after a natural miscarriage (proper healing, future fertility).
- No **aveilut** for a fetus, but the loss can be very painful → the couple say "*Barukh… Dayan ha-emet*" (one may say it and the other answer amen) (*Berakhot* 17:8).
