## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Stage | What |
|---|---|
| **Before the wedding** | Learn the halakhot (a good *madrikhat kallot* starts from the joy of ona); pills to time her period, tried two cycles ahead with a knowledgeable doctor |
| **Tahara for the wedding** | Hefsek at least 8 days before; 7 clean days with external bedikot only (hymen); tevila ideally 1–2 days before (max ~4). Required even with no period (*dam ḥimud*), with a berakha |
| **Week before** | Custom: bride and groom don't meet (many don't talk on the phone) |
| **Wedding day** | Teshuva and prayer; most fast until after the ḥuppa (not if it causes suffering; follow parents' custom) |
| **After the first full union** | She's tamei (*dam betulim*), even with no blood seen; wait 4 days (or clean) → hefsek → 7 clean days |
| **Ḥuppat nidda** | Hard: harḥakot apply, no *yiḥud* until she's tahor and they've been together; stay with parents or host two children |
| **Recommended timing** | Rav Melamed: postpone the *be'ilat mitzva* to the end of sheva berakhot and enjoy each other first (others say do it right away) |

## 1. Preparing for the wedding

- Learn the basics of simḥat ona and tahara, ideally with a **madrikhat kallot**. A good one knows the halakhot, explains them well, and **starts from the value of ona** (*Simḥat Ha-bayit* 1–3), presenting tahara as directing and raising love. A weaker one reverses it and doesn't build on the mitzva itself.
- **Pills:** most brides time their last period so she can be tahor for the wedding and the period falls **at least 3 weeks before**, so that after dam betulim and stopping the pills, the next period comes right away and the tamei time is shorter.
    - Pills differ; some cause spotting or low mood. Consult a knowledgeable doctor early enough to take them **two cycles** before, so the type or dose can be adjusted and she arrives tahor and calm.

## 2. Avoiding a ḥuppat nidda

- Make every effort to be **tahor** at the wedding. Otherwise the joy is badly hurt and all harḥakot (ch. 3) apply: no hand-holding, no passing things.
    - At the **ḥuppa**, to avoid embarrassment, he may lower the veil, put on the ring, hand her the cup and ketuba, trying not to touch.
    - **Yiḥud is forbidden** until she's tahor: sleep in separate rooms at parents' or another home with people (§13). No *ḥeder yiḥud*.
    - Rambam: marriage depends on a proper yiḥud, so it may not even take effect. But since postponing causes great loss and pain, we follow most poskim and **hold the wedding** anyway.

## 3. Bedikot for a bride

- She must become tahor from her period and from possible *dam ḥimud* (§4).
- **Hefsek tahara at least 8 days** before the wedding; 7 clean days from that sunset.
- Bedikot twice a day (once if hard; even 2 in total suffice, 4:8), with a white cloth.
- Because of the hymen she checks **only at the opening**, gently, without any pain (no bleeding).
- The 7 days end exactly a week after the hefsek (hefsek late Tuesday → tevila Tuesday night a week later).

## 4. Dam ḥimud

- The Sages enacted: when a bride **agrees to marry**, she counts 7 clean days and immerses. Her excitement might cause a little blood, possibly lost in normal moisture (Nidda 66a).
- The time of concern: when she agrees; if that was long before, then during the **final preparations** (2–3 weeks before).
- Even a woman who no longer menstruates (older) → still 7 clean days and tevila, **with a berakha** (it's an enactment).
- If she could be tahor 1–2 weeks early, better to **delay** so tevila is **1–2 days** (at most ~4) before the wedding; the concern grows as it nears. After tevila, external checks each day until the wedding.
- Engagement **broken and renewed** after tevila → again 7 clean days and tevila (SA YD 192:3).
- **Remarrying his ex-wife**, even pregnant from him → also (192:5).
- **Older couples** too, unless it's clear the groom is no longer capable and the marriage is for companionship.

## 5. Not meeting the week before

- Strictly, only actual blood would make her tamei; the Sages were stricter and required every bride to become tahor.
- A spiritual reason: she purifies herself from the time of **searching and longing** without completion, and sanctifies herself for union with her husband. Like counting the 7 weeks to Matan Torah, the "wedding day" of Hashem and Israel.
- **Custom (recent generations):** the couple doesn't meet the week before; many avoid phone calls. Halakhic basis: Rishonim say the closer the wedding, the greater the dam ḥimud concern, more so if they meet. Spiritually: through longing, they're purified from the time of only wanting, and come to the wedding in joy.

## 6. Teshuva before the wedding

- A mitzva for bride and groom to do teshuva, especially on the wedding day, and pray to live together in love and joy, in Torah and mitzvot, helping each other realize their talents and good aims; to honor parents; children raised to Torah, ḥuppa and good deeds; health and livelihood; joyful Shabbatot and festivals. Pray for family and friends too.
- "*When a man marries, his sins are stopped up*" ("*matza isha matza tov*", Yevamot 63b); the bride too.
    - Single, they're like two halves ("*a man without a wife is not a man*", 63a; without joy, blessing, good, Torah, wall, peace, 62b). Joined in faith and love, they're like newborn; past sins are covered.
    - Full teshuva → sins become merits; sinning again → they come back.
- On Yom Kippur we work hard for teshuva; on the wedding day, from the holiness of the moment and their sincere desire, it comes naturally, and their prayers are answered.

## 7. Fasting on the wedding day

- Most communities: bride and groom **fast** (teshuva and prayer; also so the groom doesn't arrive tipsy or unfocused). Some Sefardi/Mizraḥi don't fast; some: only the groom (her teshuva is her tevila).
- **Groom follows his father, bride her mother.** Ba'alei teshuva choose.
- Not on days when fasting is forbidden: Rosh Ḥodesh, Ḥanuka, Nisan.
- Worried it'll exhaust them or cause headaches → **not required**. Today fasts are harder emotionally, and this fast is for teshuva, not atonement through suffering. Eat as needed, no alcohol, set Torah study and give tzedaka; if possible fast until midday.
- The fast lasts until after the ḥuppa or tzeit, whichever is first.

## 8. Be'ilat mitzva

- The first full union has a special virtue: they make an **eternal covenant**, "*ve-hayu le-basar eḥad*" (Bereshit 2:23–24). From it they continue in ona all their lives, and peru u-revu (Tosafot Ketubot 4a).
- Betulim express the bride keeping herself for her husband, who opens the source of life in her. It brings joy and sometimes pain, expressing a covenant for good times and bad: "*bedamayikh ḥayi*" (Yeḥezkel 16:6). Her readiness to open herself to him and his commitment to her happiness cover every lack.

## 9. Betulim then and now

- The ketuba: 200 zuz for a betula, 100 otherwise (Ketubot 10b). In the past, no blood → he could claim she wasn't a betula.
- **Today** that claim doesn't hold: brides are usually over 18, and the hymen often stretches naturally; more so with age or tampon use.
- The virtue of be'ilat mitzva remains: through it they unite and make the covenant.

## 10. Dam betulim and its tum'a

- **Enactment:** after a full be'ilat mitzva with a betula, she becomes **tamei** from dam betulim. No need to separate right away; once they part, all harḥakot begin.
- By Torah law it's **wound blood** (tahor). The Sages worried:
    - Uterine blood may also come out with the excitement.
    - Bleeding may continue in the next unions (no time to heal), and later a period might be mistaken for betulim blood → karet, especially as desire is strongest in the first days.
- **No blood seen** → still tamei if both are sure it was a **full union** (maybe a little blood was lost or covered).
    - **Unsure** it was full: blood seen on the sheet/cloth → tamei; none → tahor. Only after a full union is she tamei without blood.
    - To avoid the doubt: use **dark** sheets and cloths.
- **Not a betula** (widow, divorcee, or with prior relations) → not tamei from the first union.

## 11. Becoming tahor after dam betulim

- Can't start right away: wait for the **4th day** (semen, 4:5–7), hefsek at its end, then 7 clean days. Or the next day **clean the canal** well, hefsek before sunset, start.
- **In practice:** the bride stops the pills right after the full union; her period comes within days; then hefsek and 7 clean days after it ends.
- Bleeding from the hymen **again** after the next union → tamei again (and a third time) as long as remnants remain. Very painful, so prevent it:
    - **Advice:** the day after be'ilat mitzva, gently go over the area with a damp cloth to remove remnants. Experience shows recurrence becomes rare; blood seen afterward (not at her period time) is treated as **wound blood** from the canal → tahor.

## 12. Medical opening of the hymen

- Union too painful and repeated tries fail (thick hymen, or great anxiety) → a doctor opens it in a minor procedure.
- **Halakha:** the bleeding doesn't make her tamei. The enactment is for betulim blood **during union** (excitement, maybe uterine blood); not here. Blood after later unions = wound blood → tahor.
- Couldn't do the advice of §11 → a doctor can check whether it's fully open and open any remainder; then later blood = wound blood.

## 13. When yiḥud is forbidden

- Normally forbidden man and woman may not be alone. A married couple in nidda days may, since they've already been together and will again; they can wait (the "fence of roses", Sanhedrin 37a; 3:1).
- But only **after** they've been together. **Ḥuppat nidda**, or her period started before they were together → **yiḥud forbidden** until tevila and union. (If they lay close with no barrier at all, no more yiḥud issur. If out of excessive anxiety they didn't, and she saw her period → forbidden.)
- **Practical:** stay at the **parents'** until tevila night, in separate rooms that can only be reached through a hallway family members use (Ketubot 4a; SA 192:4). Awake → not alone in a closed room: by day not locked; at night door open and light on.
- **At home** → host **two children** age ~6+ from the family; at night he sleeps with the boy in one room, she with the girl in another.

## 14. Postponing be'ilat mitzva

- Couples want to rejoice together during the **seven days of celebration** and are pained that she becomes tamei right after the first night. And going straight from no touch to full union is hard for many; the first time may be unpleasant, the memory of the first days sad, and if she only has a painful memory she may struggle to open up to ona with joy.
- **Rav Melamed's advice:** postpone be'ilat mitzva to the **end of the seven days** and meanwhile bring each other joy with much love; when desire builds, they may be close in a way that does not affect the hymen at all.
- Some say do be'ilat mitzva as soon as possible (with concerns about postponing), and many follow that. Rav Melamed: from experience, postponing is the right guidance today.

## 15. Be'ilat mitzva on Shabbat

- **Permitted.** The joy of the mitzva outweighs the pain; not *ḥovel* (betulim blood is considered stored in place, not drawn from the body); not *tikun* of making an opening (intact is the "fixed" state; opening it is a loss worth it for the union) (Ketubot 5b–7a; SA OḤ 280:2).

## 16. Bedikot after the wedding

- Some say a bride **without a fixed cycle** checks that union doesn't trigger her period: after betulim bleeding ends, for the first **3 times**, she checks before (clean), and both check with cloths after (SA YD 186:2, per Rif).
- Most Rishonim: no reason to worry; in practice the chance is negligible (Shakh 186:1).
- **Halakha:** lenient, no extra checks. Those who wish may be strict.
