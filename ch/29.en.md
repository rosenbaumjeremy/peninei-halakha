## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Piece of the eruv | What's required |
|---|---|
| **Enclosure, reshut ha-rabim d'oraita** | Real wall/fence ≥ 10 tefaḥim (~1 m), gates locked at night |
| **Enclosure, karmelit** (most streets today) | **Tzurot ha-petaḥ** are enough |
| **Tzurat ha-petaḥ** | Two posts ≥ 10 tefaḥim + a wire **directly over** the tops, never lower than 10 tefaḥim |
| **Gaps** | Each < 10 amot (4.56 m); on each side, gaps < standing parts |
| **The food ("eruv")** | Bread/matza owned by everyone: 2 meals' worth (8 eggs le-khatchila, 6 be-di'avad) |
| **Residents who don't join** (non-observant Jews, non-Jews) | Rent their share from a city official with the right to enter homes |
| **Broke on Shabbat** | Non-Jew may fix it (even d'oraita); a Jew only with a **bow knot**; don't announce it publicly |

**Name confusion:** technically the *eruv* is the shared food that "merges" everyone; the fence/wire is the meḥitza. Everyone calls the wire "the eruv" too.

## 1. How an eruv transforms a public domain

- Recap (ch. 21): carrying is allowed in a reshut ha-yaḥid; ✗ 4 amot in reshut ha-rabim; ✗ between domains.
- An eruv turns the area into a **reshut ha-yaḥid**: carry anywhere inside and between homes, yards and streets.
- **Two parts:**
    1. **The area**: enclosed by a meḥitza.
    2. **The people**: a partnership via **two meals' worth of food** owned by everyone. That food is the real "*eruv*" (it merges the homes into one domain).
- **Reshut ha-rabim d'oraita** → needs a **real wall/fence** ≥ 10 tefaḥim (~1 m) and gates **locked at night** (SA 364:2).
- **Karmelit** (d'rabbanan) → **tzurot ha-petaḥ** suffice (SA 362:10–11).

## 2. Tzurat ha-petaḥ

- Most poskim: today's streets are a **karmelit** (21:8–9) → tzurot ha-petaḥ around them are enough.
- **Shape:** two posts + a lintel (board or wire) over them, like a doorway.
- **Height:** posts ≥ **10 tefaḥim**; the wire ≥ 10 tefaḥim off the ground. If it **sags** below that anywhere, the whole span between those posts is a breach.
- Posts more than **10 amot** (4.56 m) apart with no valid lintel → **breach** → the whole eruv is invalid.
- Posts must be **sturdy**: not sway in a normal wind, and able to hold some door, even a flimsy straw one (SA 362:11).
- **Width:** most poskim: unlimited (a doorway can be 1,000 amot wide). Rambam: if most of the perimeter is tzurot ha-petaḥ, each ≤ 10 amot. Consider Rambam le-khatchila when possible; in practice, enclosing cities that way is too hard → lenient (SA 362:10).
- **The wire:** secured so a normal wind won't detach it. Le-khatchila **taut** (no swaying, no sagging below the post tops). Be-di'avad, swaying or sagging is still kosher (MB 362:65; AHS 362:37).

## 3. Wire over the posts; utility poles

- The wire must run **over** the post, not **beside** it (a lintel sits *on* the doorposts).
- Even a short post with the wire far above it (like power lines) is fine **if the wire is directly above** it and the post is ≥ 10 tefaḥim.
- **Crooked post** → the wire must be over its **tip** (SA 362:11; MB 64).
- **Utility poles** usually carry cables **beside**, not over, them → don't count. Fix: put up extra ~1 m posts **directly under** the cables.

## 4. Fences, gaps, and a tel ha-mitlaket

- A fence ≥ 10 tefaḥim = a real wall, works even for reshut ha-rabim d'oraita (21:2–3). **Chain-link** is fine if each hole is < 3 tefaḥim (*lavud*).
- **Gaps:** kosher if each gap is **< 10 amot** and, on each side, the total of the gaps is **less than** the total standing fence/tzurot ha-petaḥ (MB 362:45; AHS 362:23: compare the whole perimeter). A gap > 10 amot, or more open than closed on a side → invalid (SA 362:9).
- **Fenced yards** along one side: each yard > 10 amot wide and the spaces between them < 10 amot → that side needs nothing more.
- **Terraces** ≥ 10 tefaḥim high around the city (or part of it) → count as a wall there.
- **Tel ha-mitlaket:** a slope dropping **10 tefaḥim (76 cm) within 4 amot (1.824 m)** horizontally = a wall (SA 345:2).
- **Gate** at the entrance wider than 10 amot: if it's **closed at night**, the eruv is valid even while it's open (SA 364:2; Melumdei Milḥama §74). Gate usually left open, or just a **boom barrier** → put a tzurat ha-petaḥ over it.

## 5. The partnership: two meals' worth of food

- Fencing isn't enough; all residents must be **partners** via jointly owned **bread**. Need not be the five grains; **rice bread** is ok (SA 366:8).
- **Amount:**
    - Fewer than 18 people → a **grogeret** (dried-fig volume) per person.
    - 18 or more (even 1,000) → fixed at **two meals' worth**: le-khatchila **8 eggs (~400 ml)**, be-di'avad **6 eggs (~300 ml)** (SA 368:3).
- Anyone may eat it. **Eaten on Shabbat** → still may carry all that Shabbat (it was there at bein ha-shmashot). Next Shabbat needs a new one.
- **Kibbutz** with a communal dining room → just the tzurat ha-petaḥ; the shared kitchen food is the partnership.

## 6. Setting the eruv and its berakha

- Custom: use **matza** (lasts, valid as long as it's edible) (SA 368:5).
- Custom: make a new one **before Pesaḥ** each year, with the berakha and declaration; often the rav does it for the community. Forgot? As long as the old one exists, keep carrying.
- Keep it in **one box or bag**, usually **in or near the shul** (communal space) (SA 366:4).
- **Acquiring for everyone:** the one setting it lifts the matzot **a tefaḥ**, intending to acquire them for all residents. They must have belonged to **someone else** before.
- **Berakha:** "*… asher kideshanu be-mitzvotav ve-tzivanu **al mitzvat eruv***."
- **Declaration:** "*Ba-eruv ha-zeh yehei mutar lanu…*": with this eruv it will be permitted for us, all who live in this city and whoever joins us, to carry out and in, from houses to yard and yard to houses, house to house, yard to yard, roof to roof, houses and yards to alleys and back, on all upcoming Shabbatot and Yamim Tovim (SA 366:15; MB 366:83).

## 7. An eruv where Shabbat desecrators live

- The eruv works only if **every** resident is a partner. One resident who doesn't want in → the area isn't one domain → **invalid**.
- So a problem: **non-observant Jews** (not interested) and **non-Jews** (their homes aren't included) (SA 385:3; 382:1).
- Classic solution: he **rents his home** for Shabbat to an observant resident. Impossible in a big town or city.
- **Custom:** rent all homes from a **city official** with the right to enter every house (SA 391:1).
    - Objection: in a democracy the mayor can't enter homes without a warrant.
    - Still lenient: in **wartime** (and drills) the town major / **Home Front Command** can requisition homes → they have a share in every house → rent that share before Shabbat.

## 8. An eruv that falls on Shabbat

- Wire snapped mid-Shabbat. Two questions: fix it? tell everyone?
- **Fixing:**
    - Le-khatchila a **non-Jew** fixes it, even if it takes d'oraita melakhot (great need: saving the public from the carrying issur) (MB 276:25; 9:12).
    - **A Jew may never do d'oraita**: ✗ stick a fallen pole back into the ground; ✗ tie a **permanent knot**.
    - **Bow knot?** Some forbid: it creates a **meḥitza ha-materet** (a wall that makes something permitted, 15:4). Others permit, to save the public from sin. **That's the custom** (Mahari Ashkenazi §13; Panim Me'irot 1:30; Sho'el U-meshiv; SSK 17:34).
- **Can't be fixed → don't announce it publicly.** Some would carry anyway, now be-mezid; better they be shogeg. Tell only those who will definitely stop carrying.
