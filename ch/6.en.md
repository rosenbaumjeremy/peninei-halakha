## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Obligation** | Friday night = Torah (Zakhor); day = rabbinic ("Kidusha Raba") |
| **Who** | Men and women equally; a woman can be motzi a man; a minor can't be motzi adults |
| **Over what** | Wine (grape juice, mevushal, white all ok); night no wine → bread; day → beer/vodka, then bread |
| **Amounts** | Cup holds a revi'it (75 ml; strict 150 ml); drink melo lugmav (~50–55 ml) |
| **Before kiddush** | No eating or drinking (even water) once Shabbat started; by day, after Shaḥarit |
| **Be-makom se'uda** | Eat a kezayit of bread/mezonot (or a revi'it of wine) where you made kiddush, without a long break |

## 1. Zakhor and Shamor

- Shamor clears space; Zakhor fills it with the fundamentals of faith.
- Kiddush mentions **creation** (Shemot 20) and **yetziat Mitzrayim** (Devarim 5): God created, and keeps running the world.
- Main fulfilment: kiddush over wine; really the whole day, and preparing during the week.
- **Women** are obligated (Zakhor and Shamor said together) → can be motzi men. **Minors** (rabbinic) can't be motzi adults (SA 271:2).

## 2. Fulfilling Zakhor

- Torah: words mentioning Shabbat, creation and Exodus. Sages (Anshei Knesset HaGedola): fixed berakha, over **wine**, before a **meal**. Va-yekhulu said first.
- Many: **havdala** is also Torah-level Zakhor (marking the end).
- Day kiddush: only Ha-gafen with some verses; "Kidusha Raba" is a euphemism.
- Kiddush ASAP after davening Friday night. Missed it → make the night kiddush (without Va-yekhulu) in the morning, or any time until **shki'a**, and eat after.
- The Amida probably doesn't fulfill Zakhor (no intent; no Exodus) → rely on kiddush over wine.

## 3. Why wine

- Wine both **nourishes and gladdens**; used for kiddushin, sheva berakhot, brit. Its own berakhot (Ha-gafen / Al ha-gefen); exempts other drinks.
- Ideally a wine you **enjoy**. No wine: night → **bread**; day → **ḥamar medina** (beer, vodka), else bread.
- Holiness on Shabbat shows in the physical too: joy is a partner, not a threat.

> Line to remember: other religions run from joy to find holiness; we make kiddush over wine.

## 4. Which wines

| Wine | Kiddush? |
|---|---|
| Left exposed for hours, or smells bad | ✗ |
| Grape juice, very sweet wine | ✓ (good alcoholic wine is best) |
| Diluted, still mostly wine | ✓ (majority water: machloket) |
| Mevushal or sugar added | ✓ (Rambam disagrees); use what you prefer |
| White wine | ✓ (SA); Ramban only red → mix a little red in |

## 5. How much wine

- Cup must hold a **revi'it**: 75 ml (R. Ḥayim Naeh was 86); Ḥazon Ish 150 ml (many Ashkenazim le-khatchila).
- Drink a **melo lugmav**: majority of a revi'it, at least 38 ml; most people ~50–55 ml; never more than a revi'it.
- Maker can't drink → a listener can. Be-di'eved, several together making up a cheekful works.

## 6. The kos shel berakha

- **Whole** (no chips); **nicest** available (disposable be-di'eved); **clean** inside and out.
- **Full**: near the top, not overflowing.
- **Pagum** (drunk from) → fix by adding wine (or water to strong wine); be-di'eved use it anyway.
- Take it with both hands, hold in the **right** hand, cupped in the fingers, a **tefaḥ** above the table, eyes on the cup; then give some to your wife.

## 7. Sharing the wine

- One melo lugmav by someone suffices; ideally everyone tastes.
- Family drinking straight from the cup → not pagum. Pouring out after drinking → pagum.
- Solutions: add wine to the cup before pouring; or drink from your own cup; or **pour for everyone before** kiddush (best for big groups / guests).
- Listeners stay silent until the maker drinks (be-di'eved they're yotzei).

## 8. Customs; covering the challah

- Friday night: **stand** (testimony; Arizal; Sephardim, Ḥasidim) or **sit** (most Ashkenazim; place of meal, defines the group). Some stand for Va-yekhulu only. Day: sit.
- Usually the man makes kiddush; several families → one for all ("berov am").
- **Cover the challah**: so bread isn't "shamed" by wine going first (also cover mezonot); or like the manna between layers of dew.

## 9. No eating before kiddush

- From the start of Shabbat: no food or drink, even **water**. Rinsing the mouth and medicine are ok.
- Applies once you light/accept, and certainly after shki'a.
- Day: obligation starts after **Shaḥarit**. Before davening: water, coffee or tea (a bit of sugar) are ok; sick/very hungry → a little food, no kiddush.
- Woman who davens Shaḥarit → may drink before davening. One who says only Birkhot Ha-shaḥar → kiddush right after them.
- Hungry children may eat before kiddush.

## 10. Kiddush be-makom se'uda

- "Call Shabbat delight": kiddush only counts where you **eat**.
- Minimum: a **kezayit** of bread or mezonot; Geonim: a **revi'it** of wine (at night, the maker drinks melo lugmav + a revi'it).
- Kiddush at shul with just juice and a bite → not yotzei, and ate before kiddush.
- "Same place": same room / can see it / intended to move. Ideally two of three.
- Don't wait or go out before eating; over **72 min** with intent to separate → repeat kiddush.
