## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Who did the melakha, how | On Shabbat | After Shabbat |
|---|---|---|
| **Jew, be-mezid** (knowingly) | ✗ everyone | ✓ others; ✗ **he** may never benefit |
| **Jew, be-shogeg** (forgot / didn't know it's assur) | ✗ (most poskim); some allow in sha'at ha-dechak | ✓ everyone, right away |
| **Jew, d'rabbanan, be-shogeg** | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Mitasek** (oblivious, e.g. leaned on a switch) | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Disputed melakha** (cooked "half-cooked" food, heated cold soup) | ✓ be-di'avad | ✓ |
| **Someone who does it regularly for others** | ✗ | ✗ them too (bread, restaurants, filmed games) |
| **Child for an adult / non-Jew for a Jew** | ✗ | ✓ after bi-khdei she-ya'asu |

**Rule of thumb for "benefit":** if you could have done it without the melakha, even with difficulty, you still may (walk the stairs, use the bathroom, keep reading). Turning *off* = removing an obstacle, so it's fine.

**Lifnei iver / mesayei'a:** don't lend a car, don't give driving directions on Shabbat · ✓ invite a non-observant guest for all of Shabbat (with a real place to sleep) · ✓ bar mitzva/brit in shul even if guests drive.

## 1. The basic idea

- The Sages made a fence: don't benefit on Shabbat from **ma'aseh Shabbat** (melakha done on Shabbat). It isn't fitting to enjoy Shabbat from ḥillul Shabbat.
- **Mezid or shogeg** → no Jew may benefit for the rest of that Shabbat. (Some allow shogeg; used in pressing cases, §2.)
    - **Shogeg** = knew what he was doing but forgot it's Shabbat or didn't know it's a melakha.
- **After Shabbat:** everyone may benefit, **except the one who did it be-mezid**, who never may (SA 318:1; §7).
- Recap of related chapters: child for an adult (24:4); non-Jew for a Jew, wait bi-khdei she-ya'asu; non-Jew for himself ✓ (25:2); hinting (25:3); mitzva/loss (25:4).

## 2. The prohibition and what counts as benefit

- Mezid → ✗ for everyone on Shabbat. Shogeg → most say ✗ too (SA 318:1); some permit, and **when needed** we can rely on them (MB 318:7).
- A **non-observant Jew** who knows it's Shabbat and that it might be assur → counts as **mezid**, even for the lenient view.
- **Light turned on**: whatever you could have done, even with difficulty, **without** it, you may do with it:
    - Stairwell you could climb in the dark → may climb (but don't **run** up, that's using the light).
    - Bathroom → may use.
    - ✗ Arranging things you couldn't see to arrange.
    - Already enough light to read with difficulty, someone added a light → keep reading.
- **Turned off** a light knowingly → may sleep there; that's removing an obstacle, not benefiting.
- **Radio/music** turned on by a Jew → don't listen. If leaving the room is a bother, you don't have to; it's against your will. Even if a **non-Jew** turned it on → still don't listen (weekday activity, kavod Shabbat, 22:19).
- **Roommate turned on the heat:** you don't have to leave your room. Try to stop him first; if you can't, have in mind not to benefit, **don't move toward the heater**, stay where you usually are. Unwanted benefit isn't an issur. Opening a window is better (Rema 276:1; MB 11–13).
- **Mitzva by that light** (learning, davening): machloket. Some say mitzvot aren't "benefit" → may rely on it.
    - But **food cooked on Shabbat** → all agree ✗, even for oneg Shabbat (the mitzva *is* the pleasure). Likewise ✗ eating by a light turned on on Shabbat.

## 3. When benefit is allowed: d'rabbanan & mitasek

- **D'rabbanan be-shogeg** → may benefit on Shabbat. D'rabbanan be-mezid → like d'oraita, only after Shabbat (MB 318:3; BHL; Yalkut Yosef).
    - Separated **terumot/ma'asrot/ḥalla** on Shabbat (d'rabbanan, like tikun): shogeg → eat on Shabbat; mezid → after Shabbat (MB 339:25; 22:5).
- **Electric appliance without a heating element** (fan, AC, fridge) turned on be-shogeg → some say it's not d'oraita → may be lenient.
- **Heater coils / incandescent bulb** → definitely d'oraita → ✗ even shogeg. Open a window or leave the room.
- **Mitasek** (no awareness of the act) → may benefit, even d'oraita. Brushed against or leaned on a switch → ✓.
    - Shogeg brought a ḥatat; mitasek didn't. So mitasek is lighter.
    - But turning on the light **out of habit** walking into a room = **shogeg** (the action intended to turn on the light) → ✗.

## 4. Melakha that didn't change the object

- **Carried** from reshut ha-rabim, or food **driven** in a car on Shabbat: the object is unchanged.
    - Rabbeinu Yona, Ritva: not forbidden. Tosafot, Ramban, Rashba: forbidden.
    - **Le-khatchila strict; sha'at ha-dechak may rely on the lenient**, especially if shogeg.
- **Melakha enabling a permitted act:** a hammer fixed on Shabbat may not be used even to crack nuts. But if someone did crack nuts with it → may eat the nuts.
- **Door unlocked forbiddenly** (electronic key card): some say don't enter; others: it just removed an obstacle → **be-di'avad, when needed, lenient**.
- **Fridge light** went on when opened → may take the food (17:9).
- **Automatic door** opened by a non-observant Jew → don't enter (only in sha'at ha-dechak). Opened unintentionally by a passerby → ✓ (17:11).

## 5. Actions under dispute

- Benefit is forbidden only for **clearly** forbidden melakha. If it's a **machloket** (even if we're normally strict), be-di'avad may benefit, since ma'aseh Shabbat is d'rabbanan → safek d'rabbanan le-kula (Pri Megadim; MB 318:2).
- Examples:
    - **Raw meat** cooked → ✗.
    - **Partly cooked** (edible in a pinch) food finished on the fire → halakha says d'oraita (SA 318:4), but some say once edible no bishul (ch. 10) → may eat.
    - **Cold cooked soup** heated on a plata → many say d'oraita, but Rambam permits (10:5–6) → may eat.
    - **Tea bag** with water poured from the urn (kli rishon) → forbidden (10:7–8), but Rashbam/Ramban/Rashba say only putting it *into* a kli rishon is bishul → may drink be-di'avad.

## 6. Power outages

- **Citywide or local outage** → Jewish technicians may restore it: somewhere there are sick people on equipment (pikuaḥ nefesh). Since it's permitted, **everyone may benefit** (17:5).
    - Food on the plata that cooled and reheated, even finished cooking → may eat (cooked permissibly).
- **Small area, clearly no danger** → may not restore. If restored anyway:
    - Light → nothing you couldn't do without it (§2).
    - Food that **re-warmed** → wait until it cools to eat. Food that **finished cooking** → ✗ (RSZ Auerbach in SSK 32 n. 182; 10:3).

## 7. Benefit after Shabbat

- **Shogeg** → he and everyone may benefit right after Shabbat. **Mezid** → **he never**, others after Shabbat (MB 318:5).
    - Cooked on Shabbat be-mezid → he never eats it; the people he cooked for can after Shabbat.
    - Built a house on Shabbat → he never uses it; may **sell** it (MB 318:4).
    - **Laundered be-mezid** → can't wear even after Shabbat. Fix: wash them again during the week (Ben Ish Ḥai, Vayeḥi 19).
- **Someone who regularly does melakha on Shabbat for others** → they **never** benefit (it encourages him):
    - ✗ Bread from a Jew who bakes on Shabbat to sell on Motza'ei Shabbat, even if all kosher.
    - ✗ A restaurant whose Jewish chef cooks on Shabbat for after-Shabbat customers.
    - ✗ Watching games/events **filmed by Jews on Shabbat**.
    - ✗ Produce on Sunday **known for sure** to be picked by Jews on Shabbat.
- **Mixed/uncertain:** milk from a dairy that milks on Shabbat (mixed with other days' milk) → technically ✓ (safek d'rabbanan) (20:4). Same for a paper factory running 24/7.
    - Still, **better to buy from Shabbat-observant companies**. If all Shabbat-keepers united, a temporary ban could be put on them (Yalkut Yosef 318:72–75; Orḥot Shabbat 25:57–61).

## 8. Right after Shabbat

- **News by Jews**: from **half an hour** after Shabbat (time to gather and write it). Right away = benefiting from ma'aseh Shabbat.
- **Other programs**: from **15 minutes** after.
- Technicians who drove to the studio on Shabbat → listeners gain nothing from that → ok.
- ✗ Broadcasts by Jews in **America** (Shabbat there still on for 7–10 more hours). From **Western Europe** → wait about **2 hours**.
- Non-Jewish broadcasts → right away.
- **Buses:** from **half an hour** after Shabbat. If the route is so long that the driver clearly started on Shabbat → machloket (Minḥat Yitzḥak 9:39 forbids; Mishneh Halakhot 7:50 permits). Many say technically ok but **better to be strict** as a fence (Tzitz Eliezer 13:48; SSK 59:9).

## 9. Non-observant Jews and lifnei iver

- "*Lifnei iver lo titen mikhshol*" (Vayikra 19:14): don't cause another to sin. If he'd sin anyway → many say no Torah issur, but there's the d'rabbanan of **mesayei'a** (aiding).
- ✗ Lending a non-observant Jew your **car or radio** for Shabbat.
- ✗ Giving **directions** to a Jew driving on Shabbat, even if he'll drive longer without them (mesayei'a + speaking about forbidden things, 22:9). Politely apologize and explain.
- ✓ Observant **soldier** may leave the bathroom light on before Shabbat even though someone will turn it off. No need to give up his right; and the other would switch it anyway (RSZ Auerbach).
- ✓ **Inviting a non-observant Jew for Shabbat**, even if he'll drive home after dinner, if you **honestly offer him a place to sleep**. Some forbid; may be lenient out of ahavat Yisrael and kiruv. Sometimes better to be strict for educational reasons.
- **Bar mitzva of a non-observant family in shul** where guests will drive:
    - Some forbid (like accepting ḥillul Shabbat); others permit (they'd drive anyway; we'd prefer they walk).
    - In practice ✓ if they respect the shul and don't desecrate Shabbat inside. Still, many say it's better to suggest celebrating the aliya at **Minḥa on a Monday or Thursday** instead.
- **Brit mila on Shabbat** when family will drive → a mohel may do it.

## 10. Websites and vending machines

- **Vending machine** owned by a Jew: mostly Jewish users → **shut it off** for Shabbat (mesayei'a); mostly non-Jews → not needed. Their money is for the product, not Shabbat work (SSK 29:28–29).
- **Websites** for a Jewish audience:
    - Some say take them down. Not required, since visitors can easily sin elsewhere and the owner isn't promoting it on Shabbat.
    - **Commercial**, mostly Jewish customers → he profits from Shabbat activity → should try to disable it; if very hard, not obligatory (some say mesayei'a doesn't apply to those who knowingly sin regularly).
    - Mostly non-Jewish customers → no need. Purchases aren't Shabbat wages: the work was done during the week.
