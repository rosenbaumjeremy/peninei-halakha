## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Way of doing it | Level |
|---|---|
| Normal, intended, constructive, lasting melakha | ✗ Torah (melekhet maḥshevet) |
| With a **shinui** / two people who could each do it alone | ✗ Rabbinic |
| **Mekalkel** (destructive) / not lasting | ✗ Rabbinic |
| **Melakha she-eina tzerikha le-gufah** | ✗ Rabbinic (most), treated seriously |
| **Davar she-eino mitkaven** (unintended, not certain) | ✓ Permitted |
| **Psik reisha** (unintended but certain) | ✗ Forbidden (Torah if you want the result) |
| **Grama** (indirect, unusual) | Only for loss, a mitzva or great need |
| **Shvut di-shvut** | ✓ For a mitzva, minor illness, big loss |

## 1. The Torah's mitzva

- Positive mitzva ("on the seventh day you shall rest") + negative ("do no melakha"). Witnesses + warning → stoning; intentional → karet; unintentional → ḥatat.
- Four melakhot named in the Torah: **Ḥoresh** and **Kotzer** (even food production stops), **Mav'ir** (each melakha counts separately), **Hotza'ah** (even moving without changing anything).
- Melakha = **creative** work, not effort: carrying a needle out is melakha; moving tables isn't. Reheating cooked food isn't; cooking raw is.
- Only **melekhet maḥshevet** is Torah-level: not with a shinui, unintended, not le-gufah, destructive, or temporary.
- "Mountains hanging by a hair" (Ḥagiga 1:8). Food shiur: a dried fig; Ḥoresh/Zore'a/Kotzer/Boneh: any amount.

## 2. The 39 melakhot and toladot

- Learned from the **Mishkan**: "but keep My Shabbatot" (Shemot 31:13). Building the Mikdash doesn't override Shabbat.
- Field/bread (1–11), wool/cloth (12–24), leather/hides (25–31), writing (32–33), building (34–35), fire (36–37), finishing touch (38), carrying (39).
- **Av** vs. **tolada**: both Torah, same punishment. Difference: separate ḥatat for each **av**; one for an av and its toladot.

> Line to remember: we stop building the Mishkan on Shabbat so we remember what all our building is for.

## 3. Shinui and two people

- **Shinui** (ki-le'aḥar yad) → rabbinic: carrying with foot/mouth/elbow, writing with the weak hand, biting nails.
- **Two people**: each could do it alone → rabbinic; neither could → both Torah; one could → he's Torah, the helper rabbinic.
- Safek de-Oraita → strict; safek de-rabbanan → lenient. In need, adding a shinui can turn a Torah doubt into a rabbinic one.

## 4. Why the Sages added decrees

- Fences: medicine (grinding), riding animals / climbing trees (branches), terumot, musical instruments (fixing), amira le-akum, benefiting from melakha done on Shabbat.
- Spirit of Shabbat: no weekday business talk, no inspecting fields, no preparing for the week, no rushing, no measuring, **muktzeh**.
- Without a ban on shinui and muktzeh, people would never really **rest** (Ramban).
- Authority from the Torah: "make a safeguard for My safeguard" (Yevamot 21a); "do not turn aside" (Devarim 17:11). Given to the Sanhedrin.

## 5. Davar she-eino mitkaven, psik reisha

- **Not intended and not certain** → permitted: dragging a bench (maybe furrows), walking on grass.
- **Certain** → psik reisha → forbidden. Torah level only if he **wants** the result; otherwise rabbinic.
- Closing a door with a deer inside = trapping. "Cut off its head and it won't die?" (Shabbat 75a).
- Applies to all Torah prohibitions, not just Shabbat.

## 6. Melakha she-eina tzerikha le-gufah

- Done intentionally, but not for the melakha's own purpose: putting out a candle to save oil; digging for the dirt, not the hole.
- R. Shimon: rabbinic; R. Yehuda: Torah.
- Most poskim: **rabbinic**, but treated more seriously than other rabbinic bans (Rambam is strict).

## 7. Mekalkel (destructive)

- "All who destroy are exempt" (m. Shabbat 13:3): **patur** = not punished, still rabbinically forbidden.
- Tearing to sew → Torah; just tearing → rabbinic. Taking apart to rebuild → Torah; just destroying → rabbinic. Erasing to rewrite → Torah.
- Lighting to destroy → rabbinic. Killing ants/mosquitoes → rabbinic (nothing constructive).

## 8. Kiyum (lasting)

- Pen/pencil on paper → Torah. Fruit juice, a leaf, sand, window condensation → rabbinic.
- Lasting knot → Torah; temporary → rabbinic; single knot/bow → permitted.
- Permanent hook → Torah; temporary → rabbinic.

## 9. Grama

- "Don't **do** melakha": indirect causing isn't Torah-level.
- Only for **loss, a mitzva or great need**: water-filled jugs around a spreading fire (SA 334:22; Rema).
- Not grama if that's the **normal** way: winnowing in the wind, putting a pot on the fire → Torah.
- Ko'aḥ rishon (close / immediate) → direct; ko'aḥ sheni (distant / delayed) → grama.

## 10. Children, non-Jews, animals

- "You, your son, your daughter, your servant, your ox…": a Torah duty that children not do melakha (beyond ḥinukh; ch. 24).
- Converted eved → keeps Shabbat. Non-converted servant → may work for himself, not for the Jew ("that the son of your maidservant may rest").
- **Amira le-akum** → rabbinic (exceptions: §11–12, ch. 25).
- Animals must rest (ch. 20); **tools** needn't → may lend to a non-Jew (Beit Hillel).

## 11. Shvut di-shvut

- Itur: amira le-akum ok for any mitzva. Rambam and most: not a Torah melakha; but a **rabbinic** one via a non-Jew (= shvut di-shvut) ok for a mitzva.
- Brit, no knife: non-Jew may carry it through a **karmelit**, or through reshut ha-rabim with a **shinui**.
- Most: a Jew himself may do a shvut di-shvut for a mitzva (e.g., carry with a shinui through a karmelit).
- Also for **minor illness** (SA 307:5) and **big monetary loss**. Not as routine.

## 12. Shvut for a great or public mitzva

- Communal need: rely on Itur → ask a non-Jew to fix a broken **eruv**, even a Torah melakha (MB 276:25).
- No non-Jew and many will carry → custom: a Jew may fix it (with a bow if possible).
- **Kevod ha-briyot**: in the bathroom with nothing → muktzeh or tearing with a shinui allowed.
- **Yishuv Eretz Yisrael**: buying a house from a non-Jew in Israel → may ask him to write and register the contract on Shabbat, even for 4 amot (SA 306:11).
