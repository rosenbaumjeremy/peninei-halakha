## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Concept | The rule |
|---|---|
| **Teḥum** | Mekom shevita + **2,000 amot (~912 m)** each way |
| **Mekom shevita in a field** | 4 × 4 amot (you lying down, arms and legs stretched) |
| **In a city** | The whole built-up area (houses ≤ 32 m apart, or all within a wall/eruv) |
| **Shape** | Always **squared** (by the compass directions, or the city's own layout) → extra space in the corners |
| **Neighborhoods** | ≤ 64 m apart → one area; more → each gets its own teḥum |
| **Left the teḥum** | Lose it; now only **4 amot** (unless shogeg/forced and inside an enclosure; or saving lives) |
| **Eruv teḥumin** | Food for 2 meals (6 eggs' volume) or just being there at bein ha-shmashot → new mekom shevita; gain one way, lose the other. Only for a mitzva (be-di'avad works anyway) |
| **Max reach with an eruv teḥumin** | ~4,000 amot, up to ~5,600 by aiming the square's diagonal |

**D'oraita or d'rabbanan?** Rambam & Smag: Torah law beyond **12 mil** (~11 km) ("*al yetze ish mi-mekomo*", Shemot 16:29). Ramban, Rosh, Rashba and most Rishonim: all teḥumin are d'rabbanan.

## 1. General principles

- **Why:** traveling comes from need (making a living, meeting needs). Shabbat is for resting from that, contemplating creation's inner perfection, thanking Hashem for choosing us and giving the Torah, delighting in Him. So the Sages set **boundaries**.
- **Teḥum** = **mekom shevita** + **2,000 amot** (≈ 2,000 average steps ≈ **912 m**) in each direction.
    - **Field:** mekom shevita = **4 amot square** (lying with arms/legs outstretched).
    - **City/town:** the whole settled area is one "place" → 2,000 amot from its edge (§8).
- **Source:** Rambam & Smag: d'oraita only beyond **12 mil** (24,000 amot ≈ 11 km, the size of the camp in the desert; "*al yetze ish mi-mekomo ba-yom ha-shevi'i*", Shemot 16:29). Ramban, Rosh, Rashba, most Rishonim: that verse is about carrying; teḥumin are **all d'rabbanan**.
- **Eruv teḥumin** extends the teḥum in one direction for a mitzva (a wedding, a shiur) (§12–14).
- Leave the teḥum → lose it, stay within **4 amot** (§11).

## 2. Squaring the teḥum

- Mekom shevita and teḥum are **squares**. In a field: the 4-ama circle is boxed into a 4-ama square along the compass directions (adds the corners). A round city is boxed into a square/rectangle. Then 2,000 amot out in each direction, and squared again → more corners (Mishna Eruvin 53a).
- **Source:** the Levite cities: "2,000 amot east… south… west… north, with the city in the middle" (Bamidbar 35:5).
- **Practical reason:** a circle would need measurements from every point; a square needs only four.
- **Spiritual reason:** a circle = infinite, cyclical (our habits, thoughts, even our limbs), hard to turn into action. A **square** = a finite frame that lets the infinite be absorbed and realized. Teḥum Shabbat is that frame for Shabbat's holiness, and that's why the **Levites'** cities, whose job is revealing emuna, have square boundaries.

## 3. Teḥum is individual

- Each person's teḥum depends on **where he is**. Two neighbors (not in a city) 1,000 amot apart → overlapping but different teḥumin.
- Applies to one's **animals and belongings** too, and to non-Jews' things.
    - I carried my tallit to the edge of my teḥum; a friend with a different teḥum borrows it → he can't take it past **my** teḥum (SA 397:3).
    - **Jointly owned** → only where both teḥumin overlap (SA 397:9).

## 4. Spending Shabbat in or outside a city

- **In a city** (Jewish or not): all contiguous built-up area = one place; 2,000 amot from its edge. Spread-out houses enclosed by a **wall or eruv** → also one place (§8).
- That's for someone inside the city or its squared area.
- **In a field near a city** → 2,000 amot from his spot, period. If it ends in the middle of the city, his teḥum ends there; the city doesn't count as his 4 amot.

## 5. Measuring: then and now

- **Rope of exactly 50 amot** (longer sags → too short; shorter is pulled too tight → too long) (Eruvin 57b).
- Hold it at **chest height** on both ends (SA 399:1–3).
- **Valley**: one person on each side, measure through the air. **Hill**: tall poles so the rope passes over. Wider than 50 amot → measure with a **4-ama rope**, upper person at his feet, lower at his chest. Too steep → estimate. A drop < 4 amot wide → ignore (SA 399:4–5).
- Only an **expert** measures. Two experts disagree → follow the **larger** teḥum (d'rabbanan → lenient) (SA 399:7–9).
- Teḥum never measured, need to go for a mitzva → walk **2,000 medium strides** (SA 397:2; MB 5).
- **Today:** use **aerial maps / GPS**. The Sages' methods aimed at precision with minimal bother; now we have better tools, so use them.

## 6. Compass directions and squaring a city

- City squared along the **four compass directions** (SA 398:3). If the city's own corners suggest another orientation → follow them (SA 398:1).
- **Machloket** when the shape suggests a non-compass orientation:
    - Some: only with a compelling reason (SAH 398:3; Ḥayei Adam 76:14).
    - **Most:** if the shape clearly suggests it, follow it. In doubt → the local rabbinate decides.
    - Clear cases: one side of the city is **straight end to end**; the perimeter has a **right angle** (follow the angle).
- **In a field** (4-ama mekom shevita) → you may orient your square **any way you like**, and the 2,000-ama teḥum follows (§12).

## 7. When we don't square a city

- Squaring adds corners, but sometimes the resulting square would swallow too much empty land: an **L-shaped** or **bow-shaped** city.
- **Rule:** if there are **4,000 amot** between the two ends of the shape, that part isn't squared.

## 8. Connecting settled areas

- Houses ≤ a **karpif** apart (~**32 m**) → one area. Farther → each house has its own teḥum.
- A house sticking out of line but ≤ 32 m from the next → the rectangle expands to include it, and the next, and so on, even for days' walking.
- **Neighborhoods** more than **two karpifs (64 m)** apart → separate towns, each squared separately. ≤ 64 m → squared together.
- A "neighborhood" needs **50 residents**, or three courtyards each joining two homes, or six homes each with a yard (Eruvin 60a; MB 398:38; Ḥazon Ish 110:19).
- **Wall or eruv** around it → all one area, even if the wall is far from the last house and the neighborhoods are far apart.

## 9. Overlapping squares

- Rectangles of two cities **overlap** (even without a joint eruv) → they're connected; draw one rectangle around both; everyone gets 2,000 amot beyond it.
- But if the corners of the two rectangles are more than **4,000 amot** apart → don't square together (§7); each city gets its own 2,000 amot.

## 10. Large cities

- A **highway** or open area (**park**) > **64 m** wide that **cuts the whole city** → splits it into two for teḥum purposes.
- **Ayalon Highway** in Tel Aviv: seemingly splits it, but:
    1. An **eruv** around Tel Aviv and neighbors unites it.
    2. If each half's rectangle **overlaps** the other → joined (§9).
    3. A highway/park meant for **all residents** is part of the city.
- Some reject all three. **Main view: lenient.** But proper to be strict not to go more than **12 mil** beyond the highway (in case 12 mil is d'oraita, §1).

## 11. Leaving the teḥum; things from outside it

- Went out, **knowingly or not** → lose the 2,000 amot; only **4 amot** (SA 405:1).
    - Bathroom needs: may go somewhere private, then move away from the smell to daven/make berakhot, then stay within 4 amot there (SA 406:1).
- Reached an **enclosed area** (wall/eruv): **be-mezid** → still 4 amot, even inside a house. **Shogeg or forced** → may walk the whole enclosure (SA 405:6).
- Left to **save a life** → after finishing, 2,000 amot from there; if it overlaps his original teḥum, he may go home and keeps it (Eruvin 44b). Sometimes may return regardless (27:10).
- **Plane** landing unexpectedly on Shabbat:
    - Fenced airport (often with sleeping areas) → the whole airport = his 4 amot; plus 2,000 amot beyond.
    - **Not fenced** → mekom shevita set at **touchdown**. Taxiing 2,000+ amot → he's out → stays **on the plane** till after Shabbat. Crew/security make him leave, or he needs the bathroom → may; then inside an enclosure he can move freely (forced) (SA 405:6).
    - Flight was **for a mitzva** → even if it taxied a km and no fence, 2,000 amot from the plane door (SA 248:4; MB 32).
- **Ship** docking on Shabbat → may disembark and walk 2,000 amot (teḥum didn't apply while > 10 tefaḥim above the sea floor; it's set on stepping ashore). Fenced port → 2,000 beyond it (SA 404:1).
- **Returned** into his teḥum: if he **left** shogeg/forced and came back the same way → keeps his teḥum (SA 406:1). If he **left be-mezid**, even returning shogeg → loses the teḥum, but may walk the whole city (SA 405:8).
- **Possessions** also can't go beyond the teḥum. Fruit taken out **shogeg** → may eat (not carry > 4 amot); **mezid** → may not eat (SA 405:9; MB 52).
- **Non-Jew brought fruit from outside the teḥum**: for himself/another non-Jew → a Jew may eat; carry only 4 amot, unless brought into a house/enclosure. **For a Jew** → that Jew and his household wait **bi-khdei she-yavi'u** after Shabbat (SA 325:8).

## 12. Eruv teḥumin

- Moves your **mekom shevita** to where the eruv is → it "merges" the old and new teḥum.
- **Gain one way, lose the other:** eruv 2,000 amot **east** → may go 4,000 amot east, but **0 west**.
- **Two ways:**
    1. **Be there in person** at **bein ha-shmashot**, intending it as your place. No words needed. Just hiking there without intending → home remains your place (SA 409:7; MB 29).
    2. **Food for two meals** placed there, with a **berakha** and declaration (§13).
- Only for a **mitzva** (shiur, mitzva celebration). Other reasons → still works **be-di'avad** (SA 415:1).
- Must be placed **within 2,000 amot of home**, so home is inside the new teḥum. Otherwise it doesn't work.
- **Up to 5,600 amot:** the eruv's spot is temporary (unlike a city), so you can **orient the square** so its **diagonal** points where you want → gain the corners.

## 13. Placing the eruv teḥumin; the berakha

- **Two meals' worth** per person: bread **6 eggs (~300 ml)** (some: 8). A spread/side eaten with bread → enough for 6 eggs of bread (SA 409:7).
    - For many people and less bulk: **olive oil, chocolate spread, peanut butter**; **vinegar** a revi'it (75 ml) (dip for two meals' vegetables) (MB 386:35; 409:36).
    - Drinks: **two revi'iyot (150 ml)** per person (SA 386:6). ✗ Salt or water (Eruvin 26a).
- The food must **belong to the user**. For several people → a third person lifts it to acquire for all (SA 413:1).
- **Eaten before bein ha-shmashot** → doesn't work. After → fine (Rema 394:2).
- Left where it couldn't be reached at bein ha-shmashot without a d'oraita (e.g., rolling away a boulder) → doesn't work (SA 394:3; 409:3–4).
- **Berakha:** "*… ve-tzivanu al mitzvat eruv*." **Declaration:** "With this eruv it will be permitted for me to walk 2,000 amot from this place in every direction." Be-di'avad "this is an eruv" is enough; **saying nothing** → no eruv (SA 415:4; MB 15).
    - For several people → **name them**, and food for each.
    - For all year → add "**for all Shabbatot of the year**"; works as long as it exists (MB 16).
- **Shali'aḥ** may place it (not a minor, a non-Jew, or one who denies eruv). He must say the berakha/declaration; if he said nothing → invalid, unless the owner said "With the eruv my shali'aḥ is placing…" (SA 409:8; BHL).
- Can't make one for someone **without his knowledge**.
- **Family:** for minor kids → binding. For household members over bar/bat mitzva → works, unless they object on hearing. A child under 6 follows his **mother** (SA 414:1–2).

## 14. Conditional eruv; an eruv for the whole town

- **Conditional:** two shiurim, one east and one west, undecided → place an eruv in each and decide on Shabbat (home, east, or west). Once decided, or once he starts walking according to one teḥum, he's bound (Eruvin 82a; SA 413:1; MB 8).
- **For all local residents:** "With this eruv all local residents and their guests may walk 2,000 amot from this place in every direction." Anyone who **knew about it before Shabbat** can decide on Shabbat to use it; one who didn't know → can't (SA 413:1).
- **Too many people** to put food for each: if he knows no more than **20** will use it → food for 20, declare "for anyone who wishes… for all Shabbatot of the year." Users must know about it before that Shabbat (BHL).
- **Berakha:** if someone will surely use it → yes; if uncertain → no berakha.
- **Unconditional** eruv → his teḥum is set by the eruv; he can't switch back to his home's teḥum.
