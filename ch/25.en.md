## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Situation | Ask him? | Benefit from it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-Jew does melakha for himself** (or another non-Jew) | – | ✓ (unless it's something new that was muktzeh: picked fruit, fish, milk) |
| **For a Jew, Torah melakha** | ✗ | ✗ for anyone on Shabbat; after Shabbat wait *bi-khdei she-ya'asu* |
| **For a Jew, d'rabbanan melakha** | ✗ (normally) | ✗ for that Jew; ✓ for other Jews |
| **Mitzva / great need / loss** | ✓ d'rabbanan only (shvut di-shvut) | ✓ |
| **Sick (even not dangerously) or a needy child** | ✓ even d'oraita | ✓ |
| **Communal mitzva / yishuv Eretz Yisrael / huge loss** | ✓ even d'oraita | ✓ |

**How to "ask" when you can't ask:** describe the facts ("It's hard to read with this light"), don't command ("Do me a favor…") and don't point. Then you may use only what you could have managed without it; turning *off* (dark, oven) is always fine to benefit from.

**Workers:** wage-earner ✗ · contractor (kablanut) ✓ if no marit ayin and no Shabbat deadline · sharecropper / renter ✓ (unless it's known as a Jewish place) · stock in non-Jewish companies ✓.

## 1. Amira le-nokhri: the basic prohibition

- Shabbat belongs only to the Jewish people: "*ki ot hi beini u-veineikhem*" (Shemot 31:13). A non-Jew who makes himself a Shabbat is "*ḥayav mita*" (San. 58b; Rambam, Melakhim 10:9). So a non-Jew may do melakha.
- But the Sages forbade **asking** a non-Jew to do melakha for a Jew, **even d'rabbanan** melakha. Asmakhta: "*kol melakha **lo ye'aseh** bahem*" (Shemot 12:16), passive: not by you, your friend, or a non-Jew (Mekhilta).
- **With a Jew's property**, can't ask even "for yourself": ✗ "Turn on the light for yourself", ✗ "Cook my food for yourself". With **his own** meat → may tell him to cook it for himself (SA 307:21; MB 73).
- **Benefit:** a Jew may not benefit from melakha a non-Jew did **for a Jew** (SA 276:1). Neighbor turned on our lights → no Jew may use that light.
    - After Shabbat: wait **bi-khdei she-ya'asu**, the time it would take to do it after Shabbat (fruit picked, fish caught) (SA 325:5–6).
    - **D'rabbanan melakha** done for a Jew → only **that** Jew can't benefit on Shabbat; others can. After Shabbat + bi-khdei she-ya'asu, he can too (SA 325:8; MB 41).

## 2. A non-Jew's melakha for himself

- Did it for himself or another non-Jew → a Jew may benefit. He turned on a light to read → I can use it (SA 276:2).
- **For both himself and the Jew** → ok, since he needed it anyway (Ḥayei Adam; AHS 276:8; BHL leans this way, against MA).
- **Setting it up:** can't ask the neighbor to turn on the light, but can **invite him to eat**. He sees it's dark and turns it on for himself → I can use it (AHS 276:9).
- **Household help:** send him to wash dishes in a dark kitchen; he'll turn on the light for himself. Can even ask him to leave it on afterwards (MB 276:27; SSK 30:57).
- ✗ Asking him to come along on a dark walk so he'll use a flashlight "for himself": obviously for me (SA 276:3).
- **Leftover hot water** he heated: ✗ if he knows me (maybe he added some for me, or will next time); ✓ if he doesn't know me (SA 325:11; MB 66).
- **Fruit picked, fish caught, cows milked** for himself → ✗ on Shabbat even if he's a stranger: they weren't usable at the start of Shabbat → **muktzeh** (SA 325:5).

## 3. Using melakha done for a Jew; hinting

- **Extra light:** the room was lit enough to eat and wash dishes but not to read, and a non-Jew added a light → may eat and wash dishes; ✗ read. Only what was already possible (SA 276:4; MB 32).
- **Turning off a light** so a Jew can sleep → may sleep there. Benefit from *absence* of light isn't benefit from the melakha.
- Still, can't **ask** him to turn it on or off. Can **hint**, carefully:
    - ✗ A hint that's really a command: "Do me a favor, there's not enough light"; **pointing** at the light (Ḥayei Adam 62:2); "Do what needs to be done"; "Whoever turns it off won't lose out."
    - ✓ A **description**: "It's hard for me to read/clean with such dim light"; "It's hard for me to sleep with the light on"; "I have no toilet paper" (uncut paper is usable in a pinch, so no problem of benefit); "What a waste of electricity" (oven left on).
- **Two d'rabbanan issurim to avoid:**
    1. Telling him to do melakha (→ describe, don't ask).
    2. Benefiting when it lets me do something I **couldn't** do before.
- These are fine **le-khatchila**. If they don't help → for a great need or mitzva, may ask for a d'rabbanan melakha, sometimes even d'oraita (§4–5).

## 4. For a mitzva or a great need

- **D'rabbanan melakha** for great need (suffering, loss) or a mitzva → may ask. It's a **shvut di-shvut** (the act is d'rabbanan + amira le-nokhri is d'rabbanan).
- **D'oraita melakha** → ✗, even for a mitzva. Except: **yishuv Eretz Yisrael**, a **pressing communal** mitzva need, or a **very large loss** (the Sages feared he'd desecrate Shabbat himself out of distress) (9:11–12; 16:5).
- Examples:
    - Take a **shofar** down from a tree for Rosh Ha-shana (tree use is d'rabbanan) (SA 307:7).
    - Bring **wine for kiddush** or **siddurim** to shul through a **karmelit** (MB 325:60). Food **essential** to the Shabbat meals too; not extras (MB 325:62).
    - Move **muktzeh** to prevent loss: gather scattered money; bring cement bags in from the rain (SA 307:19).
    - **Oil a squeaky door** that keeps you up (d'rabbanan; the door works anyway).
    - **Spray mosquitoes** that won't let you sleep (killing not for the body = d'rabbanan, 20:8).
- **Safek** whether d'oraita or d'rabbanan → may ask for a mitzva/great need (safek d'rabbanan le-kula).
- **Lights went out in shul / beit midrash:** ask him to turn on with a **shinui**; if impossible and there's a pressing communal need, even without. Best: give him something to eat there so he turns it on for himself.

## 5. For the sick and the suffering (and air conditioning)

- **Choleh she-ein bo sakana** → may ask a non-Jew even for **d'oraita** melakha. (Dangerously ill → a Jew himself desecrates Shabbat; SA 328:17. Details in ch. 27–28.)
- **A child who really needs something** = sick → non-Jew may cook for him, or turn on a light for kids terrified of the dark (Rema 276:1; 24:6).
- **The "Shabbos goy":** in cold Europe the stove went out by Shabbat morning; in extreme cold "everyone is sick," so it was allowed to have a non-Jew relight it (SA 276:5).
    - Today, with electric/gas heat, **no basis for a regular Shabbos goy**. Only if the heat happened to go off and it's **very cold** (even with no kids). With kids who really need it → even if not extremely cold (SSK 23:28).
- **Suffering but not sick** → d'rabbanan only.
- **Air conditioner on a hot day:**
    - Some say turning it on is d'rabbanan → may ask, for oneg Shabbat and real suffering.
    - Others say d'oraita (17:2) → so ask him to use a **shinui** (e.g., press the button with a teaspoon) → shvut di-shvut.
    - **Too cold** → may ask him to turn it off. Better: say "it's too cold" and let him figure it out; if he doesn't, can tell him directly.

## 6. Employees, sharecroppers, renters

- ✗ Hiring a non-Jew to work for me on Shabbat (field, factory, store): can't ask him to do what I can't do.
- ✓ Hiring him to **serve food or wash dishes** on Shabbat (things I may do myself). Don't even need to bundle the pay into weekday wages (22:14).
- Worker did work for me on Shabbat → no benefit on Shabbat; after Shabbat + bi-khdei she-ya'asu. **Public work** like building a house → the Sages forbade **living** there ever; may sell it to another Jew; in great need, may live there (SA 244:3–4; MB 19–20; SA 325:14).
- **Sharecropper (aris)** → ✓ may work Shabbat; he works for his share. So a Jewish store/factory owner may let a non-Jew run it on Shabbat for a **percentage**.
- **Rental** for a fixed fee (e.g., a business abroad) → ✓; he's working for himself. Must be rented **weekly/monthly/yearly**, not just for Shabbat.

## 7. Marit ayin and causing other Jews to sin

- Everything in §6 only where there's **no marit ayin**. A place **known to be Jewish-owned** that's open on Shabbat → people will think he hired workers → ✗ renting or sharecropping.
- That's the Gemara's **bathhouse**: usually run by day-laborers, so it looked like the Jew hired them. ✓ If it was **publicized** that it's rented, or local custom is to run them by sharecropping (SA 243:1–2).
- **Store:** same. Publicized rental → ✓. Non-observant Jews might shop there on Shabbat → still ok, they could buy elsewhere. But if **most customers are Jews** and it breaches Shabbat → ✗ (Tzitz Eliezer 13:39).

## 8. Contractors (kablanut)

- **Kablan** = agreed job, agreed price, agreed deadline, any days he likes. If he chooses to work Shabbat, it's for himself → ✓ (SA 244:1).
    - Tailor, shoemaker: ✓. ✗ Asking on Friday to have it ready by Motza'ei Shabbat (forces Shabbat work).
    - **Car to a non-Jewish garage on Friday** → ✓ even if he might fix it Shabbat; may pick it up right after Shabbat. ✗ A deadline that needs Shabbat work (MB 244:2).
- ✗ If it's **obvious** the work is for a Jew, e.g., in the Jew's home → marit ayin.
- **Building a house:** the Sages said don't let a non-Jewish contractor build on Shabbat (SA 244:1).
    - Today people assume workers are a contractor's, so logically could be lenient. But **custom is strict** (Ran: people may still suspect; and the owner may come supervise and desecrate Shabbat).
    - **Great need** (otherwise the building won't get finished) → may be lenient **with a rav's ruling**.

## 9. Partnerships, stocks, banks

- **Jew + non-Jew partners:** agree at the start that Shabbat's profit is all the non-Jew's and a weekday's is all the Jew's; split the rest. If daily profits are similar or untracked, split everything evenly (it's assumed each took "his" day) (SA 245:1; MB 5–6; Igrot Moshe OḤ 2:65). Only if the partnership is known, or such partnerships are common.
- **Stocks** in a non-Jewish company open on Shabbat → ✓; they work for their own profit (SA 245:4). Even if some Jewish employees desecrate Shabbat, I'm not responsible.
    - ✗ If the company is **run by Jews** or most work on Shabbat is done by Jews → I'd be a partner in ḥillul Shabbat.
- **Jewish bank** with non-Jewish clients → may arrange for a non-Jewish bank to handle those clients' transactions on Shabbat (it profits per transaction → working for itself) (Melamed Le-ho'il 1:33).
