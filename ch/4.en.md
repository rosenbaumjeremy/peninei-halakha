## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why light? | Kavod Shabbat, oneg Shabbat, shalom bayit (rabbinic mitzva) |
| Where? | Mainly where you **eat**; some light in every room in use |
| Who? | Everyone is obligated; the **wife** takes precedence and covers the household |
| How many? | Law: one; custom: **two** (Zakhor + Shamor); guests: two |
| Berakha before or after? | Most women: light, cover eyes, berakha, look. SA / some Sephardim: berakha first. Men: berakha first |
| Electric light? | Incandescent bulb works if needed; **not** fluorescent |
| Poor and must choose | Bread → candle → wine |

## 1. The mitzva

- Rabbinic mitzva, three reasons: **kavod** (a feast without light isn't a feast), **oneg** (see your food), **shalom bayit** (don't trip and get angry).
- So important that a poor person should even go collecting for it (SA 263:2).
- Little money: **bread** first, then **candle**, then **wine** (kiddush can be on bread) (Shabbat 23b).
- Meticulous about candles → **children who are talmidei ḥakhamim** (Shabbat 23b) → women pray for this after lighting.

> Line to remember: in the dark everything seems to be against you; light a candle and you see it's all in its place.

## 2. Where to light and who lights

- Light where people will be Friday night; the berakha is on the candles where you **eat** (Rema 263:10). Electric/street light in other rooms is enough.
- Candles must last through the **meal**; ideally some light until bedtime / all night.
- Everyone is obligated; the **wife** comes first and covers the household. Running late near shki'a → better that the husband or a child lights.
- Wife away → the **husband** lights with a berakha (may ask a daughter to light for him).
- Girls lighting with a berakha alongside their mother: **Chabad** custom; most communities only the mother. Follow family custom.

## 3. What to light with, and how many

- Wick must draw the oil well; otherwise the flame flickers and you might fix it (Shabbat ch. 2).
- **Olive oil** is best (SA 264:6); most use paraffin candles today.
- Law: **one** candle. Custom: **two** (Zakhor, Shamor). Others: one per family member, 7, or 10. As a guest: two.
- Forgot once → custom to add a candle every week (Rema 263:1), but only if there was **no light at all**.

## 4. The berakha

| Custom | Order |
|---|---|
| SA; some Sephardim | Berakha → light |
| Ashkenazim (Rema), Ḥida, Ben Ish Ḥai, Morocco, Iraq | Light → cover eyes → berakha → look |
| Single man / widower | Berakha → light (men accept Shabbat later) |

- "…**lehadlik ner shel Shabbat**." Reason for lighting first: the berakha accepts Shabbat (Behag).
- Each woman follows her **mother's** family custom.

## 5. Electric lights

- An **incandescent** bulb (filament = wick) works when needed; most poskim agree. Candles still preferred.
- Ideal: turn electric lights off a few minutes before, then turn them on and light the candles right away, with both in mind for the berakha.
- Lights already on → may still light with a berakha.

## 6. Two families, hotels

- Guest lighting at the same table: SA → no berakha; Rema and most → berakha (extra light).
- Easy solution: hostess at the table, guest in the **kitchen or her room** with a berakha.
- Separate guest apartment → light there; someone must **see** the candles on Shabbat or the berakha was in vain.
- **Hotel**: all light in the dining room (Ashkenazim all with a berakha; many Sephardim only the first). Or: turn on an incandescent bulb in the room with a berakha. **Fluorescent → no berakha.**

## 7. Away from home

- Two parts: light in the **place**, and the **person's** obligation (that's why a candle lit before Shabbat is blown out and relit).
- Man eating and sleeping by a family → covered by the hostess.
- Eats there, sleeps elsewhere: single who lights at home weekly → light where he sleeps; lives with parents or married → not obligated. Nice: give the hostess a **coin** to share in her candles.
- Eating **alone** in a room → light with a berakha. Soldiers → make sure someone lights in the base dining room.
- Women away customarily light themselves (see §6 on where).

## 8. Dormitories and hospitals

- **Yeshiva boys**: independent obligation; one lights in the dining room for all; some light in the rooms (hall light is enough).
- **Women's dorm**: one in the dining room, and each may light in her room with a berakha; if not allowed, rely on the dining room (or an electric bulb).
- **Hospital**: covered by the dining room; a woman who always lights may light in her room, or the dining room / hallway.
