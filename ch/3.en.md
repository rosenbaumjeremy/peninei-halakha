## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Time | Status |
|---|---|
| Before candle-lighting / accepting Shabbat | ✓ Weekday |
| **Tosefet Shabbat** (accepted, before shki'a) | ✗ Torah melakhot; rabbinic ones ok for a mitzva / Shabbat need / great need |
| **Shki'a → tzeit** (bein ha-shmashot) | ✗ Safek → treated as Shabbat (safek de-Oraita le-ḥumra) |
| Motza'ei Shabbat until the listed time | ✗ Still Shabbat (listed times include tosefet) |

**Also:** earliest acceptance = 1¼ seasonal hours before shki'a · women accept with candles · men accept with "Bo'i kalla" or a declaration before shki'a.

## 1. Shabbat times

- "Evening and morning": the Jewish day starts at **night**. Darkness first, then light, like our history.
- Doubt: does night start at **sunset** (shki'a) or when three medium **stars** appear (tzeit)? In Israel about 20 minutes apart.
- That in-between time is **bein ha-shmashot**. Torah doubt → strict: Shabbat starts at shki'a and ends at tzeit.

> Line to remember: for Israel night comes before day: first the questions, then the light.

## 2. Tosefet Shabbat

- Mitzva (most say **Torah**) to add a bit of weekday onto Shabbat at the start and the end (SA 261:2).
- Like an honored guest: go out to greet it, walk it out when it leaves.
- Candle-lighting customs: **Jerusalem 40** min, **Haifa 30**, **Tel Aviv** and most places **20** before shki'a.
- Men davening Minḥa late must still accept a few minutes **before shki'a**.
- Earliest: **1¼ seasonal hours** before shki'a (plag ha-minḥa) (SA 263:4).
- End: three **small** stars together (SA 293:2). Today use the calendar time, which already includes tosefet.

## 3. How to accept it

- Say "I hereby accept the sanctity of Shabbat"; some say even thinking it works (MB 261:21).
- **Women** accept with the berakha on the candles. Most allow lighting with a **condition** (not accepting yet, e.g. to drive to shul), but better to accept then.
- **Men**: once at "Bo'i kalla." Today Lekha Dodi often ends after shki'a → the gabbai should announce "Bo'i kalla" after Minḥa, or each person says it to himself, even during the ḥazara.

## 4. After accepting Shabbat

- All **Torah** melakhot forbidden.
- **Rabbinic** ones allowed for a mitzva, a Shabbat need or great need (e.g., tithing produce he forgot).
- May ask a Jew who hasn't accepted yet to do melakha: a wife who lit can ask her husband (before he accepts) to turn on a light (SA 263:17).
- Same on Motza'ei Shabbat: one who hasn't made havdala can ask one who has.

## 5. Minḥa after accepting Shabbat?

| View | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Strict (SA 263:15; MB) | ✗ No weekday Minḥa after accepting → daven Ma'ariv twice (tashlumin). Women: Minḥa before lighting |
| Lenient (Tzitz Eliezer, Minḥat Yitzḥak) | ✓ Tosefet is about melakha, not prayer; only not if accepted with the congregation |
| Yabi'a Omer | Accept only **in thought**, then daven Minḥa |

- Common custom: shki'a approaching → accept (out loud or in thought), then daven weekday Minḥa. Women be-di'eved may daven Minḥa after lighting.
- Best: if the minyan's Minḥa runs late, daven Minḥa **alone** earlier and accept before shki'a (tosefet is Torah, minyan is rabbinic) — or accept during the ḥazara if sure you can.
