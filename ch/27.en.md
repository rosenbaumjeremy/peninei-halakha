## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Is it dangerous?** | Whatever doctors (or normal people) would rush to treat → yes. Unsure → ask a doctor/medic. Patient says "I'm in danger" → believe him (unless clearly a hypochondriac) |
| **Who does the melakha?** | An **adult Jew**, ideally the most respected one present. Non-Jew/child only if it causes **zero** delay |
| **Shinui?** | Only if it won't slow anything down. Driving to the hospital: drive normally |
| **For whom?** | Anyone: a shoteh, a dying person, a fetus, a premature baby, a non-Jew |
| **After the emergency** | Turn off/lock the car with a shinui; staff may go home with a non-Jewish driver; discharged patients **may not** drive home |
| **Which hospital?** | The closest, unless there's a real medical reason |
| **Who comes along?** | One chaperone for a woman in labor (husband / mother / doula) |
| **War & security** | Defensive war, deterrence, routine security, retrieving bodies → yes. Routine times: minimize, use shinui |
| **Do I have to give something up to reduce ḥillul Shabbat?** | No (rest, property, gardens, the eruv) |

> **Line to remember:** "*Va-ḥai bahem*", *ve-lo she-yamut bahem*. Asking a rav first in a real emergency is not piety; it's bloodshed.

## 1. Principles of pikuaḥ nefesh

- "*Asher ya'aseh otam ha-adam va-ḥai bahem*" (Vayikra 18:5) → "and not die by them" (Yoma 85b).
- Desecrate Shabbat **even if the chance of success is slim**: a medicine that helps only sometimes, even experimental. But not for something with **no real reason** to think it helps (MA 328:1).
- **Safek** → still desecrate. Building collapsed, don't know if anyone's inside or alive → clear the rubble (**mefakḥin et ha-gal**, SA 329:2–5).
- Even a failed rescue gets reward; several people who drove to get the same medicine all get reward (Menaḥot 64a; SA 328:15).
- **Prepare beforehand:** if you know you'll care for a dangerously ill person on Shabbat, prepare before (MB 344:11). If only possible → good but not required (MB 330:1).
    - Someone who sometimes treats the wounded → cut bandages before Shabbat.
    - Near due date → pack the hospital bag, clear the car before Shabbat. **Not** required to spend Shabbatot near the hospital (SSK 32:34; 36:6–7).

## 2. Determining danger

- Any illness doctors consider dangerous, or that people would rush to treat, even if only a **small minority** die → **sakana**. So a **woman in labor** is driven to the hospital even though most could give birth at home safely (Magid Mishneh 2:11).
- ✗ Not for things not generally considered dangerous.
- Sages' list: **internal** injuries (severe pain, internal bleeding), wounds on the **back of the hand or foot** (dangerous infections/cuts), **very high fever**, **scorpion/snake bites**, **eye** conditions (SA 328:3–9). Doctors today agree, with different terms.
- **General rule:** if those present think he **might** be in mortal danger → act immediately (call a doctor, drive to the hospital).
- Unsure → ask a doctor, nurse, medic, or call one.
    - **Doctor says danger**, patient says no → follow the doctor (SA 328:10; 618:1, 5).
    - **Patient says danger**, doctor says no → desecrate Shabbat and take him to be checked: "*lev yode'a marat nafsho*" (Mishlei 14:10). Same if he demands a specific treatment from experience (SA 618:1).
    - Only if his claim makes some sense. Known illness + treatment doctors say is useless → follow doctors (BHL 328:10). Known **hypochondriac** + knowledgeable person sure it's nothing → don't.
- **Asking a rav first in a life-threatening case "to be pious" → he's a murderer** ("*lo ta'amod al dam re'ekha*"). And the rav whose students ask that is to be blamed for not teaching them (Yerushalmi Yoma 8:5; MB 328:6).

## 3. For whom do we desecrate Shabbat?

- The Sages' reason: "*ḥalel alav Shabbat aḥat kedei she-yishmor Shabbatot harbeh*" (Yoma 85b). But in practice we save even someone who won't keep Shabbat; the Torah wants **more life**.
- ✓ A **shoteh** (exempt from mitzvot).
- ✓ Someone **unconscious and dying**, to extend his life a little (BHL 329:4).
- ✓ A **fetus**, even before 40 days (Behag; Ritva; BHL 330:7).
- ✓ A **premature baby**. In the past an 8-month baby without hair/nails wasn't expected to live; today with incubators, whenever doctors see a chance of long-term survival → yes (SA 330:7–8; SSK 36:12).
- **Non-Jew:** technically one desecrates Shabbat only for someone commanded in Shabbat, and that applies **only if another non-Jew is there** to save him. Otherwise **must** treat him: we want them to save us, so we save them → it's pikuaḥ nefesh.

## 4. A non-Jew or a child to do the melakha?

- A Jew's melakha = d'oraita; asking a non-Jew = d'rabbanan; a child's melakha = d'rabbanan. So why not use them?
- Sages: "*ein osin devarim elu lo al yedei goyim ve-lo al yedei ketanim, ela al yedei gedolei Yisrael*" (Yoma 84b; SA 328:12). Two reasons:
    1. They may **hesitate** and not act forcefully enough (Tosafot).
    2. People might think an adult Jew **can't** desecrate Shabbat to save a life, and next time waste time looking for a non-Jew → the patient dies (Ran).
- So with many helpers, it's a **mitzva for the most respected person** to do it, to make the point (Ri'az; Tashbetz; MB 328:34).
- **Less pressured** and a non-Jew/child is easily available with **no delay** → it's a **hiddur** to have them do it (SSK 38:2).
- **Any shadow of a doubt** of delay, now or in the future → an adult Jew does it.

## 5. Shinui?

- Dilemma: a shinui makes it d'rabbanan (9:3), but "*ha-zariz harei zeh meshubaḥ*" (Yoma 84b; SA 328:2): don't put brakes on the rescuer.
- **Rule:** rescue must be the **best and fastest** way. If a shinui might delay → do it normally (it's fully permitted). If it **clearly won't** delay → le-khatchila use a shinui.
- → Doctors, nurses, medics should **learn** how to reduce ḥillul Shabbat.
- **Routine treatments** for a gravely ill patient: caregivers who don't know what's essential → treat as on a weekday. One who **knows for sure** a melakha isn't needed or can wait → wait (SA 328:4).
- **Pain relief** → ✓ (less pain = more strength to fight the illness).

## 6. Driving to the hospital

- Drive **normally**, no shinui (delay/danger). Private car or ambulance.
- **Carrying** to the car even without an eruv: vital medicines, medical documents, ID → ✓.
- **Not vital but important** (clothes, food, books) → ✓ but with a **shinui**, walking straight to the car without stopping → shvut di-shvut for a great need.
- **Muktzeh**: don't bring, but if packed in the bag with needed things → take the bag. Muktzeh needed after Shabbat (**money, phone**) → may put in the bag with a shinui on Shabbat.
- **At the hospital** (park so you don't block others):
    - Turning off the car is for the **car's** sake, not the patient → ideally ask a **non-Jew** to turn off the engine and lights and lock it.
    - None available or it would delay caring for the patient → turn off and lock **with a shinui** (back of the fingers on the key / light switch / remote).
    - Why allowed: the Sages permit d'rabbanan at the **end** of a rescue so people won't hesitate at the **start**.

## 7. Choosing a hospital and doctor

- Go to the **nearest** hospital. For routine things (births, injuries, common illnesses) hospitals are about the same.
- ✗ Farther because it's cheaper, nicer, closer for visitors: not medical reasons. Away from home for Shabbat → local hospital.
- ✓ Farther for a **real medical reason**: specializes in his illness; already knows his complex case; a high-risk pregnancy with a protocol there. Follow **accepted medical recommendations**.
- **Proportion:** the more complex, the farther you may go. A small advantage → only a bit farther (on a weekday nobody drives far for minor things either).
- Woman in labor **insists** a farther hospital is better, even without basis → may go **a bit** farther to calm her, not much.
- A **hospital that runs according to halakha** → may go a bit farther (less ḥillul Shabbat there, more comfortable), not much.

## 8. Accompanying a patient or a woman in labor

- A patient needs a **chaperone** (support, making sure he isn't overlooked). Family learns a relative was rushed in **alone** with a serious condition → one must go, even on Shabbat.
- **Woman in labor** must be accompanied (husband, mother, or doula), even if she didn't ask. Arrived alone → may call someone to come. Even a normal birth: she might panic and endanger herself → treated as sakana (SA 330:1; MB 3).
- **How many?**
    - Only **one** chaperone: husband, mother, **or** doula.
    - If she becomes **hysterical** and insists on both husband and mother, or demands the doula → ok. But **don't plan** on more than one.
    - Some say do whatever she wants, even picking people up. Rav Melamed: seems excessive.
    - Custom: one. But a **long drive** with the husband driving → mother/doula may come too to help her.
- **Small kids at home:** arrange neighbors in advance. If remote, or the neighbors are dangerous/untrustworthy and leaving kids alone is dangerous → bring them along; may make a small detour to drop them with a family.

## 9. Driving back home

- Patient **discharged, not in danger** → he and his chaperone may **not** drive home. If needed (he must rest) → a **non-Jewish driver**; the chaperone may not ride unless the patient needs help on the way (28:2).
- **False labor**, sent home → same: not with a Jew; if she still needs to lie down → non-Jewish driver, without the chaperone.
- **Ambulance from an outlying community** (needs the ambulance there) → driver may return. ✗ May not drive the patient/chaperone home (their weight burns more fuel → d'oraita; even if d'rabbanan, still forbidden).
- **City ambulance** → don't drive back to the station; they can radio him. Unless there's a real need (another medic or equipment for the next call; resting there helps him treat better).
- **Ambulance no longer needed** → **call to cancel**: it may be needed elsewhere; and medics shouldn't learn that Shabbat calls may be false alarms.
- **Contractions stopped** on the way (she wouldn't go to the hospital if she were home) → the driver stops in a safe place until after Shabbat (RSZ Auerbach). If the ambulance must return for pikuaḥ nefesh, she and her chaperone can ride back.
- **Doctor called for a gravely ill patient** → may just drive. Still better to **call** first: maybe it's unnecessary; he can give instructions; he'll know what to bring.

## 10. Doctors and nurses going to and from shifts

- Shabbat-morning shift, too far to walk → **drive before Shabbat**. Be-di'avad may drive on Shabbat. If he knows in advance, he must arrange to stay at/near the hospital (Igrot Moshe OḤ 1:131).
- **Best:** hire a **non-Jewish driver** to take him from home. Benefit from a non-Jew's melakha is allowed for a sick person.
- **Going home** after a Shabbat shift → ✓ with a non-Jewish driver. Rescuers may do d'rabbanan to get home, or they'd stop taking Shabbat shifts.

## 11. Scheduling surgeries and britot before Shabbat

- Non-urgent surgery needing follow-up melakhot, or painful (wisdom tooth) → le-khatchila **Sunday–Tuesday**. If the **best surgeon** is only available later in the week → ok (2:10–11).
- **Inducing labor** on Friday per doctors → ✓ even though she'll likely give birth on Shabbat (SSK 32:33).
- **Urgent** surgery → don't postpone (delays happen).
- **Delayed brit** (baby was sick) on Thursday/Friday:
    - Many Sefardim: don't (it's no longer the 8th day; he may need care on Shabbat) (Tashbetz 1:21; Rav Pe'alim; Yabi'a Omer YD 5:23).
    - Ashkenazim, Yemenites, some Sefardim: do it as soon as possible; the risk is low (Shakh YD 266:18; MA 331:9).

## 12. War on Shabbat

- A **defensive war** is a mitzva even greater than saving a life: you're not required to risk your life for another, but **everyone must** risk his life to save Klal Yisrael (Mishpat Kohen §143; Tzitz Eliezer 13:100).
- Enemies attack → fight on Shabbat. Rambam: help besieged brothers on Shabbat; don't wait (MT Shabbat 2:23).
- **Preemptive attack** on terrorists planning attacks → ✓, including on Shabbat if there's a strategic reason (Heikhal Yitzḥak 37:3; Amud Ha-yemini §16).
- **Even over "straw and hay"** at border towns → go out armed on Shabbat; otherwise they'll come for lives (Eruvin 45a; SA 329:6). Today **all of Israel** is like a border town (R. Goren) → ongoing security on Shabbat is a mitzva.
- **Non-observant hikers** in an area that needs an armed escort (and they can't be stopped) → soldiers must protect them. But don't **help** their ḥillul Shabbat: no opening checkpoints, permits, or boarding to get them started; escort only once they're on their way (Meshiv Milḥama 1:7; 2:110).
- **Fallen soldiers' bodies** → may retrieve on Shabbat (morale; Israel trades terrorists for bodies → danger to life). After retrieval, no further ḥillul Shabbat for the bodies.
- **Conquest of Eretz Yisrael** = milḥemet mitzva → may even **initiate** an attack on Shabbat with a tactical advantage (Yeriḥo was conquered on Shabbat; Yerushalmi Shabbat 1:8).

## 13. Commanders' authority: war vs. routine

- **War:** do everything for victory, fast. Don't stop to ask rabbis or bother officers about what's necessary.
- **Routine** security and intelligence → minimize; only what prevents danger to life; use a shinui when possible. The IDF rabbinate must set Shabbat procedures.
- **Training** ✗ on Shabbat, except on high alert with a security need for an upcoming operation.
- **Orders:** when the system works, trust the officer (he knows security + rabbinate guidelines) → obey.
    - Reason to doubt (ignores the rabbinate, rabbinate not doing its job, illogical order) → ask whether it's needed for security.
    - **Clearly unnecessary** → don't obey.
    - **Unsure** → obey (safek pikuaḥ nefesh), then after Shabbat check with the rabbinate/his rav; if wrong, **file a complaint**.
- **Test:** how does the army treat this operation during the week? Done even at the cost of rest or entertainment → may be done on Shabbat. Sometimes canceled for convenience → no.

## 14. Common army questions

- **Patrol vehicle**: don't leave the set route to eat somewhere nicer or meet friends. No fixed route → may plan it conveniently. A half-hour break may be where convenient. Patrolling a settlement for presence → may park by the shul or a convenient place to eat.
- **Operation definitely on Shabbat** → stay on base for Shabbat (going home means driving back on Shabbat) (MB 344:11). Even a married officer whose family will be upset.
    - **Might be canceled** → may go home; explain to the family why, so they don't take Shabbat lightly. If it's on → drive to base.
    - A **non-Jew** can drive him → may go home even if it's certain. Back home after the operation only with a non-Jewish driver.
- **Soldiers no longer needed** → call to tell them not to come (less ḥillul Shabbat; and they shouldn't learn that cancellations aren't reported).
- **Swapping guard duty**: good to swap a Shabbat shift with a non-observant soldier's weekday shift if he's willing and it won't make guarding lax. (While guarding, he's doing a mitzva, not ḥillul Shabbat.) But no need to **initiate**: defending the country is a mitzva (Ha-tzava Ka-halakha ch. 27).

## 15. What must one give up to reduce ḥillul Shabbat?

- **Nothing dear to you.** And if forced, people might hesitate to deal with hazards.
- **Downed live wires**: you could stand guard all Shabbat, but if that's hard → call the electric company (RSZ Auerbach; Tzitz Eliezer 8:15).
- **Freezing home of a gravely ill person**: don't need to ask the heated neighbor to take him in; turn on the heat. The neighbor isn't required to take him in (RSZ Auerbach, from the rule that one needn't give up property).
- **Guard** needn't do an extra shift so his replacement won't travel.
- **Tanks** whose shortest route would break the **eruv** → take a longer route (unless all the eruv's users agree).
- **Ambulance/car** to the hospital → don't drive through private or public **gardens** even if it's shorter (Ha-tzava Ka-halakha 26:4–7; there's a dissenting view).

## 16. Police on Shabbat

- **Danger to life** → everyone agrees: call the police (suspicious object, dangerous people, a fight that could turn deadly, robbers in a home with people).
- **No danger** (property only):
    - Some poskim: no d'oraita for police. ✗ Calling after thieves left; ✗ thieves in an empty home; ✗ a break-in at a closed store/bank; ✗ writing reports, fingerprints, bringing a thief in (SSK 41:24–25; Yalkut Yosef 329:20–27).
    - Many leading poskim: ✓ call to prevent theft/damage; police may drive there. Otherwise crime soars and eventually endangers lives. Some allow returning to the station and regular patrols if drivers aren't Jewish (Heikhal Yitzḥak 32; Yaskil Avdi; Tzitz Eliezer 4:4).
    - **R. Shaul Yisraeli** (Amud Ha-yemini §17): Jewish police may patrol, return to the station, and transport suspects. Hampering the police → crime → danger. Like rescuers going home (§10) and like preferring adult Jews (§4). Also in Israel crime is often tied to **terror**.
- All agree ✗: calling police for **purely financial** reports (insurance), or about **noisy neighbors** on Friday night.
- The police should have rabbis review operations, set Shabbat protocols (shinui, non-Jewish officers drive and write), and give every officer a **Shabbat pen** (18:2).

## 17. Phones and weapons for health and security

- **With an eruv**: medics/volunteers who always carry a **beeper or phone** may carry them; one who always carries a **gun** may too.
    - Muktzeh: gun = kli she-melakhto le-heter (defense); **two-way radio** = le-heter (saves lives); **cell phone** = le-isur, but may carry for its use (gufo), i.e., saving lives.
- **Without an eruv**: normally not.
    - When people **must stay near** weapons/communications, may carry them to usual Shabbat places (shul, simḥas). Otherwise no one would volunteer. Usually this permits only d'rabbanan, so: **radio with a shinui** (under the shirt); **gun normally** (a shinui would be dangerous; some say for security personnel a weapon is like clothing).
    - ✗ For a **leisure walk** outside the eruv, and don't take walks where you'd need to be armed.
- **Soldier going to a nearby community's minyan** through an area with no eruv → after leaving the base rest the gun and radio on a **mekom petur**, then continue; same on the way back (21:7).
- Fires: 16:6–7. Alarms: 17:15.
