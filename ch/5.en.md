## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Topic | Key point |
|---|---|
| **How much Torah?** | Half the waking day (~9 hrs incl. prayer → at least ~6 hrs learning) |
| **What to learn** | Enjoyable; halakha, emunah, mussar; teach your kids |
| **Sleep** | A bit extra is oneg; not to make up the week or to work Motza'ei Shabbat |
| **Kri'at haTorah** | 7 aliyot + maftir + haftara; 3 at Minḥa |
| **Shnayim mikra** | Parsha twice + Onkelos (or Rashi) once; ideally before Shabbat lunch |
| **Amida** | 7 berakhot; Ata Kidashta / Yismaḥ Moshe / Ata Eḥad; plus Musaf |
| **Friday night extras** | Va-yekhulu ×3, Me'ein Sheva (only with a regular minyan), Kabbalat Shabbat |
| **Women** | No set half-day; learning on Shabbat is a mitzva and part of oneg |

## 1. Torah study on Shabbat

- "Shabbat and Yom Tov were given only for Torah" (y. Shabbat 15). Torah's partner is Shabbat (Tur 290).
- Split the day: **half Torah, half eating/drinking/sleep** (Pesaḥim 68b). Baḥ: more than half on Shabbat; most: half.
- Practically: 25 hrs − 7 sleep = 18 → **9 hrs** Torah incl. prayer → about **6 hrs** learning + 3 davening.
- Working people: **more** Torah; full-time learners: a bit **more pleasure** (Rema 290:2).

## 2. What to learn

- Learning should be a **pleasure**: some say easy, clear material; others (Ḥida) say scholars should delve. Depends on what you enjoy.
- Mainly what guides life: **halakha**, emunah, mussar, Tanakh, Ḥasidut, whatever lights you up.
- Try to have a **ḥidush**: on Motza'ei Shabbat God asks what you innovated with your neshama yeteira (Zohar). Can't? Learn something new.
- Learn with your **children**: double mitzva. Teaching a grandchild = like receiving the Torah at Sinai.

## 3. Sleep on Shabbat

- "Sleep on Shabbat is a pleasure": e.g., 8 hours instead of 7.
- Not to sleep so you can **work** (or even learn) Motza'ei Shabbat. Not to plan a sleepless Thursday/Friday to catch up on Shabbat.
- Afternoon nap: Rambam's pious skipped it; Tur: fine if usual. Just keep the 6 hours of learning.
- Don't **overeat**: heavy and sleepy isn't oneg.

## 4. The Shabbat drasha

- Moshe instituted gathering every Shabbat to teach its laws (Yalkut Shimoni).
- No scheduling a meal during the drasha (SA 290:2); a Jerusalem family that did was wiped out (Gittin 38b).
- "The reward of the drasha is the **running**" (Berakhot 6b). Not going → learn during that time, don't walk or eat.
- R. Ḥiya (halakha) vs. R. Abahu (aggada): mainly halakha, plus reasons, emunah and mussar per community need.
- Leaders should set up many shiurim for everyone.

## 5. Women and Torah on Shabbat

- Men: obligation to set time "day and night"; on Shabbat, literally.
- Women: must **know** halakha, emunah and mussar; today that means more learning.
- Not obligated to give half of Shabbat, but learning is a **mitzva** and part of oneg. Busy with young kids → exempt; otherwise learn a lot, joyfully; shiurim recommended.
- Couples learning together: wonderful; if it's hard, no guilt.

## 6. Reading the Torah

- From Moshe: read on Shabbat, Mon, Thu. Shabbat: **7** olim (days of the week).
- Eretz Yisrael once: 3-year cycle; today: annual, ending Simḥat Torah. Missed a parsha → read two next week.
- Adding aliyot (min. 3 verses each): ideally not (extra berakhot, burden); ok to avoid offense.
- Minors: SA allows (most olim adults); Ashkenazim don't; some Sephardim only 7th; Yemenites 6th.

## 7. Haftara

- Began under a decree banning Torah reading; kept afterward. One berakha before, **four** after.
- Min. **21 verses** (7 × 3), unless the topic ends sooner.
- Maftir reads a few verses from the Torah first; custom: maftir isn't one of the 7, after Half-Kaddish.
- From a printed book is fine (most); better a full book of Nevi'im.
- Better the maftir reads alone; others may read along quietly.

## 8. Minḥa reading

- Ezra: **3** olim read the start of next week's parsha.
- Why: for "**yoshvei keranot**" (shopkeepers who missed Mon/Thu), or to keep people from getting drunk after lunch.
- Hence "Va-ani tefillati… et ratzon" before the reading.

## 9. Shnayim mikra ve-eḥad targum

- Every man: parsha **twice** + **Onkelos** once → long life (Berakhot 8a).
- Other translations don't count; **Rashi** does (but verses without Rashi → read 3 times). Mehadrin: both.
- Window: from last Shabbat's Minḥa → ideally before **Shabbat lunch** → by Minḥa → by **Tuesday** → by Simḥat Torah.

## 10. Customs of shnayim mikra

- All at once Friday (Arizal) or one aliya a day (Gra). Either works.
- Verse-verse-targum (Arizal) or by parshiya (Shlah, Gra). Order doesn't matter be-di'eved.
- During kri'at haTorah: better to read along quietly with the ba'al korei and count it as one time.
- Women are exempt; doing it is a mitzva.

## 11. Shabbat prayers

- Amida = **7** berakhot: middle one ends "mekadesh ha-Shabbat." Openings: Ma'ariv **Ata Kidashta**, Shaḥarit **Yismaḥ Moshe**, Minḥa **Ata Eḥad**. Mixed them up → yotzei.
- No weekday requests: to honor Shabbat and avoid sorrow.
- Started a weekday berakha by mistake → finish it, then switch. Skipped the Shabbat berakha: before finishing → go back; finished the Amida → repeat.
- **Musaf** = the extra korban.

## 12. Va-yekhulu

- Saying it Friday night = **partner in creation**; two angels: "your sin is purged" (Shabbat 119b).
- Said in the Amida, again **standing aloud** after (for Yom Tov on Shabbat / public testimony), and in **kiddush** = 3 times.

## 13. Me'ein Sheva

- Ḥazan's summary of the 7 berakhot, so latecomers wouldn't walk home alone from shuls in the fields.
- Only in a shul / **regular** minyan; not at a private minyan (ḥatan, avel) (SA 268:10). Kabbalists: any minyan. Jerusalem: always.
- If the congregation says Magen Avot, the ḥazan repeats it. Bowing at the start: follow custom.

## 14. Kabbalat Shabbat and additions

- Tzefat kabbalists, 400+ years ago; **Lekha Dodi** by R. Shlomo Alkabetz.
- "Bo'i kalla": face **west** (Arizal; Shekhina in the west), some face the door.
- **Ba-meh Madlikin** (or Kegavna). Morning: extra psalms of creation and Torah, and **Nishmat**.
- Women: Birkhot ha-Shaḥar + Amida; good to come to shul on Shabbat if possible.

## 15. "Shabbat shalom" to the rav

- Visiting your rav: far → festivals; nearer → monthly; close → **every Shabbat** (Shunamite woman, II Melakhim 4:23).
- Today: wishing the rav Shabbat shalom after davening, or attending his drasha.
- R. Tzvi Yehuda: women connect more to the Torah's overall spirit; "house of Yaakov" was addressed first.
