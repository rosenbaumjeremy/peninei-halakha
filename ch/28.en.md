## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Category | Who | Non-Jew? | Jew with shinui? | Medicine? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Choleh she-yesh bo sakana** | Life in danger | Everything | Everything, even d'oraita (ch. 27) | ✓ |
| **Choleh she-ein bo sakana** | Bedridden, or the kind of illness that puts you in bed; whole-body pain (migraine); a needy child | ✓ even d'oraita | ✓ d'rabbanan (best with shinui) | ✓ |
| **Miḥush / ailment with pain** | Walking around, but it really hurts | ✓ d'rabbanan only, with a shinui (shvut di-shvut) | ✗ | Mass-produced: ✓ |
| **Miḥush / ailment, just annoying** | Walking around, uncomfortable | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |

**Medicine rule (*shḥikat samamanim*):** a gezeira so you won't grind herbs. Healthy-people foods and treatments are always ok (hard candy, prune juice, ice, hot-water bottle, cotton in the ear). A course already started → continue on Shabbat.

**Watch the melakhot:** no **spreading** creams (Memare'aḥ) · no **wringing** compresses (Seḥita) · no **cutting** bandages (Meḥatekh) · pour iodine directly, not on cotton · Band-Aids ✓.

## 1. Three levels of illness

1. **Dangerously ill** → do everything as on a weekday (ch. 27).
2. **"Regular" sick person** (whole body sick, no danger) → **d'rabbanan** issurim may be set aside; d'oraita not (§2).
3. **Mildly sick / ailment** (*miḥush*), walks around like a healthy person → all d'rabbanan apply, not even shvut di-shvut. **If in pain** → shvut di-shvut ok: a non-Jew does a d'rabbanan, or a Jew with a shinui (SA 307:5; MB 328:3; 9:11).
- Plus a specific gezeira against **medicine** for a mildly sick person. Machloket whether it applies to factory-made medicine (§3–5).
- **The idea:** on Shabbat we accept reality calmly. No clean clothes → wear dirty. Forgot to cook something → do without, or ask a neighbor. Forgot the heat → wear a coat. Forgot the AC → be a bit hot. It frees us from the constant pressure of fixing every detail; that *menuḥa* and emuna is a gift. Not running for medicine for a minor ache is part of that rest. But real pain that ruins oneg Shabbat → shvut di-shvut allowed; and real illness → all d'rabbanan, since caring for health is a mitzva.

## 2. A regular sick person

- Lies in bed because of illness, or has the **kind of illness** that normally puts people in bed even if he isn't lying down, or **pain weakening the whole body** (migraine) (SA 328:17).
- Walks around fine but clearly **will end up in bed** without some treatment → also (SSK 33:1).
- A **child who really needs** something → same (Rema 328:17; 24:6).
- **Easiest: a non-Jew** (allowed for a sick person; Shabbat 129a). He may: turn lights on/off, turn on a heater, cook, travel to bring medicine, press elevator buttons, do X-rays; a non-Jewish **dentist** for a toothache that hurts the whole body; a non-Jewish doctor writes a prescription; drive him to a doctor/hospital (a Jew may come along if neither does melakha).
- **Medicine** → ✓ any he needs (the gezeira is only for the mildly sick) (Rema 328:37).
- **No non-Jew available:** Ran: a Jew may not do d'rabbanan. **Rashba: may**, and that's the halakha. Le-khatchila still prefer a non-Jew or a **shinui** (→ shvut di-shvut). E.g., turn a light, heater, or AC on/off **with an elbow or foot** (9:3).

## 3. Mildly sick or uncomfortable

- Looks healthy but has a mild illness/ailment → **like everyone else**: all d'rabbanan apply.
    - ✗ Ask a non-Jew to turn off a light bothering him, or turn on light, heat, AC. Not even with a shinui (SA 328:1).
- **If it hurts** → shvut di-shvut ok: a **non-Jew with a shinui** (e.g., with his arm) for light/heat/AC he really needs. ✗ A Jew, even with a shinui, since that's still a plain shvut (SA 307:5; 328:25; 9:11–12).
- **Fingernail torn most of the way:**
    - Just annoying → ✗ removing it, even shvut di-shvut.
    - Painful → may remove **with a shinui** (hand or teeth). Since it's mostly off, it's already like detached → d'rabbanan (Shabbat 94b; SA 328:31; 14:2).
    - Not mostly torn but painful → a non-Jew with a shinui.
- **Splinter** whose removal will clearly cause bleeding: annoying → ✗; painful → ✓ (unintended bleeding via a shinui = shvut di-shvut) (MB 328:88).

## 4. The gezeira of grinding ingredients (*shḥikat samamanim*)

- The Sages forbade someone with a minor ailment to take medicine, apply medicinal ointment, or do anything meant to heal, lest he **grind herbs** (Toḥen, d'oraita) (Shabbat 54b; Rashi).
    - ✗ Wine or drops in a sore eye (SA 328:20); ✗ healing ointment on a wound (328:22); ✗ gargling oil for a sore throat; ✗ rinsing a toothache with vinegar, salt water, or alcohol. ✓ May **drink** liquor to dull the pain, if he doesn't hold it in his mouth longer than normal (328:32).
- **In pain** → a non-Jew may put the drops in or the alcohol on the tooth (shvut di-shvut). (Radbaz & R. Mordechai Benet: someone in pain may even take it himself.)
- **Food:** ✗ things **only sick people** eat/drink; ✓ things **healthy people** also eat, even if meant as medicine (Shabbat 109b; SA 328:37).
    - ✗ Throat lozenges; ✓ regular hard candies (SSK 34:4).
    - ✗ Flaxseed water for constipation; ✓ prune juice.
- ✓ Things healthy people normally do: oil that healthy people use on itchy skin (SA 327:1); oil/lip balm on hands and lips (people use it for softness anyway).
- **Trick:** mix the medicine into a drink **before Shabbat** so no one can tell, and drink it on Shabbat (RSZ Auerbach; SSK 34:5).

## 5. Modern medications

- Some say factory-made medicine isn't included (no one grinds anything). **Most poskim:** still forbidden for a mildly sick person:
    1. A rabbinic decree can only be cancelled by a greater beit din.
    2. Some people still make home remedies.
- **In practice:**
    - **Annoying, not painful** → be strict, no medicine.
    - **Painful** → may take **mass-produced** medicine (some say the gezeira never covered pain; and with factory-made medicine no one will make it himself).
- When it's permitted, **use it**; that's oneg Shabbat.
    - Eyes/ears bothering → no drops; painful → drops. Runny nose: annoying → no nose drops; painful → use them.
    - **Sleeping pills** for insomnia → ✓ (otherwise he'll suffer).
- **Rav Melamed's test:** would you **walk a kilometer** to get the medicine? Then it's real pain → take it. Not worth the effort → it's a mere ailment → don't.

## 6. Ongoing courses of medicine

- Started during the week, needs consecutive days, skipping Shabbat hurts its effect → **continue on Shabbat**. Some say the gezeira doesn't apply to a course begun before Shabbat (R. Shlomo Kluger). With mass-produced medicine → le-khatchila, even without pain.
- ✓ Birth control pills, pregnancy-support medication, daily vitamins, weight-loss pills.

## 7. Injections, IVs, nursing

- **Subcutaneous injection** (no necessary bleeding) → like any medicine: ✓ for a sick person. Not sick but in pain → a non-Jew gives it.
- **IV / intravenous injection** → ✗ for a Jew (causes bleeding; some say d'oraita), as long as no danger. ✓ A non-Jew for a regular sick person.
    - A **port** installed before Shabbat → may hook up the IV bag on Shabbat even without danger (SSK 33:7).
- **Disinfecting** with iodine/peroxide: ✗ on cotton or gauze (squeezing = **Seḥita**). Pour directly, or use a tongue depressor or non-absorbent synthetic material.
- Know you'll need to inject → prepare and disinfect the syringe before Shabbat. Forgot or medically inadvisable → may prepare on Shabbat (no d'oraita) (SSK 33:8–10).
- **Engorged nursing mother** → may express/pump **so the milk goes to waste** (into the sink, or a container with soap). For the baby it's d'oraita (tolada of **Dash**, SA 328:34; 10:17); to waste it's d'rabbanan, allowed for pain (SA 330:8).
    - Manual pump, or an electric pump on a **timer** set before Shabbat (SSK 36:22).
    - If doctors say the baby **needs** breast milk and she never misses a pumping during the week → may pump for the baby (pikuaḥ nefesh).

## 8. Ointments and compresses

- Even when medicine is allowed: ✗ **spreading** cream/ointment on a wound or bandage = **Memare'aḥ** (tolada of Memaḥek, d'oraita) (Shabbat 75b; 18:6).
- Sages also forbade **placing** it without spreading (lest he spread), even on a bandage prepared before Shabbat (SA 328:25).
- **For pain** (to relieve or prevent) → may **place** ointment on the wound/bandage without spreading: squeeze the tube right onto it, or use a tongue depressor/spoon. When the bandage presses it out to the sides → fine, as long as he doesn't smooth it on purpose (SSK 33:14).
- **Cream rubbed in until fully absorbed** → ✓ for pain (not Memare'aḥ). Wanting a layer left on the skin to smooth it → d'oraita (Da'at Torah 328:26; RSZ Auerbach; 14:5).
- **Compresses** for someone with great, weakening pain (= sick): cloths dampened before Shabbat; if needed, wet a **completely clean** cloth on Shabbat (14:4). **Never wring it**, not to get water onto the spot nor to clean it after (SSK 33:19; 12:8, 10).

## 9. Band-Aids, bandages, wounds

- **Band-Aid** to protect a wound from rubbing → ✓, even for a mild ailment (protects, doesn't heal) (SA 328:23). Tofer doesn't apply to the body, and it's temporary.
    - Le-khatchila: don't stick its ends to each other or use it to hold a cloth bandage (some say d'rabbanan, like Tofer). If needed, lenient (temporary, like a temporary knot).
- ✗ **Cutting** a bandage or Band-Aid to size = **Meḥatekh** (d'oraita) (MB 322:18; 15:10). Too long → wrap it around more times.
- Prepare bandages of various sizes before Shabbat, plus **safety pins / an elastic sleeve** to hold them.
- **Butterfly bandage** to close a cut → ✓ (no Tofer on the body; and it just holds the edges while it heals itself) (SSK 35:25).
- **Stopping bleeding**: a bandage (tied permissibly), antihemorrhagic spray/powder → ✓, not "medicine" (SA 328:29). **Iodine** to prevent infection → ✓ (SSK 35:13).
- Wiping blood with gauze/paper towel, or a bandage stained by iodine → ✓ (that's soiling, not dyeing; none of these are meant to be dyed) (MB 303:79; 320:58; 18:5).
- **Removing** a bothersome Band-Aid → ✓. Le-khatchila avoid pulling out hairs (**Gozez**); if unavoidable, ok (unintended, a side effect) (SSK 35:30).

## 10. Opening medicine packages

- When medicine is allowed → may tear plastic/paper/cardboard packaging. The stringent **ruin** the package so it can't store the medicine (15:12).
- Prefer not to tear **letters**; if unavoidable, ok (not intended, destructive) (18:3).
- **Pill bottle with a plastic seal** → may open it; the cap and bottle were already finished, just attached (15:13–14).

## 11. Measuring and thermometers

- **Mercury thermometer, manual blood-pressure cuff** → ✓ when medically needed. Measuring is a weekday activity, but allowed for a mitzva or medical need (SA 306:7; 328:43; 22:6). May **shake down** the thermometer (SSK 40:2).
- **Forehead strip thermometer:** some permit (numbers already printed, just appear briefly; Yeḥaveh Da'at 4:29); others say temporary writing, d'rabbanan (SSK 40:2) → **when needed, lenient** (Tzitz Eliezer 14:30).
- **Tests that produce a color**: some forbid (Tzove'a); others permit (it's the result he wants, not the color). Le-khatchila strict; when needed lenient (safek d'rabbanan) (SSK 33:20).

## 12. Permitted treatments

- Treatments **not normally done with medicine** → no grinding concern → ✓ when there's real need (e.g., pain). Without need → ✗ (weekday activity) (SA 328:43; MB 136).
- ✓ **Pressing** an injury (hand or utensil) to stop swelling (Ḥayei Adam 69:5; MB 328:144).
- ✓ **Ice** on an injury to prevent swelling / reduce pain (SSK 35:35; 33:15).
- ✓ **Eye exercises** if the eyes hurt. Just strengthening → like physio (§13).
- ✓ **Retainer** (no medicine straightens teeth) (SSK 34:29).
- ✓ **Hot-water bottle** on a stomach or ear ache (MB 326:19).
- ✓ **Cotton in the ear** (protects from wind, doesn't heal) (SA 303:15).
- ✓ **Raw egg** to soothe the throat (SA 328:38).
- ✓ **Talcum powder** in shoes (sweat, odor, comfort). ✗ Medicated powder for athlete's foot, unless it hurts (§5).

## 13. Physio, massage, acupuncture

- **Physiotherapy:** done under professional guidance → weekday activity, even without equipment. Not careful to do it regularly during the week → ✗ on Shabbat; do it Friday and Motza'ei Shabbat.
    - Really necessary and he does it **several times a day** during the week → ✓ on Shabbat, even with non-electric equipment (like a course of pills, §6; Nishmat Avraham 328:93).
- ✓ **Gentle stretches** for back/neck or to refresh. ✗ **Calisthenics** for fitness (22:8).
- **Professional massage:**
    - For mere discomfort → ✗ (those aches are treated with pills/creams → the gezeira; and weekday activity).
    - **Really painful** → ✓ (if mass-produced medicine is ok, surely a non-medicine treatment; and the weekday-activity issur doesn't apply with pain).
- **Non-professional massage** → always ✓. Even a professional masseur may give his family a relaxing, non-therapeutic one.
- **Acupressure:** no great need → ✗. **In pain** → ✓, by hand or a tool made for it.
- **Acupuncture:** needles are **muktzeh machmat chesron kis** → ✗ even with pain. ✓ For a patient who **badly needs** it (the Sages suspend their decrees for the sick), but only if it won't necessarily cause bleeding (d'oraita).
- **Payment:** a professional treating on Shabbat may not take payment for it, unless it's **bundled** with weekday treatments (22:12). Don't discuss the arrangement on Shabbat; may say "we'll talk after Shabbat about what we can't discuss now" (22:3, 10).

## 14. A non-observant doctor

- A sick person (no danger) who needs a doctor → try a **religious doctor** who knows how to avoid d'oraita. A Jewish doctor who normally desecrates Shabbat may turn on lights, write details, write a prescription on his account (only d'rabbanan is permitted for this patient).
- Likewise prefer a **hospital run according to halakha**.
- None available → may see a non-observant doctor **if he asks him** not to do d'oraita on his account. If the doctor **insists** on writing normally etc. → forgo the treatment (mesayei'a).
- **Very urgent** exam/treatment → may rely on the lenient view and be treated.
