## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Idea | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Shabbat completes creation** | Six days made the world; the seventh gave it rest, meaning and blessing |
| **Zakhor** (positive) | Remember Shabbat: creation; done mainly through kiddush, really the whole day |
| **Shamor** (negative) | Refrain from melakha: linked to yetziat Mitzrayim and freedom |
| **The 39 melakhot** | Learned from building the Mishkan, which also stops on Shabbat |
| **Severity** | Intentional desecration: stoning (with witnesses and warning) or karet |
| **Peace** | Body and soul, and all forces in the world, are revealed as one |

## 1. The completion of creation

- God doesn't get tired, yet He "rested" on the seventh day to create **rest** for the world (Mekhilta; Bereishit Rabba 10:9).
- Nonstop work = endless sense of lack. Rest is possible only when you know your work has **value**.
- The world without Shabbat is like a wedding canopy without a **bride**, or a signet ring without its **seal**.
- All humanity has absorbed the idea of a day off; the true divine value of work is reached only through Shabbat, a gift to Israel.

## 2. Shabbat and the Jewish people

- "**The** sixth day" hints to the 6th of Sivan: creation exists on condition Israel accepts the Torah (Shabbat 88a).
- Shabbat complained it had no partner; God answered: **Knesset Yisrael is your partner** (Bereishit Rabba 11:8).
- Before Israel, Shabbat kept the world existing; with Israel and the Torah, the world can be **repaired**.

## 3. The Exodus and Shabbat

- The Avot kept Shabbat intuitively, but couldn't sustain it in the world. In Egypt, the Jews read scrolls of redemption **every Shabbat**.
- Leaving Egypt and receiving Shabbat = freedom from slavery to **nature, survival and money**.
- Even a slave rests on Shabbat: his spirit stays free (Devarim 5:12–15).
- The Torah was given on Shabbat; it's a day especially suited to **Torah study**.

## 4. Israel's uniqueness and Shabbat

- Shabbat is a **sign** between God and Israel (Shemot 31:13–17), "a good gift from My treasury" (Beitza 16a).
- Given "in private": its inner meaning is grasped through the **neshama yeteira**.
- A non-Jew may not keep Shabbat fully: it's like interrupting a king and his bride (San. 58b).
- Going out to greet Shabbat: R. Ḥanina ("the Shabbat queen"), R. Yannai ("Come, O bride") → **Lekha Dodi**.

## 5. The six days and Shabbat

- Weekdays are the **body** of the week, Shabbat its **soul**.
- Shabbat is both the **end** of the week (world's view) and the **start** (Adam, created on day six, began with Shabbat).
- Wed–Fri prepare for Shabbat; Sun–Tue still feel its influence (Pesaḥim 106a).
- Six = the sides of physical things; seven = the inner essence (Maharal).

## 6. Blessing and holiness

- **Kadosh** = separate, beyond time and place. **Berakha** = growth and increase.
- The holier something is, the more blessing flows from it → "HaKadosh Barukh Hu."
- All six days draw blessing from Shabbat (Zohar). Honoring Shabbat brings **wealth** (Shabbat 119a).

## 7. Manna on Shabbat

- The test: gather **one day's** portion and trust God for tomorrow; leftovers rotted.
- Friday: a **double portion** (two omers) that didn't spoil; none fell on Shabbat.
- The blessing wasn't more food but **freedom from worry** on Shabbat, which in turn blesses the weekdays.

## 8. Zakhor and Shamor

|  | Zakhor | Shamor |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Positive mitzva (ḥesed) | Negative mitzva (din) |
| Source | Shemot 20 (first Luḥot) | Devarim 5 (second Luḥot) |
| Linked to | Creation | Yetziat Mitzrayim |

- "Said in one utterance" (Shevuot 20b): one root, split in two in the human world.
- Positive mitzvot are higher; punishment for negative ones is harsher (Ramban).
- "Shabbat" = **shevita** (resting, Shamor) and **teshuva** (return, Zakhor).

## 9. Foundations of faith

- Shabbat is the **4th** of the Ten Commandments, after faith, no idols, God's name.
- Two fundamentals: God **created** the world, and He **runs** it (Exodus = providence).
- Hebrew days are counted toward Shabbat ("first day," "second day"), not named after gods (Ramban).
- Main fulfilment of Zakhor: **kiddush**, over wine near a meal. In principle the whole day is for holiness and Torah.

## 10. Ceasing from building the Mishkan

- Shabbat is "like the World to Come": rising above the pressure of daily needs.
- Look at the **good** on Shabbat (Tehillim 92); accept reality lovingly, even what wasn't prepared.
- The **39 melakhot** come from building the Mishkan, which also stops on Shabbat (Shemot 31:13): even holy work needs its soul.
- The whole world is meant to become a Mishkan; Shabbat gives weekday work its meaning.

> Line to remember: even the Mishkan stops on Shabbat, so the building never forgets what it's for.

## 11. Equal to all the mitzvot

- Shabbat = all the mitzvot: shown from Torah (manna), Nevi'im (Yeḥezkel 20) and Ketuvim (Neḥemia 9) (y. Nedarim 3:9).
- Keeping Shabbat properly, "even an idolater like the generation of Enosh," is forgiven (Shabbat 118b).
- One who keeps Shabbat is distanced from sin (Mekhilta).

## 12. The Temple and Shabbat

- Yirmiyahu (17:19–27): keep Shabbat (no carrying through the gates) → Jerusalem stands forever; if not → fire in its gates.
- "Jerusalem was destroyed only because they desecrated Shabbat" (Shabbat 119b).

## 13. Redemption depends on Shabbat

- Yeshayahu 56: justice + Shabbat → redemption. "Keep **two** Shabbatot properly → redeemed immediately" (Shabbat 118b).
- Real Shabbat frees from slavery to money → leads to justice all week.
- Keeping Shabbat is the merit for entering **Eretz Yisrael** (Bereishit Rabba 46:9).

## 14. Severity of desecration

- Intentional + witnesses + warning → **stoning**; intentional without witnesses → **karet** (Shemot 31:14).
- In practice almost never carried out (a court that killed once in 7 or 70 years was "murderous").
- The **wood-gatherer** (Bamidbar 15): one of only two executions in the Torah. Midrash: he acted to show the mitzvot still apply; some say he was Tzelofḥad.
- Gehinnom rests on Shabbat, except for those who never kept it (Zohar).

## 15. A public desecrator and the community

- Classically: a **public** Shabbat desecrator is like an idolater, treated as a non-Jew (Rambam): not counted for a minyan, his wine forbidden.
- Today many poskim: only one who desecrates **to spite** is treated that way; one who does it for convenience counts for a minyan and is owed kindness.
- Some still rule strictly → encourage lighting candles and kiddush and keeping what one can.
- A Jew never fully leaves Judaism; distance from sin, but encourage teshuva.

## 16. Shabbat, peace and unity

- On Shabbat body and soul are seen to **complement** each other → enjoy both physically and spiritually.
- Rivalries of the week are revealed as parts of one whole; the wicked exist only to rouse the good (Tehillim 92).
- Avoid arguments and sadness on Shabbat; it's the time to make up (Tikkunei Zohar).
- **Shabbat candles** = peace: in the light, everything is in its place.
