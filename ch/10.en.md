## Cheat sheet (the big picture)

| Three questions | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Is it Bishul?** (Torah) | Raw → cooked is forbidden. **Ein bishul aḥar bishul** for fully cooked solids. Liquids: SA no; Rema only if still warm; Rambam yes |
| **2. Might he stoke the fire?** (rabbinic, before Shabbat) | Not-ready food only on a **covered** heat (plata, blekh, covered knobs) |
| **3. Does it look like cooking?** (rabbinic, on Shabbat) | No putting cold cooked food on the fire; on a plata: upside-down pot (strict) or directly (lenient) |

| Vessel | Cooks? |
|---|---|
| Kli rishon (heated on fire) | ✗ Cooks everything |
| Kli sheni (poured into) | Only kalei ha-bishul → don't put uncooked food in it |
| Kli shlishi | ✓ Doesn't cook → tea bag ok |

**Also:** yad soledet bo = between 45°C and 71°C (stay out of that range) · no hatmana on Shabbat, and never with something that adds heat.

## 1. Introduction

- Animals eat raw food; man has to **work** for bread ("by the sweat of your brow").
- Before the sin food would have grown ready to eat; in the future it will again (Shabbat 30b).
- 11 melakhot of bread (sowing → baking) + 3 of meat (trapping, slaughtering, skinning). Start with Bishul, the most relevant.

## 2. General principles

- Bishul = making or improving food by **fire's heat** (boiling, baking, roasting).
- Even food edible raw, if cooking improves it (water!) → Torah. Not improved → rabbinic.
- **Toldot ha-esh** (a pan heated by fire, even off the fire) → Torah. **Microwave** → Torah.
- Three checks: (1) Torah Bishul; (2) "**shema yeḥate**" (might stoke the fire); (3) **looks like cooking**.

## 3. Cooking forbidden, reheating allowed

- Shabbat = not creating something new. Raw → cooked is new; reheating isn't.
- "Cooked" = **fully cooked**, fit to serve guests (SA 318:4), not just ben Derusa'i.
- Not fully cooked → no moving to a hotter spot, no towel, no putting the lid back.
- Fully cooked → may move to a hotter spot, re-cover, add a towel, take meat out of the freezer and reheat (without looking like cooking).
- Cholent with hard bones is fully cooked, unless you eat the bones.

## 4. Yad soledet bo

- Doubt: **71°C** (can touch only seconds) or **45°C** (uncomfortable after minutes)?
- Practice: don't heat anything into the **45–71°C** range. Below 45 → not cooked; already 71+ → cooked, may heat further.
- Another view: if most people couldn't drink it all at once, it's yad soledet bo (in need).
- No leaving water where it could reach yad soledet bo, even if you're watching (SA 318:14).

## 5. Liquids: Sephardic custom

- Liquid = spreads out evenly in a bowl.
- Rashi, Rosh: for liquids, **yesh bishul aḥar bishul** (hot vs. cold soup is a real difference). **SA 318:4** rules this way → Sephardim.
- So: no heating soup from the fridge; soup taken off the plata that cooled below yad soledet bo can't go back. Still hot → may go back (per §19) and even to the center.
- Pot with sauce → take out the solid pieces and heat them alone; a little moisture is batel.
- Congealed gravy that will melt: counts as solid; Ashkenazim le-khatchila avoid unless secondary to the food.

## 6. Liquids: Yemenite and Ashkenazi customs

| Community | Cooled cooked soup |
|---|---|
| Yemenites (Rambam) | ✓ may reheat |
| Ashkenazim (Rema) | Still warm → ✓; fully cold → ✗ (rabbinic) |
| Sephardim (SA) | Below yad soledet bo → ✗ (Torah) |

- A guest may eat a Yemenite host's reheated soup, but may not **ask** him to heat it for him.

## 7. Kli rishon, sheni, shlishi

- **Kli rishon**: heated on the fire; walls stay hot → cooks.
- **Kli sheni**: poured into → cools fast → doesn't cook, except **kalei ha-bishul** (even irui from a kli sheni is forbidden on those).
- We don't know which foods are kalei ha-bishul (except water, oil), and some forbid putting raw food in a kli sheni → practice: **don't put uncooked food into a kli sheni**; irui from a kli sheni ok unless known kal ha-bishul.
- Instant soup: water from a kli sheni onto the mix ✓. Instant mashed potatoes ✗ (Lash).
- **Kli shlishi**: doesn't cook, all agree.

## 8. Tea on Shabbat

- Tea bag → only in a **kli shlishi** (urn → cup → second cup).
- Ḥazon Ish / AHS: not even in a kli shlishi. Most: kli shlishi never cooks; color is released even at 40°C.
- Tea **essence** made before Shabbat → may pour into a kli sheni. Extra careful (coloring): essence in a kli shlishi, water onto it.
- Essence ran out → pour from a kli sheni onto the leaves (already cooked).

## 9. Hot water into a wet cup

- Some (Igrot Moshe, SSK): dry the cup first; the drops will cook.
- Most (Tzitz Eliezer, Shevet HaLevi, Yabi'a Omer): no need; not the normal way, doubtful, unintended. **Halakha follows this.**

## 10. Davar gush

- Hot solid food (meat, kugel, potatoes) in a kli sheni/shlishi: majority say vessel rules apply; MA/MB: a hot solid can still cook.
- Practice: be strict, but doubt → lenient (if you can touch it, fine).
- On a hot piece: cooked cold gravy ✓, **salt** ✓, pickles/raw vegetables ✓ (unintended). **Raw spices** (paprika, pepper) ✗ until you can touch it.

## 11. Cooking after baking

- Roasted → cooked (roast meat into cholent)? Most Rishonim, some Sephardim: ok.
- Yere'im, **Rema** (Ashkenazim), some Sephardim: no; bread in soup counts as cooking (maybe even in a kli sheni). Be-di'eved the food is fine.
- Cookie into tea → **kli shlishi**. Bread into soup ladled into a bowl → ok (ladle = kli sheni, bowl = kli shlishi).

## 12. Megis (stirring)

- Stirring food not fully cooked → Torah, even off the fire while yad soledet bo. So no ladling from such a pot until it cools.
- Fully cooked → may take out (SA 318:18; many Sephardim), but don't stir on the fire.
- Ashkenazim and some Sephardim: take the pot **off** first, then serve (and follow ḥazara rules to return it).
- Need (pot on an open fire you couldn't return it to) → may ladle out fully cooked food on the fire. Water from an urn → always ok.

## 13. Adding water to the cholent

- No **cold** water. Hot water from the **urn** (yad soledet bo) → ok, even via a cup (Ashkenazim, Yemenites, North Africans).
- Meat pot / pareve urn: lift the lid ~10 seconds first.
- Yeḥaveh Da'at: forbidden (the water gets "recooked" as a kli rishon); Sephardim who are lenient have support.

## 14. Hashhaya: introduction

- Torah: may leave food on the fire before Shabbat. Sages: not if he might **turn up the flame** (Mav'ir + Bishul); such food may not even be eaten.
- Even on max heat it's forbidden (no distinctions; might lower then raise; stirring, covering).
- Allowed when: (1) the food is **ready**; or (2) the fire is **covered** (garuf/katum → plata, blekh).

## 15. When the food is cooked

- Rif, Rambam: only if more cooking would **hurt** it. Geonim, Tosafot: once it's **ben Derusa'i** (edible in a pinch).
- Practice: ben Derusa'i is enough; le-khatchila avoid an open flame if more cooking improves it → cover the flame.

## 16. Covering the flame

- A covered fire reminds you not to adjust it → even uncooked food ok on a **plata** or **blekh**; ideally cover the knobs too.
- Still better that everything be **fully cooked** before Shabbat (otherwise lids and moving are Torah issues).

## 17. Electric ovens and timers

- Not-cooked food in an oven → a metal sheet / thick foil between food and element, or cover the knobs.
- Thermostat: opening the door may turn on the element → use **Shabbat mode**.
- Urn: boiled before Shabbat, ideally no thermostat; no auto-refill with cold water.
- Raw food + timer to turn on the oven (or a bread machine): some permit with covered knobs; others forbid entirely.

## 18. Hanaḥa (placing on Shabbat)

- Reheating cooked food is allowed, but not in a way that **looks like cooking** → not on an open flame.
- OK: on top of another pot or the urn.
- **Plata/blekh**: many → only on an **upside-down pot/plate** or a lid that lifts it a bit (not foil). Lenient → directly. Some: plata yes, blekh no.
- Practice: better strict; lenient may rely; follow family custom.
- **Radiator** → ok. Space heater not used for food → with a separation; used for food → like a gas flame.

## 19. Ḥazara (returning)

- Lenient on hanaḥa → may return fully cooked food to a covered fire.
- Strict on hanaḥa → returning is ok if clearly a return. Three conditions: (1) not put on the **floor**; (2) **intended** to return; (3) kept **holding** it.
- Great need (only way to have hot food) → may return even without them.

## 20. Plata cases

- Fully cooked food on the edge → may move to the center.
- Two platas → may move between them; blekh fire went out → move to another plata (still hot).
- Two layers of pots → bottom ones out for the meal, top ones down onto the plata (still yad soledet bo, intended).
- Open fire → blekh + plate on top, then food.

## 21. Returning food to the oven

- No returning to a hot oven (might raise heat; looks like cooking).
- Solution: a 4-sided **metal box** inside, or an overturned pan/plate, + covered knobs, + the ḥazara conditions. No cold food into a hot oven.
- Oven **off** on a timer → may put cooked food in (covered knobs / overturned pan); some forbid; lenient may rely.
- Plata with a **compartment** on top → may return food; not hatmana (not touching all sides).

## 22. Hatmana

- Two rabbinic bans: (1) never insulate with something that **adds heat** (hay, olive dregs), even before Shabbat; (2) **on Shabbat** no insulating at all (might heat it first).
- Before Shabbat: towels, blankets → ok.
- **Thermos** → ok. Bag of rice inside the cholent → ok (food in food isn't hatmana).

## 23. Plata and Crock-Pot

- Wrapping a pot **on the plata** with a towel = insulating with heat → forbidden even before Shabbat.
- Only full wrapping is hatmana → covering **most** of the pot ok, even on Shabbat (Rema); towel over a pan on top not touching the sides ok (SA 257:8). Not a **wet** towel.
- **Slow cooker**: some forbid (hatmana); halakha: top is visible → ok; food not cooked → cover the knobs.

## 24. Electric boiler

- Opening hot water lets **cold water in** to be cooked → boiler on → ✗.
- Boiler off: scalding → no; can barely touch → ok; unsure → ok (if it turns out very hot, add cold water).
- Timer for ~15 min every few hours → lukewarm, below yad soledet bo → ok.

## 25. Solar boiler

- Cooking in the **sun** → permitted; with something heated by the sun (**toldot ha-ḥama**) → rabbinic.
- Strict (Minḥat Yitzḥak, R. S.Z. Auerbach): collectors are toldot ha-ḥama; electric backup switch.
- Lenient (Tzitz Eliezer, Yabi'a Omer, R. Qafiḥ): it's the sun itself. Practice: **may be lenient**; strict is praised; never be strict when bathing babies.
- Closed-loop systems (tall buildings) → forbidden on Shabbat (Friday night pre-heated water ok).
